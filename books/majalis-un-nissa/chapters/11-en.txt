# Glossary

*Amma-jan, Abba-jan*: 'dear mother', 'dear father'; the usual polite forms of address to parents.

*Ashraf*: the well-born gentry; *sharif* families, whose manners and speech the book describes.

*Ashrafi*: a gold coin, worth about sixteen rupees.

*Atu-ji*: a respectful name for a woman teacher of girls; here, the elderly governess who tells the story.

*Begum*: a title for a lady of good family.

*Bismillah*: the ceremony of a child's first lesson, beginning with the words 'In the name of God'.

*Chhatti*: the rite held on the sixth day after a birth.

*Deorhi*: the covered entrance hall of a house, where men from outside could wait without entering the women's quarters.

*Diwan-khana*: the men's reception room of a house.

*Doli*: a covered litter carried by bearers, used by women of good family.

*Domni*: a professional woman singer who performed at women's gatherings and weddings.

*Faqir*: a wandering mendicant who has given up home and property.

*Fiqh*: Islamic law, as a subject of study.

*Ghari*: a measure of time of about twenty-four minutes.

*Hadith*: the recorded sayings and actions of the Prophet.

*Hajj*: the pilgrimage to Mecca, made in a fixed season of the year.

*Hakim*: a physician trained in the Greek (Unani) system of medicine.

*Jagir*: a grant of the land revenue of certain villages, given for service.

*Khas*: the fragrant root of vetiver grass, woven into screens that are kept wet to cool the air.

*Kos*: a measure of distance, about two miles.

*Mahr*: the dower promised by the husband to the wife in the marriage contract.

*Majlis*: a gathering or sitting; each chapter of the book is one evening's talk.

*Maktab*: the neighbourhood elementary school, often held in a mosque or a teacher's house.

*Maulvi*: a scholar of religion and of Arabic and Persian, often a teacher.

*Namaz*: the ritual prayer, said five times a day.

*Nikah*: the Muslim marriage contract.

*Paan*: betel leaf folded with lime, areca nut and spices, offered to guests; kept in a *paandaan* (betel box).

*Purdah*: the seclusion of women from men outside the family.

*Qaida-e-Baghdadi*: the first primer of the Arabic alphabet, from which children learned to read the Qur'an.

*Rum*: the Ottoman lands, with their capital at Istanbul.

*Sadqa*: alms given to ward off harm; also 'all owing to', as in 'it is all owed to Amma-jan'.

*Sarai*: an inn for travellers.

*Sharif*: well-born, respectable; the class whose ideals of conduct the book sets out.

*Sipara*: one of the thirty parts into which the Qur'an is divided for reading.

*Tafsir*: commentary on the Qur'an.

*Thag*: a member of the gangs who befriended, strangled and robbed travellers.

*Ustani-ji*: a woman teacher.

*Vilayat*: abroad, and especially England.

*Ziyarat*: a visit to a holy place; here, the visit to the Prophet's tomb at Medina after the hajj.
