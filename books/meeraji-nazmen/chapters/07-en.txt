# 31. Passing On

We just looked, and then forgot.
When beauty came before the eyes,
a storm rose in the ocean of the mind;
seeing the storm so playful, the Ganges of the sky, the milk-filled Milky Way, took fright,
and the moon hid, the stars slept, the storm faded, everything was gone;
the heart forgot its first worship, the idol in the temple of the mind was broken;
the day brought unknown things, and then the day too was new, and the night new,
the beloved too was new, the lover too new, the bed of joy new, everything new.
For a moment there came before the eyes, glimmering, the first
beauty, and then we forgot.
Do not think us faithless;
faithless? Why, how, how so?
Will the praise that lasts one moment not be called praise?
A thing of the heart and of the eyes:
why do you call it lust?
However much splendour there is, wherever, let it warm the heart;
as long as there is the earth,
as long as there is time,
this beauty and display goes on;
let us look at this one glimpse with a skimming eye and take our fill.

We are travellers of this world,
and the caravan moves on at every moment;
every settlement, every forest, desert, and the lovely form of the mountain
will charm the mind for one moment, come into view for one moment;

every scene, every man's kindness, and the sweet magic of woman
is in our power for a moment; the moment passed, all will be wiped out;
let us look at this one glimpse with a skimming eye and take our fill.
Why do you call it lust?
Will the praise that lasts one moment not be called praise?

The moon is in the sky one moment,
and one moment these stars;
and think: the span of life too is one moment.

# 32. The Washerman's Ghat

The man in whose clothes' fate is written
the heat of the rays:
I envy
him.

Why is solitude only an untouched,
unknown, strange
dream?
Why does fancy alone
amuse me?

Why does the morning, becoming
a gust of the night of pleasure,
soothe for me the nameless torment
of the cheek?
Why is the cloak of the enchanting dream not torn?
Why are the twisted, dancing tresses
not wet
with tears of the bleeding heart?
Why, from the madness of longing for touch,
do I not find
release without bonds?

The heat of the rays on the clothes
is a snare for the eye;
and for the morning after the night of pleasure, the fragrant gust of the tresses
is never
beholden to the dawn.

Why should the stains of the soiled garment not be washed?
Drunk with joy,
the heat of the rays,
why should it not become the colour of the night of pleasure, an unbroken image?

You, compelled by torment,
admit that the sight of this image
gives you the pleasure of a cup already tasted;
why are you thinking
that this cup has been drunk from by another?
Have you seen anywhere in the world today
a virgin joy?

The heat of the rays on the spread-out clothes
is the warmth of the tresses of life;
this man earns his daily bread in the world
only by washing soiled garments;
look at him.

# 33. On the Bank of a Stream

She sat for a single moment, and then rose up;
the eye saw only that there was a seated idol;
the sight had not the strength to see
how the sword struck, how the breast of the earth
for one moment became like a spring.

Twisting, this wave rose in my heart:
if only these bushes would become a range of mountains,
and I could go and stand at the foot of the mountain!
If such an impossible thing came to pass, why should this not come to pass too:
the bed of dry leaves that is spread on the ground
should become an instrument too; it is an instrument, it is an instrument;
the melody that had just woken, why could your ears
not hear it, why were they helpless to hear?
The curtain of the eye fixed only a seated idol
in the special circle of the mind.

I remember my ears had woken
when a sound of writhing came from the dry leaves,
and every wave of the skirt had flashed;
perhaps the shadow of that same sword was falling,
which in a moment had come out of the secret chamber,
as lightning flashes, unrestrained.

But every wave of that soiled skirt was wiped out;
before my eyes the water nymph vanished;
I just stood there; I did not see (alas!)
how the sword struck, how the breast of the earth
for one moment became like a spring.

I am not standing at the foot of the mountain at this moment;
the bushes are not a range of mountains, they are a curtain,
beyond which I see glimmering
an unknown scene, like an untouched bride.

Yes, now, making my fancy the bridegroom,
I will take it into the secret chamber of this curtain:
how the sword struck, how the breast of the earth
writhed like a restless heart;
in an unrestrained way, like lightning,
the water nymph had come out of her corner of seclusion!
Life was warm; in every drop, watery feet
had slipped over the dry leaves and arrived!
I too was there, a nameless, traceless worm;
I saw the cloud split, a stream poured out;
with lightning speed the bow let fly an arrow,
and it, curving, swaying, quivering, fell
like a stone falling from a mountain peak;
there was no barrier for it, for it;
on the ground itself the bed of dry leaves was spread;
on this very bed that unknown fairy lay down!

And I, the nameless worm, like the cloud,
went on gazing in this very hope, went on gazing:
that now, at this very moment, some water nymph might come,
and I, flute in hand, might become the cowherd;
where would the water nymph come from? On this very bed,
I saw, she had just lain down, content;
but alas, I am still standing alone;
my hand is soiled, it is wet, my sight is dim;
it was not tears that my hand had wiped from my eyes!

# 34. Evolution

At every step biers have been set down; lift them and go;
why do you look at me? It is not my work; this work is yours, of today and tomorrow;
lost in today, perhaps you think
that neither the past yesterday nor the coming tomorrow is yours;
but if you sink in thought like this, nothing will come of it;
biers have been set down; lift them and go;
come, lift the biers now;
how long will these flowing tears flow? Rise, and wipe them away now.

When is this a road? It is a grave;
think this too: inside the grave only a bier will gain admittance;
then will you go on crawling, keeping away from the decree of fate?
Biers have been set down; lift them and go;
the grave is open;
the grave is like the open mouth of a hungry, greedy man;
but if nothing fresh, and fresh again, is to be had, let not even a stale morsel go
inside it;
let the open mouth stay open as if it were a void;
lift, quickly lift; before your eyes some biers have been
set down; lift them and go.

Come back having sunk them in the grave, in a deep sleep of eternity;
if these dead go inside the grave, then perhaps
your dead life too may wake today.

# 35. The Story of a Tangle

One is single, the second double, the third is triple;
over the single one, moment by moment, is the bloody watch of thought;
in the road of the second, the double, the third is a piece in the game;
the third, the triple, has the brightest face of all;
as if single were the watch, double the piece, triple the face [TN: the poem plays on the rhyming words ikahra (single), dohra (double), tehra (triple), pahra (watch), mohra (piece in a game) and chehra (face)].
A single paper, folded double and triple, became a boat;
with the boat the children were amused for a moment; in play it became a wound;
when it became a wound, this thought came into the heart: let us say, come, it is done.

It is not about the games that go wrong as they are made;
this is no bier, and no wedding procession;
this is such a day as has no night before or after it.

In every fold of the triple there is indeed a new face,
but every face is a piece in this unplayed game,
whose colour is single.

How shall we take the matter further? If the matter comes off, then it goes further;
till now the colour was single; if anyone went forward, let him reach out a hand;
this is the way of fate: the night of a short day grows longer;
now whoever wants to go forward, let him go forward along with himself;
running ahead and behind, one is ahead, one behind;
how can the one behind go forward when the one ahead runs too?
Both blows are equal; who will say this to the two of them?
Defeat and victory lie in this: now who will stop, and who go forward?
