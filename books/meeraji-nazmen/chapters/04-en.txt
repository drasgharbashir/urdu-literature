# 16. The Pilgrim

One came and went, another will come; I have watched a long while; so his night will pass.
Why am I standing here, what business have I? I do not remember; memory too has become a flickering
lamp, whose every halting, hesitant ray is a soundless burst of laughter;
but how did my ears hear it? A storm blew, and having blown, faded
away; to this day it is there in my ears, a roaring, restless,
boiling, spreading and spreading. A long while I have stood here; one came and went,
another will come; his night will pass. There is an uproar, look wherever
you will: many people are coming, walking and strolling and stopping, then
moving on again and hurrying; they come and go, from here to there and from there to
here. Just as in my heart there is a storm from the wave of thought, so my eyes
keep on looking: how the ray of a flickering lamp keeps showing life, slipping
and falling in its fashion. The thought comes
to me: now darkness has become a light; but from that light keep seeping
those drops of nectar that I have held in my palm; but the palm
had become a flickering lamp; with a leap it was light, the flame fell, then a kind of darkness
began to spread, sinking and sinking, and having sunk, rising in a single moment, as when, under the storm's
sharp buffets, the leaves of a door keep opening and shutting,
fluttering like a wounded bird. I went on watching: one came
and went, another will come. The thought came to me; my feet kept refusing
to move; I just stood there. In my heart a drop said this: the night will pass just so.
Taking a drop of the heart into my eye, I went on watching: fluttering like a
wounded bird, when once the leaves of the door met, slowly, slowly
it began to dawn on me: now this wounded bird will not flutter, but it will make my heart
flutter at every moment. I will keep holding in my palm those drops of nectar which
were to seep from my eye; but my life has become a flickering lamp, whose every halting
and hesitant ray is a soundless burst of laughter, that in this darkness there is nothing
that I had seen before in the dark. This journey of light and dark has gone
on, and will go on; this is the custom of the road: one came and went, another
will come; so the night will pass. The flickering stars used to tell of the road:
the river is flowing; flow on, get out of this tangle so. Someone went straight to the goal,
but many caravans forgot the dim signs of the stars' unique round; yet they
too, walking and moving on, saw before evening that the shut door of their aim
was beginning to open. But I stand here; what business have I? My door
does not open. Every grain of the sleeping sand of the spreading desert is telling me
that in such a ruin there is a dry palm, a sole of such a kind that even when a thorn's point pricks it
it cannot say, give me a drop of your blood to drink. But why do I stand here?
There is no business; then I too, joining these coming and going, one, two, three,
millions of whirlwinds, would just so, walking on, sink somewhere, as here
in the flowing waves a boat holds every billow in the spread lotus of its palm.
The thought does not come to me that on this road the goal is within the power of
everyone who goes. Shall I set off? Let me go. "Come, come, why are you standing in this place
so silent and alone? If you say so, from an untouched little branch we will
give you two flowers." "Enough, enough, I have no need of it; I am
waiting for a friend." But he has gone too; still
no comfort comes to me, for I have begun to seem a dweller of the desert in my own
eyes; that some shut door should now be seen opening for me is not possible.
I long for another storm that will hide me altogether in its veil.
Now I have begun to feel that all the lovely scene that was in my power
has become a passing gust, which my hands cannot hold back,
for in my palm no drops of nectar remain; there is only a spreading,
dry, leafless, colourless desert, in which it is not possible for me to say,
one came and went, another will come; my night will pass.

# 17. Oneness

There is no evil in the world;
only a swing of continuity is moving.
This is what I say:
I am no evil, I am not the world, I am not the swing of continuity.
What do I know of what is in evil, what is in the world? And then I will say this too,
that for the thing that stays alone, the goal is extinction, only extinction.
Evil, good, the world, continuity: these things have come from the house of permanence.
I have no connection with any house at all;
I am one, and I am alone, I am a stranger.
This settlement, this forest, these flowing roads and rivers,
these mountains, some tall building suddenly coming into view,
these ruined tombs, and the keepers like the image of unending death,
these laughing little children, this blind traveller dying, struck by a cart,
the winds, the plants, and a few clouds coming and going this way and that in the sky:
what are these?
This is the world itself; it is a swing of continuity moving.
This is what I say:
this settlement, this forest, these roads, these rivers, these mountains, building, keeper, traveller,
the winds, the plants, and a few clouds coming and going this way and that in the sky,
all this, everything, has come from my own house;
I am the world; by my breath alone the swing of unending continuity moves.
But there is no evil in me;
how shall I say this,
that in me extinction and permanence both have come and met?

# 18. The Self's Rebellion

Life is dear, and yet why does my heart
ask night and day in prayer for death?
Why can a word of joy never
come to the lips of a grieving fate?
Why are veils of tears spread over my eyes?
Why is no answer found to this lasting question?
Are the tangles of selfhood, breaking my resolves,
making me a failure at life in this world?
Why does that night not come
whose brighter dawn,
I long for it, should now speak with me?
All comforts have vanished from my imagination;
I cannot see the road;
why can I not see the road to joy?
Come, my heart, today come out of this narrow solitude;
yes, steady yourself, do not fall into the pit of silence; yes, steady yourself now;
make self-forgetting your creed, forget all yesterday and tomorrow;
give up the longing for a centre; be restless, and surge;
boil with heat like the breast of a volcano.
Come, my heart, see the road of joys,
and see the flame of moments of pleasure;
dry the tears of a heavy heart;
see the road, dry the tears;
give up the longing for a centre; be restless, and surge;
come, my heart, today come out of this narrow solitude.

# 19. The Far Shore

On the breast of the spreading earth there are forests too, waving,
and rivers too, going far away,
and mountains too, absorbed in their silence,
and oceans too, seething;
over them spreads the blue sky;
in the blue sky there is the sun too by day, bringing light;
when evening goes there is the meeting with the moon;
when night comes there are little stars too, twinkling;
and nothing else.
You have not come till now, nor will you come hereafter: that is all,
and nothing else.
One you, one I: we are far, far apart;
till today everything has gone on far, far apart;
far, far apart life will pass.
Tell me, how should wave strike wave,
and tell me, how should it touch the shore?
Keeping wave apart from wave, in between are hundreds of other waves too,
and nothing else.
The intoxication of forgetting that spread over my heart
says one thing only, again and again;
in the pain of one thought alone the heart found delight.
When did the bud of longing open?
I just drifted on a single wave.
You have not come till now, nor will you come hereafter.
Even if there are no forests on the breast of the earth,
even if there are no mountains, no rivers, no oceans,
no moon and stars in the blue sky, no sun,
no night and day, no evening and dawn in the world,
I do not care;
there is one thought only:
far, far apart life will pass, and nothing else.

# 20. The Rival

Today it had to be you who came face to face with me,
and in a guise one could never have imagined:
a fierce look, an angry heart, harsh words.

As in a garden a gardener's eyes
might have watched, beside the path, a tiny plant
blossom, grow lovely and bright,
so is my story and yours.

On the path, head held high, far from every thought,
I was lost in my own mood; every fresh step
kept bringing near to me every ray
of the star shining on my horizon.
I had no fear of a thorn or of stumbling;
but this was my mistake, that forgetting of myself.

It has come before me itself, in another form:
a fierce look, an angry heart, harsh words.
But there is no need of it now; I think
today it should not have been you who came face to face with me;
there were others in the world, far greater than you or I,
who were strangers, who were to stay strangers.

"Someone has told me that this friend of yours
always comes home late at night,
whistling with his lips, humming."
One person's sigh is wiped out by another's kindness.

"I tell you, sister, what times these are!
No regard for her own name, no care for the honour of the house;
since last month, every night, in secret,
our lady meets some wretched man."

I do not worry; servants have this habit,
they make a crow of a feather, a mountain of a mustard seed.
Only one thought, straight as an arrow,
becoming this reflection, comes and settles in my heart:
this is the one whose name once was Lajwanti (the bashful one).

# 21. There Was a Woman

I wish you were a tiny little girl and I could take you up and sit you in my lap,
and you would just shout and cry out and laugh, just raise your hands, wave them in the air and let them drop,
sometimes as if you were about to say something,
sometimes as if to say, "I won't speak to you,"
sometimes smiling, making a noise, then clinging to my neck and saying such things
that I would remember the rustling wind
that strikes the branches of thick trees and sets the heart a strange riddle, a riddle it cannot understand.

That I would remember some cold spring, bubbling and restless,
that to look at is a few dripping drops,
but, growing beyond its bounds, becomes a stream, a river, an ocean.
I wish that on the waves of such an ocean, with such a wind, I could float that boat which does not float,
yet carries the traveller on and on, and does not come back, and goes on joining him to a deep peace.
I wish that I too could just shout and cry out and laugh, just raise my hands,
wave them in the air and let them drop,
sometimes as if I were about to say something;
but you would take me up and sit me in your lap,
and steady me when I began to fret,
sometimes smiling, making a noise, then clinging to your neck I would say such things
that you would remember the rustling wind,
that same rustling wind by whose sweet spell the scarf slips,
that same rustling wind which, giving every unknowing woman's scattered tresses
the new guise of a dense black cloud over some sleeping forest,
wakes them.

That you would remember the rustling wind,
that I would remember the rustling wind:
this is my wish.
But, growing beyond its bounds, everything becomes a stream, a river, an ocean,
that ocean which does not carry the drifting traveller on, but only keeps rocking him,
keeps rocking him;
and then the traveller says within himself, my story is not new, after all;
what pleasure is there in an old story?
Who asked me today to tell an old story?
