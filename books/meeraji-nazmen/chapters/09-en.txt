# 40. Temptation

The fragrance of luscious crimes
is coming into my mind;
the fragrance of luscious crimes
is carrying me far beyond the bounds of perception.

It is the blood of youth;
it is the season of spring on the earth!
Today what I like is madness;
in my eyes is the tangle of intoxication,
for the net of temptation is spread over every beauty;
the fragrance of luscious crimes is tempting me today!
All the bonds of the laws of morality look broken;
lovely and forbidden clusters are making my heart slip;
these silken dresses and their trembling,
this rouge..... this assembly,
every charm of feminine enchantment is conspiring today,
is leading my heart astray!
It is coming into my mind,
the fragrance of luscious crimes!

# 41. Support

There is no saltiness in the drops of dew;
if the flower wishes to know all at once,
in a single moment, the end of its night,
it must say to the cold gust of wind:
go and kiss his tears;
having kissed the tears, it began to feel
that one tear, one drop,
in a moment becomes a blue ocean
and spreads over the lonely boat.
What if there is no saltiness in the drops of dew?
If there were saltiness in the drops of dew, what then?
The flower, floating and floating on it,
could not reach its goal, could not become a boat.
What is the flower, you or I?
I am the flower, not you;
you are a lonely boat on the blue ocean,
drifting on, never stopping for a moment;
when do you know that there is no saltiness in the drops of dew?
On the strength of words alone, in the luscious shade of your nights,
you think that every moment, suddenly spreading,
with the beating of the heart, began to spread over the six directions,
as a stillness in the dark night of separation
might keep stirring up pleasure, too, shoulder to shoulder with pain.
Here, take the cup, hold it;
there is still the poison of grief left in it,
drinking which I too went on running away from my life.
There is no use at all in talk, but for me
every hint is a net of words,
in which birds, fluttering, screaming,
screaming and screaming and falling silent,
come to know that now that very night will become the cure for the heap of pain
in whose scattered, hundred-times-torn skirt
the drops of dew on the wet petal of the flower
bring with them the light saltiness of the tender soul, tasting which
the tongue says: why say now
that there is no saltiness in the drops of dew?
Look, far off:
a lonely boat on the blue ocean, little by little moving on, is coming near; look:
who knew that distant things too
would come near like this in the course of talk?
Just look:
the bond of the pledge of imagination was closed;
it began to open;
little by little a new form came into view,
a new form, but some features are familiar,
and from every moment, from every tear, a blue palace stands erect;
far off in its ceiling are two chandeliers.

# 42. A Contradiction

After the golden torment has passed from the mountain,
after the evening's red song has died in a moment,
yes, after the cry, and the trembling of the world's heart,
after the day's dusky, yellow and tulip-red labour,
how shall I wash the stains of darkness from my heart?
Waking, waking, I will weep again till morning.

Yes, the same I whose eye by day was also the sun,
yes, the same I who saw the world brimming with life;
my human mind, writhing, says:
see, the beauty of scenes has no permanence;
my heart is used to the clamour of the tavern;
I have no connection at all with the calm of stone;
the night-dark air of peace does not please me;
the soul will find comfort in a ceaseless war.

# 43. The End of the War

The daughter-in-law says: this old woman will be the death of me;
the mother-in-law says: she has a tongue a yard long, she'll say whatever comes to her mouth;
the daughter-in-law says: whenever you look, she drags things out for no reason;
the mother-in-law cries: O my God, I repent; save me now, You alone;
the daughter-in-law says: what sort of home of my own is this? Here even my own are strangers;
the mother-in-law says, burning with spite: even a royal palace wouldn't suit her;
the daughter-in-law says: whoever holds the ladle, everyone is hers;
the mother-in-law cries: go on, go on; the shoe of the foot has climbed onto the head;
the daughter-in-law says: to whose lot have they tied me, ill-starred from my birth?
the mother-in-law says: who can tell now whose the newcomer is, or what is whose?
the daughter-in-law says: this one who aches for her son, if she could she'd poison me;
the mother-in-law says: that's how I am, I hear abuse and still give blessings;
the daughter-in-law says: now it has fallen on my head; somehow it will pass;
the mother-in-law says: whenever you look at her, she'll be eating milk and sweet crumbs.
The daughter-in-law was saying whatever came into her head before her mother-in-law,
but the mother-in-law too, tit for tat, was letting out the daughter-in-law's secrets.
The little one spied his chance; at once he reached the kitchen;
the cream that had risen on the milk, he quietly got there to eat it;
having eaten, as he came back, on the way he found a black cat passing;
seeing it, out of fear he let out a loud scream;
with one scream of his, in a moment, the quarrel of mother-in-law and daughter-in-law was settled:
the daughter-in-law ran, "My darling, what happened?" The mother-in-law cried, "Oh God!"

# 44. Ease of the Body

In the bathroom, she says, I like only china tiles;
on china tiles, she says, if there's a splash, then in a moment
each drop slips off very quickly.
Someone might ask, what use is there
in the drops slipping off so quickly?
When there was need, when one felt like it, one went quietly, bathed, and came back;
washed and fresh, one came out as on the water of some lake a waterfowl
suddenly dives, and diving, bobs up at once,
and then goes on swimming, never stopping a moment.

So she says; but if there is a floor of china tiles, and walls,
the heart says that the clean, bright colour of everything
looks so pleasing to the eyes,
as in the rains, when the clouds stop after raining, every flower garden
looks
as if it had to go, had to go somewhere to meet some lover, but
were still thinking something over.
Someone might ask, what has a china tile to do with any thought?
China tiles are lifeless; in the flower garden every flower, bud and leaf
waves with the light of life;
the flower withers, the bud opens,
and every leaf sings the praises of a new flower;
china tiles cannot sing any song;
china tiles stay silent;
tired of such silence, the one bathing
takes up a melody in such a manner that one is reminded of Luqman.
When I say this, she asks:
someone might ask, what has a melody to do with Luqman?
And I say, leave Luqman, Luqman, or the melody; come,
let us talk of something else;
and so one goes on lying in someone's heart;
the thought never comes:
let me set foot in the bathroom, bathe and sleep;
lying there just so, sleep comes, and one sleeps.

# 45. Rustling

Here, let me lay my hand on these folds;
these are waves, they flow on and carry me along;
these are a wave of wine; the sleeping air of the cup in the heart
suddenly wakes.
If someone from the world of reality should enter this world,
his lips would be smiling; perhaps laughter would rise
and grip my heart with its hands;
but I think that these waves are still unacquainted with the scene of the shore; they are just making an excuse; what is called an excuse?

It is excuses, only excuses;
I reached out and laid my hand on the waves; my hand, like a boat, having brought before me the spectacle of the onset of a fierce
wave, is lost;
all this was the flow of the wave of imagination;
but I think, why did I not say at the very first the thing that was to be said? A useless spending
of time;
every hidden scene
that moment, when it comes, will spit out,
when, on hearing this thing, the listeners will think:
what was the excuse, what was the fold, what was the wave of wine?
But lost behind the curtain of the night's dark, nameless seclusion, they will know it in a moment,
and lost in the brief rapture of a pleasure, they will say this, unrestrained: may
I?
Here, let me lay my hand on these folds; why this hesitation?
These are waves; they are related to the sorrowful river of black night,
which goes on flowing and does not stop for a moment,
which does not care at all whether I lay my hand, or hesitation presses this hand of mine
to my heart, and I sleep in the bed of these waves;
presses it to my heart, and I sleep in the bed of these waves.
