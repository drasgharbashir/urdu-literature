# 36. On the Road in the Evening

He whose aim is to meet the night's image of fancy
comes sometimes through the door, sometimes through the window,
and every time he enters in a new disguise;
it is not right at all to think of him as one person;
in my fancy he is the image of every person, of every human being;
sometimes he takes on the guise of an artless, innocent beloved, sometimes,
becoming a clever, worldly-wise and shameless tyrant,
he comes to deceive, he leads astray,
and when the time is past, he hides.

But over my eyes, becoming a cloud, hangs
the window of a wall; coming out of this very window, the rays
cling to my eyes; longings rise restless
from the contented secret chamber of the grief-worn heart,
and I think: in this curtain of light
who is the shameless one, and who the artless beloved?
Thought is blocked by the wall; how should it move,
how should it reach the drunken idol-house of some shy seclusion,
that idol-house where two idols sit silent
and go on saying everything with their eyes?
Their dusk has made of the mind such a camera
as captures only the scenes it pleases.
I stand and watch, and think: when the two of them
leave the idol-house of the heart and go home,
they will find bitter reality standing in the courtyard;
one will think: my pocket, this world, this society;
one will see that there quite other preparations are afoot.
It troubles me: why am I not there
in the drunken idol-house of the night's shy seclusion?
My eyes see the smoke of the window,
and the heart says: this is the smoke of a burnt heart;
a dense calm, a harsh loneliness
is my hoard.
I do not care if this world is wiped out today;
I do not care if today this useless society,
stifled by its own restrictions, becomes a tale.
In my eyes the scene of the window is fixed;
to save my own being from ruin
I will squeeze into this colourless window;
but let me not become in this way that same idol
which goes on saying everything with its eyes,
which, leaving the shy air of the idol-house,
in the shameless, grievous black chamber of the house
must watch the cruelty done to longings, and waste away.
I will not go into the window, let the world be wiped out
and, stifled, become a tale,
the stony-hearted, useless society that dries the blood;
I, taking a turn of thought,
will in a moment put on the guise of love's wandering bird,
and go away into that forest
into which you went, leaving a dejected heart alone;
what if I see the road or not?
The towers of countless trees
I will go on touching,
and then this search will not end;
the quest cannot be beholden to a window in a wall;
I am free; I have no care;
a dense calm, a harsh loneliness
is my hoard.

# 37. Many Stars Are Shining

Trembling, trembling, they glow;
but when the leopard of day comes,
and these stars' time is over,
then the sharp fireflies of the sky
will in a moment become rolling tears
and go and hide behind the curtain of dawn.
You and I are together today;
your youth is telling
me the story of my love;
but there is a kind of fear in the air,
a kind of trembling in this wind;
the stars are flickering now,
the signs of autumn are spreading;
I feel
that now the stars' time is over;
now the leopard of day will come;
now the sharp fireflies of the sky,
these dear gazelles of the plain of stars,
will go and hide behind the curtain of dawn.

# 38. The Tall Building

Fixing countless eyes in its face, a strange design of building stands erect;
O herald of civilisation!
Your face is fearsome!
It is as if the storm of the human mind stood there;
cast into waves, many songs come to my ears, but
in them is a passion of oppression, a long image of complaint,
and in the words are tales of sleeplessness.
Is some grieving soul
restless in your breast too, O shining gem of civilisation?

As I heard the waves of your songs die away, I began to see
the bitter dregs of some ugly-coloured wine in a broken cup;
the sight grows dim with the drunkenness of wine;
why does the dark air of night frighten me?
In the dark air of night the gleam of your eyes cannot frighten me; why, I
used to live in a darkness greater even than this,
and in that darkness of the soul the stars of sorrow shone,
and sometimes by mistake the flames of joy leapt from every shining star,
as a melody, leaping from your window, spreads its arms,
and then the skirt of the void absorbs it;
I began to remember my own tears flowing in loneliness,
those same tears, those same flames of joy;
but it was a dream, and like a dream was the leap of the flames;
the wings of my imagination, like the wings of a wounded bird,
fluttering, tremble in vain;
the tension of my limbs would not let me live; writhing, once
I used to search for release;
but alas, when pain began to become the cure, that restraint was lifted from me.

To soothe my nerves,
forgetting the darkness of the soul, I arrived;
I took the steps of that height
on which you stand, blinking your hundreds of eyes;
people had told me about you
some strange tales:
I used to hear that in your heavy body a bed is spread,
and a delicate woman lies there; loneliness,
becoming an insipid weariness, keeps creeping
into her mind; but she is restless,
waiting for the curtain to tremble,
for the dress to become a slipping cloud,
and for some unseen, strange form to enter;
she has no concern whether
the manner of the one who comes
pleases her heart or does not please it;
she has one aim only: that he should raise up
a strange design of building of the sea of nerves,
whose face should be loathsome,
and that it should in a moment become your rival;
that the storm of the human mind should rise up,
and that delicate woman, unrestrained, without fixed intent,
should begin to seem a falling wall
to the soulless spectator of the night;
that, forgetting the song of her weariness,
with a brief trembling of the eye of the door,
she should make light, like a palace of sand,
the strange design of building of the sea of nerves,
should bend like a falling wall.

These tales, becoming a strolling fragrance in my mind,
used to dance whenever they wished;
and now I see that among your hundreds of eyes
only one shining eye comes into view;
is it in this shining eye that the flame of joy is?
Now I want to close this eye with my hand.

# 39. The Part and the Whole

Understand that the thing that appears and says, where am I?
is nowhere;
understand that the thing that is seen and is not seen
is here;
it is here, but where is it now?
But where is it now?
What is this, as if just now it was here,
but where is it now?
Is it some memory, some thought, some dream?
It is neither that memory, nor that thought, nor that dream,
but even so it is something,
but even so it is something;
it is a wave, yes, only a wave;
it is a wave such that in any wave there is nothing at all;
it is this very thing I am weeping over;
it is this very thing the world is weeping over;
if the world is weeping, let it weep,
but since the beginning of time I have been nourished on smiles, laughter, peals of laughter;
since the beginning of time my work has been to laugh and make others laugh;
then, when the world laughed, was it I who made it laugh?
This is what you say, you who have gone on weeping;
if you say this, I accept it;
but when the world's lot is to weep and make others weep, the world will go on weeping;
that I alone should laugh
is not possible;
if the world weeps, I too will weep;
if the world laughs, I too will laugh;
but this laughing of the world, this weeping, is that thing which appears and
says, where am I? I am nowhere;
the laughing of the world, the weeping of the world, is that thing
which is seen and is not seen, and is here;
I will go on laughing and go on weeping, and even so
the world will say: you have gone on weeping, you have gone on laughing;
but I tell you this, that I am that very thing
which even now appears and says, where am I? and yet is not
seen, and says, I am nowhere;
I had gone on weeping, I have gone on laughing;
but you had gone on laughing;
so now it is you alone who will weep, and only
I am the one who will go on laughing even now.
