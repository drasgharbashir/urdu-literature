# Glossary

*Azad nazm*: free verse: a poem with lines of irregular length, not bound to one metre or rhyme.

*Diwan*: a poet's collected ghazals, usually arranged by the last letter of the rhyme.

*Geet*: a song in Hindi-flavoured Urdu, often on love and longing.

*Ghat*: a landing place with steps on a river bank, where people bathe and washermen wash clothes.

*Ghazal*: a lyric poem in couplets with one rhyme and refrain, usually on love; the poet names himself in the last couplet.

*Halqa-e-Arbab-e-Zauq*: "Circle of the Men of Taste", a group of writers founded in Lahore in 1939 that met weekly to read and criticise new work; Meeraji was one of its leaders.

*Houri*: a maiden of Paradise.

*Indra*: the king of the gods in Hindu myth; his court (Indar Sabha) is full of dancing fairies.

*Lajwanti*: "the bashful one"; also the name of the touch-me-not plant, whose leaves close when touched.

*Luqman*: a sage of Arabian and Qur'anic tradition, famous for his wisdom.

*Maqta*: the last couplet of a ghazal, in which the poet names himself.

*Mir*: Mir Taqi Mir (died 1810), the great classical poet of the Urdu ghazal.

*Nazm*: a poem developing a single theme under a title, as opposed to the ghazal.

*Peon*: an office messenger or orderly.

*Plato*: the Greek philosopher; in Urdu "Plato's uncle" is a mocking name for a know-all.

*Saqi*: the cup-bearer who serves wine, a stock figure of the ghazal.

*Shahnai*: a reed pipe played at weddings.

*Sher*: a couplet of Urdu or Persian verse.

*Sindur*: the red powder a Hindu bride puts in the parting of her hair, a sign of marriage.

*Takhallus*: a poet's pen name, used in the last couplet of a ghazal.

*Timur*: Timur (Tamerlane, died 1405), the Central Asian conqueror.

*Tonga*: a light two-wheeled horse carriage.
