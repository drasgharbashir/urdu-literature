# 26. Deprivation

I tell you, if in the evening anyone has never, even by chance, seen some dim star,
there is no wonder in that, nor will there be.
Since the beginning of time the evening's seemingly harmless, playful serpent has kept to this way:
rising and swaying and writhing, she keeps saying, come, look at me; I have
set up a colourful gathering for you;
it is a strange hall with curtains hanging on every side; whoever is there,
no one can see him;
the folds of its curtains go on swaying as if the spreading surface of the river had
risen like the dusk and hidden the air from sight;
just look, the chandeliers hang from the ceiling; with each half-lit ray they hint
a song of something secret, in which are the tremblings of the canopied bed's embrace;
from behind the pillars, slowly, slowly, halting and hesitating, a thief of a shadow keeps
saying, he has come, he has come;
in a moment, suddenly, just like that, this hall will begin to glitter all through;
how neatly everything has been arranged!
I say, do agree, come, the gathering is set;
if you come, let the *shahnai* (wedding pipe) ring out, let the comings and goings in the hall raise
a bustle; but in the tremblings of the bed's embrace, if you so much as
feel it, the fault is mine.
Since the beginning of time the restless wave has kept to this way; it will not even know that a tender branch,
striking against a reckless gust, had heaved sighs.
But I tell you this: if anyone has never, even by chance, seen some dim star in the evening [TN: the text reads shad ("happy"), evidently a slip for sham ("evening")],
there is no wonder in that.
Since the beginning of time the evening's playful serpent has kept to this way;
she stings, and stinging, keeps saying,
go; if you keep hesitating,
every moment will pass in the same way, and you will just go on watching, alone, alone;
to your right and left, before you, you will see nothing; only the cold walls will go on laughing;
but even their laughter, slowly, slowly, like times gone by, will begin to seem a far-off thing;
the eye, sunk in the dusk, will see a star appearing far off through the window,
but there will be no swinging chandelier on the ceiling;
like broken, fallen pillars, your shadow will go on writhing on the grieving floor, the shadow which
had this longing; shall I say what the longing was?
Enough now; hide your sorrowful words in your own rising and changing colour.
Now I admit that in your flowing you have seen, far off through the window, dim
stars too, by the hundred thousand;
now I admit that in my eye a tear keeps glistening and does not fall;
now I admit that to my right and left, before me, I see nothing; only
the cold walls go on laughing;
now I admit that I have seen this hall in my dreams till today, but
nothing there is arranged so neatly
as you have told me, that your colourful gathering is set;
I will not even dream now of the tremblings of the bed's embrace; with my own
ears how shall I hear that ringing of the *shahnai*, the red song of the
*sindur* (the red powder of the bride's parting), hearing which the comings and goings in the hall
raise a bustle for a moment?
I hear only the cold walls laughing on and on.

# 27. Strange Waves of Sweetness

I want the eyes of the world to go on looking at me, to look as
someone looks at the soft branch of a tree,
looks at the soft, swaying branch,
but the load of leaves, like a dress taken off, with the truth lies on the floor as a crumpled
heap;
I want the gusts of wind to go on clinging to me,
restless, teasing, laughing, saying something, under the load of modesty
halting and halting, steadying themselves, in the colourful whispers of sweetness;
I want sometimes walking, sometimes running, to go on and on,
as the wind, touching the waves of the river, flows on rustling, and does not
stop;
if some bird somewhere sings a song in a lovely voice,
let the warm waves of the sound come and strike my body and turn back, not allowed to stay;
sometimes warm rays, sometimes soft gusts,
sometimes sweet, sweet, enchanting talk,
sometimes this, sometimes that, colour newer than new rising,
and as soon as it rises, dissolving in the spreading air;
let nothing be allowed to stop within the circle of my joy.

The circle of joy keeps shrinking;
the open field of wheat lies spread out;
far away the canopy of the sky, making a strange curtained bed, leads astray with luscious hints;
the sound of water in its slaps, melting into the songs of the birds, slipping, is now passing out of sight;
I am sitting;
the scarf has slipped from my head;
it does not occur to me that someone will see my tresses;
the circle of joy keeps shrinking;
enough; now let no other new thing come into the circle of my joy.

# 28. Unattainable

The night is dark, the forest lonely, there is no one with me;
the gusts of wind shake the trees, the leaves tremble and shiver;
the arrow of fear has pierced my heart, my hand is on my breast;
again and again I think, how will the night ever end?

It is the season of rains, and youth is a storm of waves;
my beloved is innocent, my heart knows nothing of the customs;
there is no one to suggest how the means can be found;
Lord, show me the way, give me knowledge.

The oars are broken, the boat is old, the ferryman far away;
the river's waves are enemies, and the beloved on the other shore;
listen, hear how a poor lover calls in sorrow:
how shall I go, how shall I reach, how shall I show my love?

How shall I wipe from my heart the sickness of the fire of separation?
How shall I solve the riddle of love, how bring about our union?
Let not the moments of talk pass; far away is his country;
far away is the beloved's country, and I am in disguise.

# 29. The Student

You know, when Timur's armies
used to advance on their enemy,
the women used to stay behind,
and the band of men who were learned and scholarly
used to walk behind everyone.
Why do those who lead everyone a step forward on the road of life
always walk behind everyone?
There is one basic lack in learning; otherwise
learning would have progressed through everything in every age.
Today Iqbal says that it is woman's flame from which Greece's
learning of Plato will live till the Day of Judgment [TN: an allusion to Iqbal's line that the spark of Plato broke from woman's flame];
today in school, in college, the first place
is reserved for women;
today English is read, geography, history, every learning here
the teacher teaches as if we
did not even know that the band of men who were learned and scholarly
used to advance behind everyone,
and the women stayed a little ahead of them.
Women stay ahead even today;
women say even today: our tresses,
whether scattered or held bound in a knot,
are bound, for the failed longings of those who look,
to one hand only,
that one hand which, holding a sword at its side,
always walks ahead of all.
It cares nothing for learning (and what does it care for woman either?);
it has no knowledge of how Plato in a moment
becomes a spark and goes out.

You are before me, but your shining face
is seen only by that ignorant man
who says that when Timur's armies
used to advance on their enemy,
the women used to stay behind,
and those who were learned and scholarly used to think:
whose is the defeat, whose the victory? Leave it;
what little things we too have got entangled in!
As I walk along, the thought has come to me quickly:
if this knot of yours opens and scatters, what will happen then?
My history, or your history,
will spread and cover today (and tomorrow too),
will tell the thinker in a moment
that women, even if they are behind, still stay ahead,
and Plato's uncle [TN: i.e. one who thinks himself wiser than Plato], sword in hand, advances;
look, that knot too has begun to say something to Plato himself;
and whom will he meet on the road? Timur;
and Timur will say to him, why have you come here?
Go, get behind me, so that always, at every moment, behind you
learning may go on advancing, crawling and crawling,
as behind everything everything
goes on advancing, crawling and crawling,
and every Plato who shines as a spark is wiped out.

# 30. The Chain of Day and Night

God has lit a bonfire;
he can see nothing;
on every side of him is void upon void;
in his shrinking heart he thinks:
strange, the light of eternity has been wiped out.

Far off, man has stopped short;
he sees a flame,
but on every side of him too is a void;
so his imagination has deceived him.

Eternity past has become eternity to come in a moment;
non-being is vexed at this thought;
a breath or two has become an excuse;
the mirror of reality is broken;
then let someone say, what is this, what is that?
It is void upon void, void upon void.
