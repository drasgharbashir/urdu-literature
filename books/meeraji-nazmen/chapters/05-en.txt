# 22. I think the play of sex is only an ease of the body

I think the play of sex is only an ease of the body;
there is another way in the world to meet the Beloved.

The great ocean of imagination, the lovely gusts of fancy
bring in the rain longings for worship;
but the longing of the heart's desire is not fulfilled.

Some woman's dress, the scents of some seclusion,
the sweet, sweet whisper of some meaningless word:
these very things hang always over my sorrowful thoughts.

The way of worship is movements, thirsting and vague;
sometimes the soul of the idol, waking from the senseless dream of death,
a hundred thousand fairies of some court of Indra might come and amuse,

dance enticing dances and sing luscious melodies too;
but this dead heart is used only to sorrowful thoughts;
the cloud does not come, it cannot bring the rain of joys;
my grieving soul is ruled by its own feeling.

There is another way in the world to meet the Beloved;
why do I think the play of sex an ease of the body?

Sometimes I reflect on the short life of man;
sometimes I wander, as if lost, in the lakes of passing longings.

# 23. Beyond the Body

I sit in a dark room,
so that some stray ray might come and see;
but it has always been the custom of the dark room that any ray should come and see:
now why should that be?
If any ray could see it, then from that moment
the dark room would not stay dark;
breaking, it would burst forth flowing as a boundless flood of darkness,
and from that moment why should any store of that light be able to hold it back?
From a distance of a thousand years I say this;
from a distance of thousands of years; but who knows if anyone is hearing it?

In the darkness of black hair your forehead is shining;
in your eyes a ray, dancing and dancing, is telling me
that in my lips is that nectar
which is seeping from a distance of thousands of years.
But all these years are not years of light, nor are they years of darkness;
these years are the tangled folds of distance.
Why is the dark room dark?
The eye is lost in the darkness of your hair;
if this tied knot opened and scattered, the ray too would grow lovely and bright.
Your dress shows itself with stripes upon a whiteness;
when the ray came into the dark room, darkness would become stripes,
and by that ray, for a moment, darkness would become light and cry out
that some stray darkness too should come here sometime;
the distance of thousands of years has become darkness.

The flowers of song smiled on your lips as you threw off your clothes
as if a singer had taken up a melody;
your every melody has become a black stream of darkness;
now why should any ray break out of it?

The black room echoes with your melodies,
has echoed for thousands of years;
the black room is shining with the darkness of your hair;
the black room is saying with every untouched turn of the dress:
come here; here it is not possible for anyone to see you;
if a ray came here, it would at once go and hide in the dark room,
and over it the stream of the dress would flow as if
the dark room had never been a dark room;
it was a ray.

# 24. The Void of Non-Being

If gusts of wind come this way, tell them:
here there is no such thing as they might take with them;
here there is no such thing as anyone might see and think,
I wish I had this too.

Here there is no wayfarer, nor any goal;
here there is no dark, no light, there is nothing;
the fiery feet of passing moments move here one after another,
showing everything, burning everything, wiping it out,
showing everything that nothing is gained even from being.

If gusts of wind come this way, tell them:
all these temples, these cities and villages
are the traces of the tale of life;
but I went and looked at every door, trampled every wall, taking every window for the heart,
and learned this secret:
the fiery feet of passing moments move everywhere one after another,
here wiping out, there making new marks to wipe out;
who knows if past life will meet the life to come?
If gusts of wind come this way, tell them:
everywhere the net of distances is spread;
in every place time stands like a demon,
as if it were the boundless image of the universe,
sometimes becoming an illusion of thought, sometimes, by mistake, becoming the sense of beauty,
it shows itself to the feeble eye of the prey;
from everything my sign of non-being is plain;
non-being too is my beggar, it moves along on my strength alone.

If gusts of wind come this way, tell them:
the scorching light of the tale of life too has been wiped out;
but, wiped out, it has not become any darkness,
for in this place there is no dark, no light; here there is nothing.

# 25. The Night's Story in the Guise of Day

In the spreading darkness of night there is no shadow;
the weak, twinkling stars keep saying:
when the moon comes, the shadows will come too;
in the spreading darkness of night there is no shadow.

Night is a tale of centuries, of many centuries,
or perhaps of some former birth;
the darkness of night spread; there was no shadow;
the spreading darkness of night was in need,
a beggar of that first ray which comes trembling and wakes
the sleeping shadows, rouses them into waking;
the swaying, dancing signs of life come into view;
but before life it was something else;
in the spreading darkness of night there was no shadow;
when the moon came, the shadows came.

Her scattered tresses were shadows,
the sweet hesitation of modesty too a shadow,
and there were other shadows too, light and deep;
the thick lashes of black eyes
sat holding the shadows in their embrace;
and in these shadows it used to be felt
that the heart's grief, the heart's ache, the heart's longing, everything
is a shadow, a trembling shadow;
and on seeing me, her thick silence
seemed only a shadow,
such a shadow as stays silent;
and on seeing her I too became just a shadow;
a shadow stays silent;
and even when there was a restless trembling, it was silence alone
that used to hold my reins on the road.

and even when there was a restless trembling, it was silence alone
that used to hold my reins on the road.
The road running straight was a shadow too;
all the people coming and going on it
were dim shadows, but shadows;
I too, going and coming, was a shadow;
I too was a shadow; whose shadow?
Clinging to whose feet did I go silently on?
Let me say it: this sorrowful thought comes into the heart:
a shadow stays silent.

It is the shadows of night that stay silent;
the shadows of day speak
of the story of past pleasure;
and my rapture too is now only a shadow of the day,
one edge of which the burning sunbeam
is bent on scorching and wiping out with its fierceness.
If only a cloud would come, a cloud spread and become
the decline of the rising sun!

The rising sun tells this:
the lengthening shadows betray someone;
flowing on, they keep saying:
those awake and asleep in the night have risen;
hanging, hanging in the sun,
when some hanging garment
trembles with a gust,
the trembling shadow too, in harmony, keeps telling
everyone the story of past pleasure.

Past pleasure too is a shadow for me,
of a cloud coming beneath some sun,
coming and going like a rustling gust;
it did not stop even for a moment to talk;
it was a shadow of the day; it did not know a single thing of the night.
Night is a tale of centuries, of many centuries;
and now it is day; I see shadows,
I see speaking shadows;
they are well known, yet they explain new meanings, and go on explaining,
then come back again,
as I used to go, and having gone, come back
on that same road which was a shadow.

The road today too is a shadow, but a new shadow;
on the road a house,
that too a shadow, thick and desolate with sadness;
every figure coming along the road
is a shadow, a witch;
no reflection of a houri (maiden of Paradise) is seen in it;
on seeing it I tremble;
blood rushes to my eyes,
a kind of mist spreads before me,
the heart goes on pounding,
and I see
shadows meeting and melting into one another become something like ghosts;
neighing, they laugh and cry out:
"Is this still the thought in your heart?
Is this still the thought in your heart,
that a shadow stays silent?
See, we speak, we are all speaking shadows;
where will you escape from us?"
And I tremble,
and they speak:
"He trembles, he shivers, this failed coward;
no one talks at all;
perhaps even now he thinks in his trembling heart
that a shadow stays silent."
