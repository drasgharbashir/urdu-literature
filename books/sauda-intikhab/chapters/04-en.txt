# 7. However many blows your cruelty dealt my life

However many blows your cruelty dealt my life,
no complaint ever came to my tongue.

Fear the heat of the sighs of the upright, proud one:
an arrow does not turn back once it reaches its mark.

In the season of old age the games of youth are useless:
when did a palm that blossoms in autumn bear fruit?

My heart, full of blood like the bud in a painting,
never opened its lips upon its hidden secret.

The stars' eyes never saw from clouds such a black day
as came upon my eyes that drip pure blood.

Seeing you at night, O moon, with another,
my heart took to taunting the rose for its linen. [TN: linen was believed to tear in moonlight; the rose's torn petals are its linen.]

Having become a master in the school of poetry, Sauda
has prevailed over the world's knowers of the rules of verse.

# 8. We have no concern with unbelief, nor business with Islam

We have no concern with unbelief, nor business with Islam;
our aim is the Saqi, and our business is with the cup.

What has my lamenting heart to do with anyone's rest?
Let anyone be restless: it minds its own business.

In this garden let no one wipe a tear from any eye;
dew, we too have this business from evening till dawn.

Why should the viper not strut proudly everywhere?
It never had to deal with your black locks.

If I find him alone somewhere, then
I would have my heart's will of that flower-limbed, playful one.

I am the captive of one who, once the prey is caught,
has no concern with the captive, nor business with the net.

This tongue is now free of saying Ram or Rahim:
he who has found the sign has no business with the name.

The sun's eye has dealings with your window by day;
by night the dew's eye has business with the edge of your roof.

When I saw the beginning of your affair, Sauda,
alas for the day when you will have to deal with its end.

# 9. The morning breeze is in your lane, and the east wind too

The morning breeze is in your lane, and the east wind too;
look, is anything left of our dust?

Your pride and my humility: how long, tyrant?
Everything has some limit in the end.

The moth burns for the candle, and I for you;
is there anywhere in the world love, anywhere faithfulness?

Though you are drunk in your own thoughts among singers,
have you ever heard the groaning of hearts?

Alas, in this age, apart from the tongue of complaint,
is anyone a friend to anyone?

Is so much cruelty right to captives, fowler?
In garden after garden, is there any song of the nightingale now?

Step carefully on the desert's thorns, Majnun:
Sauda too is barefoot in these parts.

# 10. May the counsellor never have leisure from sewing up collars

May the counsellor never have leisure from sewing up collars:
if the heart is torn from the beloved, no one can darn it. [TN: the lover tears his collar in frenzy, and the counsellor is forever sewing it up.]

That I should give this heart and take both worlds: may it never be!
It would be a bargain only if you were not in it. [TN: sauda, "bargain", is also the poet's name.]

If you were not there between, in the mirror of being within non-being,
we would have no face anywhere. [TN: obscure; translated literally.]

The quarrel of beauty and love would be settled in a moment
if only you were not present in the Qazi's court.

The knot of the drop has come undone; otherwise, O breeze,
the frenzy in the garden bird's brain would not be the rose's scent. [TN: obscure; translated literally.]

What has passed has passed for the people of the earth, O sky;
from now on let no one be so fair of face.

One should take the heart from you and give it to the lightning's flame,
but there is this fear: that it too may have the same nature.

The garden bird could not search out the seed of the rose;
we of raw nature cannot make the search for you.

Sauda, change the rhyme and then say this ghazal;
impudent one, do not set yourself face to face with Dard. [TN: Khwaja Mir Dard, Sauda's contemporary; dard also means "pain".]

# 11. Drunk at dawn and repentant at evening am I

Drunk at dawn and repentant at evening am I;
I am caught every day by the Qazi's summonses.

Call me slave, servant, or call me menial:
whatever you call me, I belong to the rose-coloured Saqi.

My heart's devotion is to the service of love;
I believe neither in unbelief nor in Islam.

Not for one day did I earn it lawfully and eat it,
I who am a servant in the tavern for two cups.

I have no care for the world, nor do I seek the faith;
in this imagined existence, what use am I?

I am of one colour; double-dealing does not please me;
in speech and verse I reject the double meaning (iham).

I want no one's prayer on my behalf;
I seek abuse from the beloved's lips.

If all are God's slaves, be sure that I, here,
am a slave of the idols, bought without gold or price.

The flask of wine is my old man's spectacles, Sauda;
I am now a gazer upon the days of grey hair.

# 12. There is no power to describe His radiance

There is no power to describe His radiance,
even if one became all tongue, like a candle. [TN: the candle's flame is likened to a tongue.]

Lift the veil of separate being (ta'ayyun) from the door of the heart:
the talisman of the world opens in a moment.

Come and look a while at love's idol-house, Shaikh:
the colour of the idols glimmers there like the candle of the Haram.

There is a wondrous sight in this garden of existence, but
when the rose's eye opened, it was the season of autumn.

One would take you and show you the market of Egypt,
but no one there desires such costly goods.

From existence to non-being the road is a few breaths long;
where else is there such a journey as passing from the world?

Sauda, if you ever hear with the ear of resolve,
this is the theme of the cry of the heart's caravan bell.
