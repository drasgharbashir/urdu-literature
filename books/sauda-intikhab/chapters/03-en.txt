# 1. See the whirling of my tear-filled eyes like this

See the whirling of my tear-filled eyes like this:
like the whirling of an eddy in a river.

I die for you, friend, I weep so much
that there is a flood in my eye, and the whirling of an eddy.

Those eyes turn away in a single moment, just as
the cup of pure wine goes round in a gathering.

Since there is such a hangover in the eyes at this hour, Saqi,
every friend in the round is asking you for wine.

Though you became dust, you still wandered, turned to a whirlwind:
the restless lover's wandering did not end.

Without the goods of reason and patience, what rest can this heart have?
For a pauper, the shifting of his goods comes hard.

Why should the heart not wander, Sauda, through the beloved's locks and face?
It delights in the wheeling of a moonlit night.

# 2. O Lord, send from somewhere a brisk market

O Lord, send from somewhere a brisk market;
I am selling my heart: send some buyer.

In my stock, my dear, this heart is all there is;
if you will not take it, what can I do, friend? Send it back.

The monsoon claims to rival my eyes:
then let it send some such cloud that rains pearls.

In the marriage-contract of beauty, lovers give the bride of their life;
if you will not come yourself, then send your sword.

You took this heart from Sauda, and it was his comforter;
in its place, please, send some other comforter.

# 3. O God, in what land do those faces dwell

O God, in what land do those faces dwell
for the sight of which my eyes now yearn?

Why did you come out of non-being, and what did you do in the world before you left?
Without you, death and life laugh at each other.

How should our heart not be pierced through like a glass lattice?
That playful one's glances sink into stone.

The rainy season left long ago, but
these clouds of eyelashes are raining still.

See how they snatch the lover's heart in a moment:
what forwardness beauties show to lovers!

Since they are wild things and might break loose,
my lashes bind my eyes tight with cords. [TN: the red veins in the eye are called "cords" (dore).]

Though we have already given both worlds as their price,
that friend's glances still come cheap.

He said to me, "Now give up the daughter of the vine:
in old age, madman, what are these revels?"

When I, Sauda, met him, this is what I told him:
"This year there is a Saqi, and there is the worship of wine."

# 4. Set free from your prison, the heart wept much

Set free from your prison, the heart wept much;
remembering the pleasure of captivity, it wept much.

The portrait of me without you that Mani painted:
Bihzad understood its manner and wept much.

Your laments, nightingale, did not wet the rose's eye;
hearing my cry, the fowler wept much.

Go and see in the garden: streams are flowing;
ashamed before your stature, the box tree wept much.

That the mirror is sunk in water has this cause:
before your hard heart, steel wept much. [TN: mirrors were of polished steel, and their sheen is called "water".]

Such thirst rises from my martyr's tomb
that the executioner who passed that way wept much.

I asked Sauda, "Shall I too give my heart to someone?"
Telling his own story, he wept much.

# 5. Treatment is needed for the one sick with love of you

Treatment is needed for the one sick with love of you,
but for a cure, the turning of fate is needed.

For the crazed heart, entangling locks are needed:
in treating a madman, a chain is needed. [TN: sauda'i, "crazed", plays on the poet's name; sauda means melancholy madness.]

I made many laments before that idol,
but to soften stone, potency is needed.

Become dust on love's road, so that you have worth:
to turn copper into gold, the elixir is needed.

One sign of your eyebrow is enough for us;
is a sword needed to kill lovers?

At least ask after the breaking and ruin of my heart:
for any house to last, building is needed.

For what sin do you punish us, friend?
For punishment, surely, a fault is needed.

Sauda, I am in this garden like a closed bud, heavy at heart:
in a house of mourning, a sad face is needed.

# 6. Without you my time passes very badly

Without you my time passes very badly;
somehow the days get by, but the night goes badly.

Every deed has its due reward of one kind;
the requital for love's deeds is beyond all kinds.

Nightingale, build your nest in this garden with some thought:
the fowler lies in wait for you, and badly.

When I asked the messenger for my friend's reply,
he said, "Hush, the matter has gone badly."

The rival will not let you meet us for a moment;
the wretch goes on following you, and badly.

If any ant is still in it, let it stay there, Shaikh-ji:
your beard has now fallen badly into the comb's hands. [TN: obscure; mor is taken as mur, "ant", and the jest is at the Shaikh's long, unkempt beard.]

Sauda, do not meet him: have some pity now on your life;
that young one's way of meeting goes badly.
