# Glossary

*Ali*: Ali ibn Abi Talib, cousin and son-in-law of the Prophet, the fourth caliph and first Imam of the Shia.

*Bihzad and Mani*: Bihzad, the great Persian miniature painter of the late fifteenth century; Mani, the third-century founder of Manichaeism, remembered in Persian tradition as a matchless painter.

*Farhad and Parvez*: rivals for Shirin: Farhad, who cut through a mountain for her love, and Khusrau Parvez, the Persian king she married.

*Ghazal*: a lyric poem in couplets with one rhyme and often a refrain, usually on love.

*Haram*: the sacred precinct of the Ka'ba at Mecca.

*Huma*: a fabulous bird whose shadow falling on a man was said to make him king.

*Iham*: double meaning: a figure of speech in which a word carries two senses, a near and a far one.

*Joseph*: the Prophet Joseph of Canaan, the type of perfect beauty, sold in the market of Egypt; the "moon of Canaan".

*Khizr*: the immortal guide of travellers, who drank the water of life.

*Kulliyat*: the collected works of a poet.

*Majnun*: "the madman", the legendary lover of Laila, who wandered the desert for her love.

*Matla*: the opening couplet of a poem, in which both lines rhyme; a long qasida may have a second opening.

*Muhtasib*: the officer who enforced public morals and punished drinking.

*Na't*: a poem in praise of the Prophet Muhammad.

*Qasida*: a long ode in one rhyme, usually in praise of God, the Prophet or a patron, often opening with a prelude.

*Qazi*: a judge of Islamic law.

*Ram and Rahim*: names of God for Hindus (Ram) and Muslims (Rahim, "the Merciful").

*Rubai*: a quatrain in a fixed metre, rhyming in the first, second and fourth lines.

*Saqi*: the cup-bearer who serves wine, a stock figure of the ghazal.

*Shahr Ashob*: "the city's ruin": a poem lamenting or satirising the decline of a city and its people.

*Shaikh*: a religious elder; in the ghazal, the pious hypocrite who reproaches the lover and the drinker.

*Sher*: a couplet of verse, the unit of the ghazal and the qasida.

*Solomon*: the Prophet-king Solomon, who ruled men, jinn and beasts; the ant spoke before him.

*Takhallus*: a poet's pen name, used in the last couplet of a ghazal.

*Ta'ayyun*: in Sufi thought, the particular, separate being that veils the one Reality.

*Zamin*: "ground": the metre, rhyme and refrain of a poem, which other poets may take up.

*Zunnar*: the sacred thread worn by Brahmins; in Persian poetry a sign of unbelief.
