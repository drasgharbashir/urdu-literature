# 19. When did one of the broken-hearted come to plead his case?

When did one of the broken-hearted come to plead his case?
China with a hairline crack in it gives no ring.

When at night I brought a prayer from my breast up to my lips,
Acceptance said, "What has come into your head?"

The heart for which I had been offered both worlds as its price:
such was my luck, I went and threw it away for a single glance.

The world's generosity was ready to give both worlds,
but here no word of asking came to the tongue.

Do not be proud of it if you have learned
the skill and art of drawing a pearl out of stone.

In the eyes of those who understand, he is the man of skill
who has drawn malice out of someone's heart.

Yesterday, in the ruined temple, on a drunkard's tongue
came this verse, how apt to that place:

"Seeing your deeds, wine sweats with shame;
O muhtasib, have you felt any shame at all?" [TN: arq means both "sweat" and "distilled spirit".]

Here weakness of heart forbids even a moment's meeting;
he grew bored and left, and then I came to myself.

The gardener sowed the palm of our life in the garden
out of desire, but did not tend it.

If it is the elixir, what is it worth, Sauda, that handful of dust
which has grieved someone's heart?

# 20. Do not tell him what passed over me: what happened, happened

Do not tell him what passed over me: what happened, happened;
whatever befell the sufferers of love, what happened, happened.

Lest some tyrant seize you by the collar,
wash my blood from your skirt: what happened, happened.

Friends, it has reached the very mouth of the heart's wound:
someone bring a jug, someone a salve; what happened, happened.

Hearing my story, that pitiless one says,
"What talk is this? Let it go; what happened, happened."

For God's sake come, forgive my sin;
it will not happen again, O fierce one; what happened, happened.

What state is this you bring upon my heart, O eyes?
Do not flow so, bursting into tears; what happened, happened.

No harm came to the sword, nor to the hands;
whatever happened, executioner, happened on my own head.

I gave him heart and faith; now there is this life, Sauda;
let us see what comes next: what will be, will be; what happened, happened.

# 21. Every rose that is here goes with that rose-cheeked one

Every rose that is here goes with that rose-cheeked one;
what a rose he is, with whom this rose garden goes!

You are drunk, the night is dark, and strangers are with you;
say whatever comes to your heart: this sinner is with you.

Be silent, nightingale of the garden; what argument have I with you?
My talk is with the captive bird.

The message of that glance in which there is a scent of love:
who knows whose last sight goes with it?

This knot never came undone: that a champion like my heart
is bound to a single strand of your hair.

You treat the sickness of my heart,
but friends, if this is the heart, its ailment goes with it.

How could Sauda lay hands on that wealth of beauty?
When he steps out of the house with it, the whole market goes with him.

# 22. When the beloved lifted the hair of his locks and tied it up

When the beloved lifted the hair of his locks and tied it up,
then I tied a hundred thousand fancies in my heart.

We were charmed in two days; alas for those
whose hearts have been bound here for months and years.

How should this heart not be caught in the thread of his glance,
whose eyes have bound a hundred thousand wild gazelles?

Whatever his colour, it is in my eye,
whether he wears a yellow robe or binds on a red turban.

Only before you does my lament go astray;
otherwise I have hit targets as fine as a hair.

I long for a kiss, but how can I say so to one
whose temper binds every word of asking on his lips?

Whom will you kill? For whom have you girded your waist?
Why do you go about, darling, with sword and shield bound on?

When I recited a few verses before him, he said,
"You have bound these themes to suit your own case."

Sauda, if he bound your heart in his locks, it serves you right:
why did you bind his down and mole into your verses? [TN: bandhna, "to bind", also means "to compose" a theme in verse.]

# 23. Hindus worship idols, Muslims worship God

Hindus worship idols, Muslims worship God;
I would worship anyone who worships friendship.

In this age the eye of courtesy has gone blind;
the modest eye is lost from the world.

Since they saw the colour of henna on your feet,
the fire-worshippers have left fire and turned to worshipping henna.

If you wish the friend's reflection to shine in you,
keep the mirror of your heart polished clean.

I am so glad of my wandering that after death
every grain of my dust will be a worshipper of the wind.

So that you may worship the dust of extinction,
do not, like Khizr, drink the water of life and worship it. [TN: the source reads mast, "drunk", evidently a slip for mat, "do not".]

Should one hurt a man like Sauda?
O self-worshipper, shame! You are a worshipper of whim. [TN: hava-parast, "worshipper of whim", also means "worshipper of the wind".]

# 24. For your existence, sir, the rose's hint is enough

For your existence, sir, the rose's hint is enough;
for my lament, the nightingale's hint is enough.

I will not seek a fatwa from the Qazi, friend:
for breaking my repentance, the wine's hint is enough.

Come, sit in my eyes; today the hour is auspicious:
these eyes are a pair of scales, as the hint of your mole tells. [TN: til means both "mole" and "sesame seed", weighed in scales.]

The cause of the heart's composure for a whole world
is, playful one, the hint of your scattered curls.

It is not the weight of piety that has bent your stature, Shaikh;
the hint of that bridge is well known among the people. [TN: obscure; perhaps the narrow bridge (Sirat) over hell.]

If your locks did not come to my mind in the garden,
the hyacinth's hint would not set my heart writhing.

Lovers of your eye have no need of wine;
the gurgle of the flask, with its hint, makes Sauda senseless.
