# 13. Perhaps he came to the garden to see the rose

Perhaps he came to the garden to see the rose,
for I found some other scent in the rose's brain.

Even an enemy becomes a cause of life if God wills:
the morning breeze is oil in the rose's lamp.

The gardens are in bloom: come with wine, Saqi;
do not leave me sad at heart in the rose's leisure.

This garden is no place for song, for there is
no wine in the rose's cup but heart's blood.

Sauda seeks the footprint of Ali just as
the morning wind goes about on the trail of the rose.

# 14. Wherever we went with our wet eyes

Wherever we went with our wet eyes,
the dry ponds were filled.

In love of you we saw no happy day;
suffering and suffering, we died.

Your cruelty is yours to answer for;
what was ours to do, we did well.

He was reciting this verse with a burning heart
when we went to Sauda's house last night:

"Like a candle, life came up to our lips;
we melted the body away.

So much forwardness, moth!
If not at evening, we went at dawn.

No one will even know
where we went from that gathering." [TN: the last three couplets are the verse Sauda was reciting.]

# 15. Whose wet eyes are under the earth still

Whose wet eyes are under the earth still,
that springs of water run everywhere below the dust?

The red flower that rises from the earth like a flame:
what burnt soul still burns beneath the dust?

One day I saw the sweep of your skirt;
the rents in my collar still go round and round it.

Searching, they brought together a calamity like you,
yet these heavens still do not cease their turning.

Since you went into the garden heavy with wine,
the roses are still yawning, and the vines still stretching.

The wound on my heart is from madness's sword, counsellor,
and you, fool, are still sewing up the rent in my collar.

How shall I, Sauda, praise the curve of his ear?
This tongue has not yet been washed clean with the lustre of pearls.

# 16. If one did not hold one's breath in captivity

If one did not hold one's breath in captivity,
the flame of one's voice would set the cage on fire at once.

The caravan leader's heart would turn to blood and flow
if my lament gave lessons to the caravan bell.

The moisture of my heart's scar reaches the tips of my lashes;
I always keep the straw fresh in the fire.

There the comb moves through the locks, and here the heart:
this thief never minded the night watch.

O love, neither Farhad escaped you nor Parvez;
you made the base and the noble level with the dust.

The captives of your lane cannot draw breath
until they plunge their breath into heart's blood.

Do not tempt us, Sauda, to stroll in the garden;
though the air there is fine, it is fine only for desire.

# 17. Every time, I cry out at your cruelty: help!

Every time, I cry out at your cruelty: help!
Not once does it work upon your heart: help!

Seeing your glance, the sky in its turning
goes about playing its juggler's tricks: help!

Proud of your beauty, how would you know
who it is behind the wall? Help!

Sauda, I tell you: keep away from love;
why are you disgraced in lane and market? Help! [TN: the refrain is the Arabic cry al-ghiyas, "help!"]

# 18. People say the beloved's eyebrow twitched

People say the beloved's eyebrow twitched:
something like a sabre flashed before my eyes.

How can I describe that wrathful air?
Like lightning it came before me and cracked.

At my lament the rose tore its robe;
nightingale, did your heart not crack at hearing it?

No one went before the killer, for fear;
only I went before him without a qualm.

It will be hard then to put out the world
if love's flame flares up a little more.

Sauda, I had just stolen the rose in the garden;
what can I say of my luck? A leaf rustled.
