# Glossary

*Achhutis*: "the Untouched Ones": women of the household of Nasir-ud-Din Haidar, king of Lucknow, vowed never to marry.

*Amin*: "So be it", said after the opening chapter of the Qur'an in prayer; whether to say it aloud divided the Hanafis and the Ahl-i Hadith.

*Aqiqa*: the sacrifice and feast held for a newborn child, at which the child is named and its head shaved.

*Azadi*: "freedom"; the heroine's name, on which the book often plays.

*Baqarkhani*: a rich, flaky sweet bread of Delhi.

*Begam*: a title of respect for a lady of good family.

*Bismillah*: "In the name of God", said before beginning anything.

*Burqa*: the outer garment that covers a woman from head to foot when she goes out.

*Chhatank*: a small weight, one sixteenth of a ser, about two ounces.

*Doha*: a Hindi couplet, often a proverb or moral saying.

*Domni*: a woman of a caste of hereditary singers who performed at women's gatherings and weddings.

*Fatiha*: the opening chapter of the Qur'an, recited in every prayer and for the souls of the dead.

*Fatwa*: a formal legal opinion given by a scholar of Islamic law.

*Ghari*: a measure of time of about twenty-four minutes.

*Ghazal*: a lyric poem in couplets, usually on love.

*Hadith*: a report of the sayings and doings of the Prophet.

*Hajj*: the pilgrimage to Mecca, one of the five pillars of Islam.

*Hakim*: a physician of the Greek-Arabic (Unani) school of medicine.

*Hanafi*: a follower of the school of law of Imam Abu Hanifa, the school of most Indian Muslims.

*Iddat*: the waiting period a widow (or divorced wife) must keep before she may remarry; for a widow, four months and ten days.

*Kos*: a measure of distance, about two miles.

*Mahr*: the marriage settlement that the husband owes the wife.

*Maktab*: an elementary school, often held in a mosque, where children learn to read the Qur'an.

*Manzil*: one of the sections into which the Qur'an is divided for recitation over a set number of days.

*Maulvi*: a Muslim religious scholar, often one who teaches or leads prayer in a mosque.

*Memsahib*: an English or European lady.

*Missi*: a black tooth powder worn by married women.

*Mufti*: a scholar who gives fatwas.

*Munshi*: a clerk or secretary; also a teacher of Persian and Urdu.

*Murshidzada*: a prince of the Mughal royal house of Delhi.

*Nawab*: a ruler of a princely state or a great noble; here the ruler of Bhopal.

*Nikah*: the Muslim marriage contract, made by offer and acceptance before witnesses.

*Nisab*: the minimum amount of wealth on which zakat becomes due.

*Pice*: a small copper coin, a quarter of an anna.

*Pir*: a Sufi saint or spiritual guide.

*Purdah*: the seclusion of women from men outside the family.

*Qit'a*: a short poem or verse fragment of two or more couplets on one theme.

*Rak'at*: one cycle of standing, bowing and prostration in the Muslim prayer.

*Ramazan*: the month of fasting.

*Sati*: a Hindu widow who burns herself on her husband's funeral pyre; the practice was forbidden by the British in 1829.

*Ser*: a weight, about two pounds.

*Shafi'i*: a follower of the school of law of Imam Shafi'i.

*Shams-ul-Ulama*: "Sun of the Learned", a title given by the British government to eminent oriental scholars.

*Sher*: a couplet of Urdu or Persian verse.

*Sunnat*: the practice of the Prophet, followed as a model by Muslims.

*Surma*: collyrium, a black powder put on the eyelids.

*Tahsildar*: the revenue officer of a subdivision of a district.

*Ta'ziya*: a model of the tomb of Imam Husain carried in procession in Muharram.

*Thanedar*: the police officer in charge of a police station; a sub-inspector.

*Waqf*: property given in perpetual endowment for religious or charitable use.

*Zakat*: the yearly alms due on wealth above the nisab, one of the five pillars of Islam.
