# 37. What do I care whether the world is dear or cheap?

What do I care whether the world is dear or cheap?
If death were offered free I would not take it; what is existence worth?

I have seen settled places and I have seen wildernesses;
the heart that is ruined and never settles again is a strange settlement.

Non-being which is itself the not-being of something: is that called being?
If there is no not-being, there is no being; what kind of being is this being?

The splendours of perfect innocence last only as long as the humility of sin;
if there is lowness, there is height; the secret of height is lowness.

A thing like life is sold in exchange for a single glance;
the rest is up to the buyer; at that price it is cheap.

To turn from the heart's wildness is to turn away from one's God;
madman, this is not sanity; this is the worship of sanity.

The world is empty without you; what has happened to my eyes?
The world was full of people then, the world is full of people now.

What tears there were have dried; yet the heart keeps welling up;
a kind of cloud hangs over the heart, that neither clears nor rains.

Granted the ruin of the heart is easy; its settling is not easy, cruel one;
settling a settlement is no game; a settlement settles slowly.

Fani, the eye in which there was no famine of tears, let alone the heart's blood,
alas, that eye now longs for two drops of water.

# 38. I had only raised my eyes when I was struck

I had only raised my eyes when I was struck;
the eye was spared; the blow fell on the heart.

What would she know of the heart's pain?
Who knows another's wound?

It did not come alone into the house of the heart;
the blow brought pain along with it.

There was no sword in her hand, nor dagger;
who knows what blow she struck?

When the murderer would not believe it otherwise,
I opened my heart and showed the wound.

What else could we do, we who suffer grief?
We bore the blow that fell upon the heart.

Can the heart's wound ever be hidden,
though Fani hid it a hundred thousand ways?

# 39. Restraint was my rule; it is no more

Restraint was my rule; it is no more;
I had some power over my heart; it is no more.

God forgive the late lamented heart;
I had one comforter; it is no more.

Come, the hour of the peace of death has come;
the lament was unpleasant; it is no more.

What do her unkindnesses know?
There was someone who hoped; he is no more.

How long can one trust the sigh?
The sigh was to be trusted; it is no more.

Granted something is favourable to the world;
what was favourable to us is no more.

Now my collar is not torn anywhere;
it was the pastime of the spring season; it is no more.

What remains is the waiting for death;
there was the waiting for you; it is no more.

Kind one, this is Fani's tomb;
there was one who would give his life for you; he is no more.

# 40. Of the beginning I know nothing, nor do I know the end

Of the beginning I know nothing, nor do I know the end;
there remained this illusion that I am, and even that, who knows?

Prayer, well, is prayer; and from prayer there is hope of good too;
if this is the aim, then the outcome of the aim is known.

The secret of acceptance was not revealed; say rather that
otherwise unknown striving was in my fate.

Why should the aim of cruelty be anything but my faithfulness?
The fruit of faithfulness is known: nothing but your cruelty.

Life simply depended on her mercy, in a way,
until she came to know the secret of love as well.

The mysteries of the thought of you opened in self-forgetting;
we could not hide them; otherwise, what would the heart have known?

There is some purpose in the illusion of safety; otherwise
the calm of the boat and the skill of the boatman are known.

The kindness that there was has its limit too;
God's curse on it, that the heart did not know even this.

This is the short account of life, Fani:
the existence of pain is certain, the cure unknown.

# 41. O death, O life of Fani, what have you done?

O death, O life of Fani, what have you done?
You killed the dying man, or did you cure him?

When talk of you came up, I suddenly fell silent;
I hid the heart's secret in such a way that I revealed it.

How tired of me my heart was for restraining longing!
When I spoke, the cruel one disgraced what the heart had done.

See the innocence with which she turned away her eyes:
in the gathering it was as if she had pointed towards me.

Love has done no favour to those who suffered pain from eternity;
when had pain ever left the heart here, that love should have created it?

Again the heart has taken up the refrain of leaving my side;
again someone has made a demand with the eyes alone.

I gave my heart and got grief, true; but tell me this:
did anyone get anything by giving, or give anything by getting?

One tear had escaped the grip of restraint;
the surge of grief made that drop a river again.

Fani in his separation was longing for death today;
you came and made him repent of his longing.

# 42. When grief for you laid the foundation of longing in my heart

When grief for you laid the foundation of longing in my heart,
fate wiped out the world of my comfort.

Now what does the lightning want with every branch for its nest?
The branch it had marked, that branch it burned down.

God knows the longing to declare my love!
I have told this story too a hundred times.

You will not let me live, nor let me die;
have you done away with every custom of love?

Now, Fani, I am counted neither among the living nor the dead;
with what word did she fold up the carpet of mourning?
