# 7. What desire, O heavens, should I seek from you?

What desire, O heavens, should I seek from you?
No heart is left at all with which to long for anything.

All shows of multiplicity would vanish in a moment
if we came before the mirror and cried out "*Hu*" (He, God).

Do not judge us, Shaikh, by our wet skirts, our sinful state;
if we wrung out our skirts, the angels would make their ablutions in the water.

Though like a candle we are tongue from head to foot,
yet where is the daring to say anything?

Though I am a mirror, I am so unwelcome
that whoever I am set before turns his face away.

The rose has no permanence, and we have no trust in life;
on what account should the garden long for colour and scent?

This is my counsel: let all the ascetics of the city,
O Dard, come and pledge allegiance to the hand of the wine jug.

# 8. Coming into the world I looked here and there

Coming into the world I looked here and there;
wherever I looked, it was you alone I saw.

Bodies were emptied of their souls
wherever you looked your fill.

Lament, complaint, sighs and weeping:
whatever I could do, I tried.

Those lips did not work the miracle of Jesus, reviving the dead;
I tried dying in a hundred ways.

He is some powerful one with a lover's temperament;
in short, I have seen Dard.

# 9. How could earth and heaven take in your vastness?

How could earth and heaven take in your vastness?
It is my heart alone in which you can be contained.

In your oneness no word of duality can enter;
how should a mirror dare to show you your face?

I am so fallen that, short of annihilation,
no one can lift me, as no one can lift a footprint.

Messenger, this is not your work; go your way;
who but the heart can bring his message?

Heedless one, never forget the remembrance of God;
forget yourself, if you can forget.

O Lord, what spell is this? Here perception and understanding
may run a thousand ways but cannot go outside themselves.

Even if you settle the matter by argument, what is gained?
Lift the veil from the heart, if you can lift it.

The fire of love is not quenched by the water of tears;
this fire is not one that water can put out.

The one drunk on the wine of love is so beside himself that the Resurrection,
O Dard, may try to bring him to himself, but cannot.

# 10. If this heart goes on tormenting me like this

If this heart goes on tormenting me like this,
one day my very life will go.

I go, leaving my heart with you;
it will keep reminding you of me.

I have taken my heart and left your lane,
but till I arrive it will keep coming back.

The aim of cruelty is the testing of faithfulness;
tell me, how long will you go on testing?

In the cage, O fellow songbirds, will any of you
keep telling us news of the rose?

Displeased, O Dard, you have gone and died;
how long will you go on hiding your grief?

# 11. If one has not seen you yourself appearing here

If one has not seen you yourself appearing here,
it is all one whether one has seen the world or not.

The bud of my heart is so oppressed
that no one has ever seen it open.

Alas, you are unique in being a stranger;
I have never seen another like you.

Torment, misfortune, reproach, calamities:
what have we not seen in love of you?

The scars made me a cypress hung with lamps;
yet you never came to see the spectacle.

Your neglect has shown me these days;
but you, towards me, have not looked, not looked at all.

It was we ourselves who were the veil over the beloved's face;
when the eye opened, I saw no curtain.

Night and day, O Dard, I am in pursuit of him
whom no one here has understood or seen.

# 12. Who has hurt my heart again?

Who has hurt my heart again?
It had gone to sleep; who has woken it?

Where am I, and where the thought of a kiss?
Who has brought mouth to mouth like this?

What does he know of my loving him?
Who has delivered this message?

I too used to see and understand something;
who has suddenly hidden it all?

He used to run from being called, and
who, Dard, has called him to you?
