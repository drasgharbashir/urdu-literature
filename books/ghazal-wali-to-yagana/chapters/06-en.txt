# 25. I made a thousand vows, but the heart's affliction was not turned away

I made a thousand vows, but the heart's affliction was not turned away;
the pain only grew when I treated my heart.

God grant that your heart, like mine, falls for someone,
so that you too go about clutching your liver and saying, "Alas, my heart!"

She took it for a bud and crushed it between her fingers like this;
for her it was only a game; my heart was plundered, alas!

Do not trample my grave; longings are buried in it;
step carefully; take care the heart is not crushed.

On the grave of a disappointed lover it was written:
whoever holds life dear, let him not give his heart anywhere.

Why ask about my griefs? Faithless idols are what one meets;
give up the friendship of idols: this is the heart's advice.

# 26. Who would trust you?

Who would trust you?
Who would wait day after day?

We would speak of love and faithfulness,
but who would put you to shame?

One who is beside himself from that drunken eye,
who would bring him to his senses again?

You are the life of a whole age;
who would sacrifice his life for you?

When you yourself are the calamity of the age,
who would complain of the age?

Keep your rosary, ascetic;
who would count it bead by bead?

In separation let me take poison and die;
who would wait for death?

The eye is a raider, the tresses a hunter;
let us see who will make the heart his prey.

They make no promise; they say:
who would make you hopeful?

Seeing Dagh's face she said:
who would love a face like that?

# 27. She has excuses for not coming, and does not call me either

She has excuses for not coming, and does not call me either;
nor does she tell me why she has stopped our meetings.

At the moment of parting they wait for him to die so they can go;
and then the favour, that they do not leave and go!

Just raise your head, just meet my eyes;
you are not drunk with wine, nor heavy with sleep.

What did you say? Say it again: "I don't listen to you"?
If you don't listen, then I don't speak to people like you.

A fine sort of veil, to sit pressed against the screen:
neither hiding properly, nor coming out in front.

Thin as I am, I still kept pricking your eyes;
delicate as you are, you do not even fit into my sight.

As soon as she saw me in the gathering, this pronouncement came:
"Who is that sitting there? Won't someone get him up?"

When ties are broken off, why the cruelties?
Those who no longer have any interest do not torment either.

If you are tired of life, Dagh, then why do you live?
Life is not dear to you, yet you do not part with it.

# 28. How strange my state would be if I were united with the beloved

How strange my state would be if I were united with the beloved:
now my life would be offered up, now my heart sacrificed.

No mischief would ever be revealed again till the Day of Judgment
if only, cruel one, I had power over your heart.

If anyone made you false promises the way you make them,
say honestly, would you believe him?

There was pleasure in the grief of love if one swallowed it knowingly;
this is a poison that would turn at last into delicious wine.

This was the pleasure of loving, that the fire would burn on both sides:
you would have no rest, and I would have no rest.

There is no pleasure in enmity, and no delight in friendship;
if only a stranger were a stranger and a friend a friend!

I would have waited still longer on your promise, tyrant,
if I could have trusted in my life.

This is not the kind of heartache for which there is any healer;
if it were wiped out once, it would come back a thousand times.

Ascetic, you lost your senses when you saw that drunken eye;
what would it not have done to me, had I not been a wine drinker?

Everyone would so revere me that even my enemies would prostrate themselves;
the beloved's door would become a Ka'ba if my tomb were there.

Why should you not be proud that you have taken Dagh's heart?
You would not have come by this sum, nor had this glory.

# 29. She has never listened to what I say

She has never listened to what I say;
she does not know good from bad at all.

Her flirting is not just flirting;
there is pain in it too, not only laughter.

What shall I tell you, ascetic, of the pleasure of wine?
Alas, wretch, you have never drunk.

Faithfulness has vanished from the world as if
it had never been in anyone at all.

How can I give you my life, when I know
you never gave back this thing once you took it?

I would have made my enemy a friend,
but what can I do? It is not your pleasure.

I live on longing for you;
if this is not there, there is no life at all.

Love is no laughing matter, counsellor;
your heart has not yet been struck [TN: dil-lagi means both "flirting, jest" and "the heart's being struck by love"; the poem plays on it].

Why would Dagh call you faithless?
He is not the complaining kind.

# 30. The heart's furious rushing towards a lovely face

The heart's furious rushing towards a lovely face:
alas, I remember the heart's time.

You too would kiss my mouth, love would come unbidden,
if ever I told you from my heart the tale of the heart.

O God, may these beauties stay childlike for ever;
as soon as they come to their senses, they learn to torment the heart.

How she broke away, struggling, from my arms!
O God, her going was this: the going of the heart.

You have the form of a houri, you are an image of light,
and on top of that you know how to burn the heart.

How shall I leave it and go from your gathering?
To lift the heart is to lift a bier.

When I told her the state of my dejection, she said:
"You have made a home for your heart somewhere else."

After long years, O Dagh, I have understood this:
he alone is wise who did not obey the heart.
