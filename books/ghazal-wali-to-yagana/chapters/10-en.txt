# Glossary

*Alif*: the first letter of the Arabic and Urdu alphabet, a straight upright stroke; an image of the beloved's tall, straight stature.

*Bihzad*: the great Persian miniature painter of the late fifteenth century.

*Farhad*: the lover of Shirin, who cut a channel through a mountain for her and killed himself with his axe on false news of her death; the "mountain-digger" (kohkan).

*Ghazal*: a lyric poem in couplets with one rhyme and often a refrain, usually on love.

*Hu*: "He", that is God; a cry of the Sufis in their remembrance of God.

*Joseph*: the Prophet Joseph of Canaan, the type of perfect beauty, sold by his brothers and bought in the market of Egypt.

*Ka'ba*: the sacred house at Mecca, towards which Muslims pray.

*Kausar*: the river or fountain of Paradise.

*Laila and Majnun*: the Arab lovers of legend; Majnun ("the madman") wandered the desert for love of Laila.

*Maqta*: the last couplet of a ghazal, in which the poet names himself.

*Matla*: the opening couplet of a ghazal, in which both lines rhyme.

*Messiah*: Jesus, whose breath raised the dead; the beloved, or the physician, is a "Messiah" whose lips could give life.

*Moses*: the Prophet Moses, who asked to see God on Mount Sinai and fainted at the sight of His light.

*Nakhuda*: a boatman or ship's captain; the word can also be read as "not-God".

*Qibla*: the direction of the Ka'ba, towards which Muslims pray and the dead are laid.

*Radif*: the refrain: a word or phrase repeated after the rhyme at the end of every couplet of a ghazal.

*Rizwan*: the angel who keeps the gate of Paradise.

*Saqi*: the cup-bearer who serves wine, a stock figure of the ghazal.

*Shaikh*: a religious elder; in the ghazal, the pious hypocrite who reproaches the lover and the drinker.

*Sher*: a couplet of verse, the unit of the ghazal.

*Sufi*: a Muslim mystic; in the ghazal also a figure of outward piety.

*Takhallus*: a poet's pen name, used in the last couplet of a ghazal.

*Zunnar*: the sacred thread worn by Brahmins, and in Persian poetry the belt of the Christian or Zoroastrian; a sign of unbelief.
