# 31. You set no value at all on my heart

You set no value at all on my heart;
the heart's laughter flew away for nothing.

So much is love the habit of the heart
that the heart's joy is bound up with grief.

Let me remember him in every state;
in short, the heart kept its word.

We have had our reward for faithfulness from one
who does not know what the heart's wound is.

He lies at ease, lost in the sleep of coquetry;
we have seen the restlessness of the heart.

Wholly spent in the wakefulness of love:
a strange thing is the self-forgetting of the heart.

Something at least I got from him, even if only grief;
something at least of the heart's honour remained.

We died and were wiped out, and not one
of the heart's longings was fulfilled by you.

When he quarrelled with my rival, Hasrat,
the heart's affair went better still.

# 32. Neither divine love is easy, nor earthly love simple

Neither divine love is easy, nor earthly love simple [TN: haqiqi and majazi, "real" and "figurative" love, i.e. love of God and love of a human beloved];
I have learned how long is the road of love.

Kindness and the glance are not bound to go together;
this too was a longing, that there be no conjuring trick.

The heart understands very well your word of grace,
though it is neither Urdu nor Turkish nor Arabic.

Neither does that beauty of the beloved's face last,
nor does that turmoil of longing remain.

O love, your victory is proved in every case:
even in dying, the martyrs of love became conquerors.

Finish us off quickly somewhere, O grief for the beloved;
on what day will your melting of the breast be of use?

The world knows this truth about Hasrat:
in private he is a wine drinker, in public a man of prayer.

# 33. You went away, making my eyes devoted to waiting

You went away, making my eyes devoted to waiting;
this indeed was a fine and pleasing deed you did.

She went away, making the despairing heart frenzied again,
waking every sleeping mischief.

Under the veil of showing kindness, she went away
making the knots of longing more tangled still.

The secret of longing could not be hidden by us, beside ourselves;
heart and eye both told her their state, and went.

She came to calm my restlessness, but
went away making the soul of restlessness grow.

This is a strange affair: having met Hasrat, she
went away leaving heart and soul still more frenzied.

# 34. Thousands of Muslims are worshippers of that idol

Thousands of Muslims are worshippers of that idol;
in this very unbelief thousands of faiths have been ruined.

The world is lost in love of her face and tresses;
thousands are bewildered, thousands distraught.

Even in solitude, thanks to the thought of you,
there are thousands of ways to keep the heart bound to grief.

O longing, curse the lowness of your courage!
Thousands of things that were easy have become hard.

The eyes have seen you; what grief have they now?
Though the heart still has thousands of longings.

In love of you, my distraction has scoured
thousands of wildernesses of the world of misfortune.

It was one burden, the head upon Hasrat's neck; there will remain
thousands of favours of your sword, O murderer.

# 35. She grew still more distant through neglect

She grew still more distant through neglect
when I tested her by restraining my love.

The heart has not left, and will not leave, the thought of meeting you;
I have tried again and again, reproaching it.

She came to see the effect of her love;
to all appearance, she came to ask after the sick.

The lowness of longing's courage now gives this advice:
sit still, content with the grief of separation.

The heart has gained this high rank in love
by serving your healing pain.

The soul has found release from the pain of parting
by making the memory of you its store of comfort.

Teasing, she now says: "Steady yourself, Hasrat,"
having laid waste the patience and endurance of the sick heart.

# 36. She began to come up on the roof; we began to meet face to face

She began to come up on the roof; we began to meet face to face;
now the declaration of love has begun to be open.

What did I say, that you began to be angry for nothing?
Did you hear anything at all, or did the judgment just come down?

Now the Saqi's glance has begun to fall on the poor as well;
the leftover wine has begun to be granted to us too.

Do not ask what state my restless mind was in
when, compelled, I began to part from her.

The memory of that faithless one began to stay with me every moment;
again, morning and evening, she began to be my talk.

What happened, Hasrat, to your claim of bearing grief in silence?
In just two days you began to complain of the pain of parting.
