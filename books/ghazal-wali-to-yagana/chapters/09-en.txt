# 43. I do not know how to be ashamed, Yas, of the heart's fault

I do not know how to be ashamed, Yas, of the heart's fault;
I do not know how to have another's crime written in my name.

Boatman, after all I have to show my face to someone;
I do not know how to make an excuse and cross over alone.

The mountain of misfortune will be cut through some day after all;
I do not know how to strike my head with the axe and die [TN: unlike Farhad, the lover in the legend, who cut through a mountain for Shirin and killed himself with his axe].

The faint heart is the guest of the slightest knock;
how will he swallow tears who does not know how to bear grief?

I am a secret from head to foot; how shall I tell who I am, what I am?
I understand, but I do not know how to make the world understand.

# 44. Why should anyone be faithful to anyone?

Why should anyone be faithful to anyone?
If the heart does not agree, what can one do?

I want neither medicine nor prayer;
if only someone would treat himself!

A kingly temper in poverty:
for what disease should one find a cure?

If there is pain, a cure too is possible;
what cure can one find for a fancy?

I even laugh, with the surface of my heart;
if the heart is not cheered, what can one do?

Even death could not come when I asked for it;
what more should one beg for?

Let not the heart's pain turn over again somewhere;
God grant that no one is startled now.

The end of the game of love is known;
let whoever wishes begin it gladly.

What else could the mountain-digger have made of it?
If a thing is made and then spoiled, what can one do?

All the light comes from one's own breath;
let one only open the eye of the heart.

What is the candle, what is the candle's light?
Let someone face me when the day has risen.

Between Ghalib and Mirza Yagana,
what judgment should anyone give today?

# 45. The world knows God as God

The world knows God as God;
if this is all it knows, what does it know?

My heart thinks its good lies in this:
that it knows a boatman as God [TN: a play on nakhuda, "boatman", literally "not-God"].

Why should he rack his brains searching for you
who knows where far-reaching thought ends?

Why should God not turn away from such a servant
as only knows how to sit and pray?

What a fine slip of the scribe, that the whole world
knows me alone as error from head to foot!

A strange sinner is this simple man:
he takes what was written for him as his own deed.

Yagana, only you know your own truth;
who knows you but yourself?

# 46. The intoxication of selfhood rose; I could not stay in myself

The intoxication of selfhood rose; I could not stay in myself;
Yagana set out to be God, but could not manage it.

A message under the breath, such that nothing could be heard;
on getting the sign she stretched, and could not hold back.

You who put off the promise of tomorrow with a laugh,
look, has not that same tomorrow come as today?

Call it the sin of high spirits or of hurting hearts:
I laughed at someone so much that I could laugh no more.

The drowning man went on calling on so many;
there were so many gods, yet none came to his rescue.

What did we understand? But we listened to the song of pain;
when it began to be understood, it could be heard no more.

To whom shall I complain of the pain that does not reach?
That it could not take me and enter the heart of the friend.

Seeing the idols, everyone knew God;
to God's house no servant of God went.

I am a worshipper of Krishna, a servant of Ali;
Yagana, seeing the glory of God, could not hold back.

# 47. What demands of the heart has courtesy borne!

What demands of the heart has courtesy borne!
What sides of longing has lust pressed down!

Who knows if it is a slip of the pen or a masterpiece of the pen?
What mischiefs the calamity of beauty has raised!

Whoever the glance fell on was blinded;
what colours of power the eye has shown!

This very deception killed us: how far off is tomorrow?
In this "today, tomorrow" what days we have wasted for nothing!

The cutters of mountains were beaten by the earth;
what rivers have sunk into this same earth!

Passing beyond ourselves, we did reach you,
but do you know what turnings we took?

Rise high, and the power of lowness will be revealed to you;
how the feet of the greatest have tottered!

It would be fitting if I kissed my own feet in joy:
how she has smiled at my stumblings!

God alone knows, Yagana, who I am, what I am;
what doubts about my own self have come into my heart!

# 48. I sit with broken feet: see the scheme!

I sit with broken feet: see the scheme!
The goal clings to my step: see fate!

The servants of love are jeering at me again;
see that the chain does not fall on my feet again.

My death has gone to sleep after making a wager with the dead;
yes, look, just look at the old sky!

Lest your senses fly at the sight of Bihzad's art,
look at the picture with a mirror set before it.

The moths had completed the business of suicide;
the glass lamp-shade came in the way: see fate!

Perhaps, God forbid, the eyes may betray you;
it is not good to look at what fate has written.

The favourable wind has blown; weigh anchor, Yas;
then go forward and see the goodness of fate.
