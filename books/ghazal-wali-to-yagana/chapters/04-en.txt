# 13. I have seen you unclothed, O envy of the moon

I have seen you unclothed, O envy of the moon;
what eye and heart had in view, I have seen.

At my last breath the beloved did not show me his face;
at the hour of departure I have seen who is enemy and who friend.

The heart's wildness took me towards the graves of strangers;
I have seen the house of my departed friends too.

My daring with my life made my rival's heart bleed;
memory has torn open my side and seen my liver.

Never to hear a single verse from the beloved's mouth:
I have seen the effect of my own tongue too.

The skirt of sight was filled with the narcissus flower
whenever you raised your eyes and looked this way.

The mirror took to staying face to face with Atish night and day;
I have seen memory mixed like milk and sugar even with a stranger.

# 14. With envy of fire I rolled on the ground, my collar torn

With envy of fire I rolled on the ground, my collar torn,
when, as she walked, her skirt trailed over the dust.

So strong was my heart's bond with the curving eyebrow
that, seeing the sword's edge bare, I rolled on the ground.

It is right that Moses could not bear the sight of beauty;
without seeing you, the heart of infidel and Muslim was plundered [TN: the verb lota means both "rolled" and "plundered"; the poem plays on it].

When the Feast of Sacrifice drew near, understanding something in his heart,
the jailer came and rolled at my feet.

Like a half-slaughtered bird, thousands of wretched hearts writhed
whenever that laughing rose rolled about with laughter.

When, Atish, I wailed at the beloved's door,
the doorkeeper, clutching his liver with both hands, rolled on the ground.

# 15. From the gold-refiners' craft this truth became clear to me

From the gold-refiners' craft this truth became clear to me:
if wealth is in one's fate, gold will come out of the dust.

From dawn to dusk kicks fly about in the night of union;
with what an insolent, what a shameless one has love been born!

Since you put it in your bud of a mouth,
the scent of the rose has come out of the fibre of the tooth-stick.

Why should we not hold dearer than life the scars of tresses and down?
From what straw and rubbish have we made these flowers grow!

# 16. I did not let the beloved sleep, nor the beloved me

I did not let the beloved sleep, nor the beloved me;
all night long wakeful fortune did not let us sleep.

On the dust, the stone of the beloved's threshold did not let me sleep;
in the sun, the shade of the wall did not let me sleep.

From evening of the night of union to morning my eye did not close;
the joy of the wealth of seeing did not let me sleep.

The restless nightingale's fortune never stayed awake a single night;
at the rose's side the thorn never let it sleep.

When my eye closed, it groaned that I had had a bad dream;
my sick heart did not let me sleep my fill.

From evening I had a headache from the madness of those tresses;
till morning the dark night did not let me sleep.

All night the restless heart talked with me;
the captive of grief and toil did not let me sleep.

The flood of my weeping drove the sleep from people's eyes;
the worry for roof and door and wall did not let them sleep.

In the garden of the world my eyes stayed longing for sleep;
the heat of the fire of the rose garden did not let me sleep.

It is true, nursing the sick is a torment to the soul;
till the moment of death the wretched heart did not let me sleep.

That rose did not keep even a pillow at her side, Atish;
the beloved never let a rival sleep with her.

# 17. In love of the life-giving lip, endure pain

In love of the life-giving lip, endure pain;
fall sick, and bear the airs of the Messiah.

The angels hope for a glance of kindness;
at least raise your eyes towards the world on high.

If this spring madness takes us with it,
pick and gather the scars of the desert tulip.

When thinking of verse, put the pen to work;
spur the horse on the field of battle.

If love shows us the splendour of the beloved's beauty,
in how many ways may we take pleasure in the spectacle!

When the beloved is as lovely as you, why should one not then
bear his fitting coquetry and his unfitting glances?

I am penniless a hundred thousand times over, but this passion has gripped my heart:
to buy Joseph on credit and bear the dunning.

The season of spring has come; drink wine, you Sufis;
enough of prayer; take up the prayer-mat.

The Saqi stands holding a cup of pure wine;
at least lift your neck, like the neck of the flask.

You have blessed the ears by letting them hear your voice;
have pity on the eyes too: lift the curtain.

In the surge of madness, look, do not turn back again;
whichever way you set your face, go like the river.

Let the beloved be a swordsman, a brave young man;
Atish, take up the challenge of the holy war of love.

# 18. This was my longing: to set you face to face with the rose

This was my longing: to set you face to face with the rose,
and the restless nightingale and I would talk.

It was good that no messenger was to be had;
why should I explain my longing through another's tongue?

Like me, the moon and the sun too are wanderers;
they too are searching for some beloved.

The colour of the world keeps changing for ever;
at last the black-haired turn white.

We would squander the wealth of the world in the tavern,
making golden wine cups and silver jugs.

I have always torn my collar to shreds;
all their lives the menders went on mending.

If they saw the state of the chain of your tresses,
the free would long to be made captive.

If I called the whiteness of the beloved's neck the dawn,
I would make the morning star the button at the throat.

Not without reason is the beloved's face likened to the Ka'ba;
not without cause do they turn the dead to face the qibla.

I would teach the cry of the night watch to break in,
I would make this sky the enemy of the grief of separation.

If that life of my life did not come, then death itself should come;
how long, after all, should I turn heart and liver into blood?

Do not ask, Atish, the state of my ill fortune:
fire rained down when I longed for rain.
