# 19. Who is there who does not grieve over me?

Who is there who does not grieve over me?
But see my courage: I do not say "ah".

What a calamity! There is still a pause before his coming,
and my breath makes no delay in going.

Lest some other suspicion pass through your heart, faithless one,
I do not breathe the Sura of Joseph over myself [TN: the Qur'an's chapter on Joseph, the type of beauty; reciting it might suggest a rival beloved].

My rival there does not read my letter in any form
until he has tampered with its contents.

My heart is so rich with the wealth of poverty
that I do not even spit on the gold and goods of the world.

Until the Sufi cleans his heart with pure wine,
the science of Sufism does him no good and no purity.

O Zauq, in formality there is nothing but trouble;
he is at ease who does not stand on formality.

# 20. Talk of youth in old age

Talk of youth in old age
is like talk of a dream.

There, look, it has taken me that way again:
the talk of my ruined heart.

Preacher, leave the mention of Paradise's blessings;
talk of wine and kebabs.

Moon-browed one, do you remember, or have you forgotten,
that talk on the moonlit night?

If my honour has come to be blamed,
it is the doing of my tearful eyes.

Teasing and teasing her, how we relish
hearing her angry words!

Just put the wine cup to your lips;
leave off this talk of shame and modesty.

They will disgrace me well, O heart,
these restless words of yours.

Go away; my palpitations only grow worse
on hearing the words of his honour the counsellor.

The tale of the beloved's tresses, for the heart,
is a strange matter of twists and torment.

What place, O Zauq, in the surge of love,
is there for us to talk of patience and endurance?

# 21. On that stone threshold is the brow of devotion

On that stone threshold is the brow of devotion;
that is my prayer-mat, and this is my prayer.

It is with the one who is out of tune with us that this tune is played;
what a fine heart it is, bravo, of which we are so proud!

The rival reached there at night by throwing up a rope;
it is true, the bastard's rope is long [TN: a proverb: the wicked prosper].

If even God loved that idol I would feel jealous,
though I know He is pure.

I praise the moles on the faces of idols; if God
forgives me, what wonder? He values a fine point.

I fear his dagger may melt into water and flow away;
in my throat is a lament that melts iron.

Do not shut the tavern door, inspector of morals;
fear God, cruel one, for the door of repentance is open.

See the ruin of the house of the heart sick with grief:
it is the home-made medicine that is bad.

Instead of dew, playfulness drips from the rose;
whose dust is in the garden, of those martyred by coquetry?

Do not sigh and cry out, Zauq, lest the heart's state be revealed;
every lament is a key to the door of the treasury of secrets.

# 22. The lust for the world drags everyone about in disgrace

The lust for the world drags everyone about in disgrace;
who goes about? It is this carrion that drags us about.

The sun would never come out of his house,
but the lust for a brisk market drags him about.

Such is the backward turning of my star of fortune
that it drags even the sky about upside down.

What sign did your eyebrow make, murderer,
that fate goes about with a sword in her hand?

Where I was to go once and never come back,
restlessness drags me a hundred times.

# 23. Ascetic, do not tease the ruined drunkard

Ascetic, do not tease the ruined drunkard;
why bother with others' affairs? Settle your own.

God grant you no nails, O hand of madness;
you would unpick all the stitches of reason.

Slaughter this writhing prey with patience;
do not smear skirt and sleeve with blood.

Who is released by death, once caught in the net of tresses?
On his grave you will find a tree of snares.

Two-faced ascetic, do not make yourself out an elder;
like the false dawn, you are still only middle-aged.

This narrow pass of the world is no place of ease;
heedless one, do not stretch out the feet of greed; draw them in.

If the tree of affection is cut and grows green again,
what would it matter if you tore it out by the roots?

If he wakes the sleeping crowd again to cause an uproar,
shut the door of the house on this dog of the world.

The swift steed of passing life was given you
so that you might quickly spur it away from here.

Lift your hand from wandering in the lane of love;
Zauq, you will not be able to bear this trouble.

# 24. Whenever I intend to visit you

Whenever I intend to visit you,
the mirrors make their ablutions with my tearful eyes.

They show their hidden enmity
when they praise my rival in front of me.

Such is the state of my heart that it tears in a hundred places
if I mend it in one.

I could break this begging bowl of the sky with one lament, but
sometimes I drink the heart's blood from it.

Perhaps they have not seen your heart-seeking stature,
that the cypresses by the stream are so proud.
