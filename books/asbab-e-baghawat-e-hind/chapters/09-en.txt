# Copy of the Proclamation

[TN: This is the proclamation of the Lieutenant-Governor of Bengal that Sir Syed refers to in the First Cause. It is printed in Persian at the end of the source edition and is translated here in full; the Persian original is on the facing page.] It has lately come to the hearing of His Honour the Lieutenant-Governor of Bengal that certain persons, out of bigotry and ignorance, and merely to bewilder and trouble the public, have spread and published some baseless and unworthy talk about the religion, faith, customs and ways of Hindus and Muslims, so that on hearing it anxious fears have lodged in people's hearts. His Honour the Lieutenant-Governor is much surprised and grieved that the inhabitants of this country, without finding out the truth of the matter, should, merely through the mischief of mischief-makers, burden themselves with alarm. Therefore, by means of a proclamation, the true facts about these inventions which have reached His Honour's truth-seeking ear are made public, so that all people may know the truth of the matter and be assured with certainty that the Government has no interference or hindrance of any kind in the faith, religion, ways, customs and practices of its subjects, nor will it have any in future; on the contrary, the protection of their lives and property and their honour and dignity is its aim, and excellent efforts in this regard are being made and will be made.

First: certain padris of Calcutta, in their own manner and as their usual practice, printed leaves of questions on religion and faith by way of debate and disputation, enclosed them in envelopes and sent them to Hindustanis generally, and these people, through their own misunderstanding, supposed that such writings had appeared at the hint of the everlasting Government. Yet the Government has no knowledge or awareness of them whatever; and it has never at all been the way of the exalted Government to urge or induce any of its subjects towards its own faith and religion. For it is plain that the subjects of this country are people of every kind and hold separate faiths, religions, creeds and rites, and their necks are under the yoke of the authority of the exalted Government, and its eye of kindness and favour is equal and alike upon them all. Through the long course of the everlasting Government's rule, no hindrance or interference with the creed and faith of any Muslim or of any other religion has ever been made. The padris carry on matters of this kind on their own account, and all this is as it were part of their usual habits, just as Muslims and Hindus preach and give counsel in their mosques and temples, set forth religious matters and urge obedience and the avoidance of what is forbidden. If it is considered, it will be quite clear that this is nothing new or novel; the way of debate and disputation has always gone on among the learned of different religions, and the Government has no connection with any of it.

Second: it has been reported in some newspapers and has also spread among the common people that the Government is now about to pass such laws as would put an end to, and stop altogether, the custom of *ta'ziya* (the Muharram mourning processions), the rites of circumcision, the seclusion of the women of good families, and other ordinances of the *shar'* (Islamic law) and the *shastras* (Hindu scriptures). This too is false and a sheer fabrication. The Government has no wish to interfere in the ways, customs, creed and religion of anyone; indeed this would be against the way and the care for subjects that is the approved nature of the Government.

Third: the superintendents of the jails in some districts, without the knowledge and awareness of the exalted Government, had issued an order to take away the prisoners' vessels for eating and drinking, with the idea of marking a difference between the hardships of prison and the comforts of home. But when the Government learned that this was harmful to their religion, and that the jail superintendent had issued such an order in ignorance, a firm order withdrawing it was sent at once by electric telegraph.

Fourth: it has come to the just ear of His Honour that the inhabitants of this country take the founding of colleges, the means of learning, the acquiring of knowledge and the spreading of the English language to be means of changing their faith and destroying the foundations of their religion; and that this is why many people are slack and careless in acquiring knowledge and perfecting skills, and some persons are unwilling to send their children to school. Plainly the source of this is nothing but lack of understanding and lack of knowledge. The real truth is this: when it was established before the exalted Government that the subjects of this country, through want of learning and skill, are so ignorant of ways of earning a living that they are unable to pass their days in comfort and ease, then, by the high order of Her Majesty the Queen of England, issued out of royal bounty, schools and colleges were founded with all care and at great expense in every district and city for their education and training; in every district gentlemen were appointed as inspectors, and many Hindustanis as their deputies, for the work of education; and further emphasis was laid on teaching and the acquisition of knowledge, skills, the English language and the like, so that the people of this country in general may be freed from ignorance and want of knowledge, may by gaining learning and knowledge earn a good living, and, coming out of the straits of hardship and want, may pass their days in happiness and comfort.

It is no secret that the inhabitants of Europe, that is, of England, through the acquisition of learning, carry out every kind of work excellently and completely by the reach of their able minds; unlike the people of this land, who, through want of learning and knowledge, are wholly without skill. If learning, skill, understanding and knowledge were spread among them, each one would gather all the means of comfort and ease, [TN: The next clause is unclear in the source; a literal reading is "and would receive the royal favour as is fitting, and would carry kindness to its proper place".] It is a pity and a grief beyond telling. His Honour the Lieutenant-Governor supposes that all these corrupt ideas arise from misunderstanding, not from bigotry or ill will. It should be known that the Government's purpose in English training and education is not that any harm should come to their religion and rites; on the contrary, everyone is free to take up whatever learning and skill he likes and finds pleasing and thinks useful. But this too should be known: at present books and treatises on every subject exist in the English language, and new experiments and fresh inventions are always coming forward that are not to be had in any other language; English is the language of the ruler of the country and of the sovereign power; and in the courts the current language of this country is used so that the common people may understand and be understood. In these circumstances, acquiring and perfecting the English, Urdu and Bengali languages is without doubt the way to a livelihood and to advancement in dignity, honour and fortune, and is a duty.

Let it not be hidden that, since His Honour the Lieutenant-Governor has seen the conditions of this land with his own eyes and heard of them from many persons, the high resolve of His Honour is wholly given to improving the state of the inhabitants of this country, to establishing ways of education and training, and to the comfort and ease and the protection of the honour and dignity of every one of them; and, out of the greatest kindness and sympathy, the improvement of the condition of the nobles, the gentry, the zamindars and the subjects is especially in view.

It is therefore proclaimed that all the inhabitants of this country, knowing and being assured of the good intentions and high resolve of the exalted Government, should give thanks to God, pass all their days in full assurance, and be engaged in praying for the everlasting continuance of the fortune of the prosperous Government.
