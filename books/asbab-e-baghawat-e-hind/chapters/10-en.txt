# Glossary

*Anna*: a coin worth one sixteenth of a rupee.

*Ain-e-Akbari*: Abul Fazl's account of the institutions of Akbar's empire, including the land revenue system.

*Bais*: the Vaishya castes of traders and moneylenders.

*Bid'ati*: one who introduces innovations into religion; a term of reproach.

*Bigha*: a measure of land, varying by region, roughly a third to five-eighths of an acre.

*Chaprasi*: an orderly or messenger attached to an office or police station.

*Chaudhri*: the headman of a village community or caste group.

*Darbar*: a royal court or a formal public audience held by a ruler.

*Dharam*: religion, religious duty; for a Hindu, also caste purity.

*Diwani*: the right to collect the land revenue and administer civil justice, granted to the East India Company in Bengal in 1765; also civil (as opposed to criminal) jurisdiction.

*Farman*: a royal decree.

*Fatwa*: a ruling on a point of Islamic law given by a qualified scholar.

*Fiqh*: Islamic jurisprudence.

*Hadith*: the recorded sayings and doings of the Prophet Muhammad.

*Jagir*: a grant of land or of its revenue made by a ruler, usually for service.

*Jihad*: in law, war waged for religion under conditions fixed by Islamic law; Sir Syed argues that those conditions were absent in 1857.

*Jizya*: the poll tax levied on non-Muslims under Muslim rule; reimposed by Aurangzeb Alamgir in 1679.

*Kala padri*: 'black padri', the villagers' name for the Indian school inspectors.

*Katha*: the public recitation and exposition of Hindu sacred stories.

*Kayasth*: a Hindu caste of scribes and officials, long employed in government service.

*Kristan*: a Christian (popular form of the word).

*Maulvi*: a Muslim religious scholar.

*Maund*: a measure of weight, about 37 kilograms.

*Muafi*: a rent-free grant of land, exempt from revenue.

*Murid*: the disciple of a Sufi master.

*Mustamin*: in Islamic law, one living under a granted protection (aman) in a non-Muslim state, who may not wage war against it.

*Na'ra-e-haidari*: the war cry 'Ya Ali!', invoking the Caliph Ali (Haidar).

*Padri*: a Christian clergyman or missionary.

*Pargana*: a revenue subdivision of a district; a 'pargana visitor' inspected village schools.

*Polaj, parauti, chachar, banjar*: the four classes of land in Akbar's revenue system: land cultivated every year, land rested now and then, land fallow three or four years, and land fallow five years or more.

*Purdah*: the seclusion of women from the sight of men outside the family.

*Qizilbash*: the Turkman soldiery of Iran, the backbone of Nadir Shah's army.

*Sahib*: a European, especially a British officer.

*Seer*: a measure of weight, about one kilogram.

*Shar' and shastras*: the religious law of Islam and the sacred law books of Hinduism.

*Subedar*: the senior Indian officer of a company of sepoys.

*Taluqdar*: the holder of a taluqa, a large estate made up of many villages, responsible for their revenue; powerful landlords in Oudh.

*Ta'ziya*: the replica of Imam Husain's tomb carried in Muharram processions.

*Thug*: a member of the gangs who befriended and strangled travellers, suppressed in the 1830s.

*Tilanga*: a sepoy, an Indian infantry soldier of the Company's army.

*Zamindar*: a landholder responsible for paying the land revenue on his estate.

*Zill-Allah*: 'the shadow of God', a title given to kings.
