# Ghazals

The counting of the rosary pleased my idol who loves what is hard:
the spectacle of carrying off a hundred hearts in one palm pleased her.

By the grace of heartlessness, eternal despair is easy;
unravelling itself took a liking to our hard knot.

The wish to stroll among the roses is a mirror of the killer's unkindness,
for the way the wounded bird rolls in its blood pleased him.

In this world the mark of faithfulness was no source of comfort;
it is a word that was never beholden to a meaning.

The green down on your cheek did not subdue your rebellious curl;
this emerald too proved no match for the serpent's breath.

I had wished to be free of the sorrow of faithfulness;
that tyrant was not content even with my dying.

Let the heart be the highway of thoughts of wine and cup,
if my breath did not become the road to the goal of piety.

I am content even with your not promising, for never
was my ear beholden to the rose-song of consolation.

To whom should one complain of being cheated by fate?
We had wished to die, and even that did not happen.

Ghalib died from the shock of a single movement of the lips;
in his weakness he was no match for the breath of Jesus.

Do not be stingy with wine today for the sake of tomorrow;
that is a suspicion against the Cupbearer of Kausar.

Why are we disgraced today, when until yesterday we would not allow
even an angel's insolence in our presence?

Why does the soul start to leave the body at the time of listening,
if that voice has entered the harp and the rebec?

The steed of life is galloping; let us see where it stops:
the hand is not on the rein, nor the foot in the stirrup.

I am as far from my own reality
as I am in torment from the illusion of the other.

The origin of the seeing, the seer and the seen is one;
I am bewildered: on what account, then, is there beholding?

The being of the sea consists in the showing of forms;
what is there here in the drop, the wave and the bubble?

Modesty is one gesture of coquetry, even if towards oneself;
how unveiled they are, who are veiled like this!

She is not yet done with adorning her beauty;
a mirror is always before her eyes inside the veil.

What we take for the seen is hidden, wholly hidden;
those who have woken in a dream are still in a dream.

Ghalib, from the Friend's companion comes the scent of the Friend;
I am busy with God in the service of Bu Turab.

A sigh needs a lifetime to take effect;
who lives until your curl is won?

In the net of every wave is the ring of a hundred crocodile-jaws;
let us see what the drop goes through before it becomes a pearl.

Love asks for patience, and desire is restless;
what colour shall I give my heart until my liver turns to blood?

I grant that you will not neglect me, but
I shall be dust by the time you hear of it.

From the sun's rays the dew learns its passing away;
I too last only until one glance of kindness.

The span of existence is no more than one glance, heedless one;
the warmth of the gathering lasts as long as a spark's dance.

Who but death can cure the grief of existence, Asad?
The candle burns in every colour until the dawn.

It was not my fate that I should be united with my love;
had I lived on longer, it would have been this same waiting.

If I lived on your promise, then know it was a lie;
for would I not have died of joy, had I believed it?

From your delicacy I knew the vow was loosely tied;
you could never have broken it, had it been firm.

Let someone ask my heart about your half-drawn arrow:
where would this ache come from, had it passed right through the liver?

What kind of friendship is this, that friends have turned preachers?
If only one of them were a healer, one a comforter!

Blood would drip from the vein of a stone, and never stop,
if this which you take for grief were a spark.

To whom shall I tell what the night of grief is? It is an evil calamity;
what harm would dying do me, if it happened only once?

Though grief takes the life, how can one escape it while one has a heart?
If there were not the grief of love, there would be the grief of earning a living.

Since I was disgraced by dying, why was I not drowned in the river?
There would never have been a funeral, nor a tomb anywhere.

Who could see Him, for He is unique, the One?
If there were even a scent of twoness, one would meet Him somewhere.

These questions of Sufism, and this exposition of yours, Ghalib!
We would take you for a saint, if you were not a drinker.

My pain was never beholden to a medicine;
I did not get well, and that was not bad.

Why do you gather the rivals together?
It turned into a show, not a complaint.

Where shall I go to try my luck,
when you yourself would not try your dagger on me?

How sweet your lips are, that the rival
took your abuse and did not find it bitter!

The news is hot that she is coming,
and today of all days there is not even a mat in the house.

Was it the godhead of Nimrod,
that my service did me no good?

I gave my life; but the life given was His;
the truth is that the debt was not paid.

If the wound closed, the blood did not stop;
if the work was halted, it was not done.

Is it highway robbery, or the stealing of hearts?
Taking the heart, the heart-stealer has gone his way.

Do say something, for people are saying
that today Ghalib has sung no *ghazal*.

No hope is fulfilled;
no way out is seen.

A day for death is fixed;
why does sleep not come all night?

Before, I used to laugh at the state of my heart;
now nothing makes me laugh.

I know the reward of obedience and abstinence,
but my nature does not turn that way.

There is some such thing that I am silent;
otherwise, do I not know how to speak?

Why should I not cry out? They miss me
when my voice does not reach them.

If the scar on the heart cannot be seen,
does not even the smell reach you, O healer?

I am where even I
get no news of myself.

I am dying in longing for death;
death comes, and yet it does not come.

With what face will you go to the Kaaba, Ghalib?
But then, you feel no shame.

Though the moon's beauty is fine when it is at the full,
my moon with the face of the sun is finer than that.

She gives no kiss, yet every moment her eye is on my heart;
she thinks to herself: goods that come free are good.

If it breaks, I can bring another from the bazaar:
better than Jamshed's cup is this earthen cup of mine.

When they give without being asked, there is more pleasure in it;
that beggar is good who has not the habit of asking.

When at the sight of them a glow comes to my face,
they think the sick man's state is good.

Let us see what profit lovers will have from the idols;
a Brahmin has said that this year is good.

The adze made Farhad speak with Shirin;
whatever skill a man may have is good.

If the drop merges in the river, it becomes the river;
that deed is good whose end is good.

May the Creator, the Greatest, keep Khizr Sultan green;
in the King's garden this fresh sapling is good. [TN: Khizr Sultan was a son of the emperor Bahadur Shah Zafar.]

I know the truth about Paradise, but
to keep the heart amused, Ghalib, the idea is good.

This was the form that the epiphany of light desired:
fortune opened for the appearing of your stature and face.

In one blood-dripping shroud there are millions of adornments;
the eye of the houri falls upon your martyrs.

Preacher, you neither drink nor can you give anyone to drink;
what a thing it is, your pure wine of Paradise!

At the Resurrection my killer quarrels with me: why did I rise?
As if he had not yet heard the sound of the Trumpet.

Spring is coming, since the nightingale is singing:
a flying rumour from the tongues of the birds.

Though they are not there, they are at least cast out from there;
these idols too have a distant kinship with the Kaaba.

Is it a duty that all should get the same answer?
Come, let us too go for a stroll on Mount Sinai.

Grant there is warmth in his speech, but not so much
that whoever he spoke with would surely complain.

Ghalib, if your Honour would take me with you on this journey,
I will offer the reward of the Hajj to your Honour.

The Friend's cruelty is news of safety for the soul;
no way of tyranny is left for the sky.

What do I care if the beloved's eyelash thirsts for blood?
Let me keep some for my own blood-shedding lashes too.

We are that dead man, O Khizr, whom all the world knows;
not you, who turned thief for eternal life. [TN: "dead man" (*murda*) is as printed; the usual text of the *diwan* reads *zinda*, "living".]

In every calamity I am caught by the calamity of jealousy too;
your grace is a calamity to the soul of a whole world.

O heaven, do not keep me far from him, for is there anyone but me
to test the long reach of the killer?

My striving is like this: a captive bird
gathering straws in the cage for a nest.

Taking me for a beggar he was silent; then my ill luck came over me:
I rose, and rising, I touched the watchman's feet.

The narrow vessel of the *ghazal* is too small for my longing;
something more is needed, room for my telling.

He has given to the people too, so that no evil eye may strike it:
pleasure was made for Tajammul Husain Khan.

O Lord, whose name has come upon my tongue,
that my speech has kissed my tongue?

Helper of state and faith, supporter of nation and realm,
for whose threshold the highest heaven was made.

In his reign the age is absorbed in adorning itself;
more stars will now be made for the sky.

The page is finished, and the praise remains;
a ship is needed for this boundless sea.

With a grace of his own Ghalib has become a teller of fine points;
it is an open invitation to friends who know fine points.
