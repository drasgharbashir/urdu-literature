# Glossary

*Abjad*: the system of giving each Arabic letter a number, used to make chronograms.

*Ahl-e Bait*: the Family of the Prophet: 'Ali, Fatima, Hasan, Husain and their descendants.

*'Aruz*: prosody, the science of Persian and Urdu metres.

*Burhan-e Qati'*: a Persian dictionary compiled in India in the seventeenth century, which Ghalib attacked in *Qati'-e Burhan*.

*Chausar*: a board game played with dice or cowries, often for stakes.

*Chikni dali*: betel nut boiled and dried, eaten with or without *pan*.

Chronogram (*tarikh*, *madda-e tarikh*): a word or phrase whose letters, by *abjad*, add up to the year of an event.

*Darbar*: a royal or official court assembly; *darbar-e 'amm*, the open court.

*Dastambu*: Ghalib's Persian diary of the Revolt of 1857 in Delhi.

*Dirafsh-e Kawiyani*: the enlarged second edition (1865) of *Qati'-e Burhan*.

*Diwan*: a poet's collected lyric poems, arranged by rhyme letter; *kulliyat*, his complete works.

*Diwan-e 'Amm*: the Hall of Public Audience in the Red Fort, Delhi, where *mushaira*s were also held.

*Farhang-e Nasiri*: the Persian dictionary (*Farhang-e Anjuman-ara-ye Nasiri*) of the Iranian scholar Riza Quli Khan Hidayat.

*Ghazal*: a lyric poem in couplets on one rhyme and refrain, usually on love; its opening couplet is the *matla'* and its closing couplet, with the poet's pen name, the *maqta'*.

*Hadith*: a reported saying or deed of the Prophet.

*Haveli*: a large town house built round courtyards.

*Hawadar*: an open sedan chair carried by bearers.

*Huma*: a fabulous bird; whoever its shadow falls on is said to become a king.

*Jagir*: a grant of land revenue for service; *mansab*, a rank in Mughal service.

*Katra*: a walled market or block of shops and dwellings let out for rent.

*Latifa*: a witticism or anecdote; Hali's marginal heading for Ghalib's jokes.

*Marsiya*: an elegy, especially on the martyrdom of Imam Husain at Karbala; *nauha*, a lament.

*Masnavi*: a long poem in rhyming couplets, narrative or didactic.

*Mihr-e Nimroz*: Ghalib's Persian history of the house of Timur up to Humayun, the first part of the planned *Partavistan*; the second part, *Mah-e Nim-mah*, was never written.

*Mirza*: a title of Mughal and Turkish gentlemen; Hali calls Ghalib simply "the Mirza".

*Misra*: a half-line of verse; a couplet (*sher*, *bait*) has two.

*Mujtahid*: a senior Shi'a jurist; *Mujtahid-ul-'Asr*, the chief Shi'a divine of the age, at Lucknow.

*Mushaira*: a gathering at which poets recite their verse, often on a set pattern (*tarh*).

*Nazr*: a ceremonial offering, usually of coins, made to a ruler or superior.

*Pan*: betel leaf with lime, catechu and betel nut; a *gilauri* or *bira* is a folded *pan*.

*Pargana*: a revenue district; a *sarkar* was a larger division of a province (*suba*).

*Qasida*: a formal ode, usually in praise of a patron, opening with a prelude (*tashbib*) on another theme.

*Qati'-e Burhan*: Ghalib's critique (1862) of the dictionary *Burhan-e Qati'*, which set off a long controversy.

*Qibla*: the direction of the Ka'ba; used of a revered person (*qibla-gah*, *qibla o ka'ba*, "revered sir").

*Qit'a*: a verse fragment, a short poem in couplets without an opening rhymed couplet.

*Rafizi*: a hostile Sunni name for a Shi'a; *Tafzili*, one who holds 'Ali superior to the other caliphs without being a Shi'a.

*Rekhta*: the older name for Urdu as a language of poetry.

*Rind*: a libertine, a drinker who scorns outward piety, a favourite figure of Persian poetry; *rind-mashrabi*, the libertine's free spirit.

*Ruba'i*: a quatrain in a set metre, rhyming in the first, second and fourth lines.

*Sulh-e kull*: "peace with all", tolerance towards every creed.

*Takhallus*: a poet's pen name, used in the closing couplet; Hali records that Ghalib wrote as Ghalib in Persian and as Asad in Urdu.

*Taqriz*: a commendation written in praise of another author's book.

*Tarkib-band*: a poem in stanzas, each closed by a linking couplet on a different rhyme.

*Tasawwuf*: Sufism, Islamic mysticism; *tauhid-e wujudi* or *wahdat-ul-wujud*, the doctrine of the unity of being, that God alone truly exists.

*Tazkira*: a biographical anthology of poets.

Wahhabi: the name given in nineteenth-century India to followers of the reformist movement of Sayyid Ahmad and Shah Isma'il Shahid.
