# 48. The Curzon Sabha

Friends, in the assembly Curzon's arrival is at hand;
among the flowers, the arrival of the envy of the garden is at hand.

Chiefs and rajas and nawabs are waiting eagerly,
for the arrival of the deputy of the King of London is at hand.

He comes as the representative of the Emperor of India;
among the stars the arrival of the bright moon is at hand.

With him are so many grandees of Europe
that it is as if the arrival of London in Delhi is at hand.

The aim is that adornment and splendour be complete;
the arrival of every science and every art is at hand.

Water and fire are seen with their belts girded;
from this side the machine, from that side the engine's arrival is at hand.

The skilled are showing the dreams of magnetism;
in hearts the arrival of an enlightened state is at hand.

Plenty is pouring in from every side;
the arrival of the harvest-heap of every commodity is at hand.

With the coming of the troops there is a world of glitter;
wherever you look, the arrival of a regiment is at hand.

There is the flash of swords, on every side the boom of cannon;
the arrival of jingle-jangle and bang-bang is at hand.

There is bustle, there are high hopes, there is the fervour of intoxication;
upon the spring of pleasure the arrival of full bloom is at hand.

Those who are old have the ardour of youth;
those who are young, the arrival of childhood is at hand.

In every religion and community an attraction has arisen;
the arrival of magus, shaikh and Brahmin is at hand.

There is no gold in the purse, and [illegible] is binding and obligatory;
for this reason the arrival of the moneylender is at hand.

The grace of speech keeps Akbar's heart lifted up,
although the arrival of old age and [illegible] is at hand. [TN: Written for the Delhi Durbar of 1903 held by the Viceroy Lord Curzon. The refrain amad amad hai means "the arrival is at hand", "is about to come".]

# 52. A moral counsel

People say that a son is the light of the eyes,
the delight of life, the joy of the heart.

Through him there is light in the house on every side;
the father is proud of him, the mother vain of him.

They take him for a sign of good fortune;
they say he is a manifestation of God's grace.

Akbar too agrees with this idea;
he too says that it is surely so,

on condition, however, that the son is promising,
inclined to good, and far from evil;

that he listens attentively to the counsel of the elders,
and when he speaks his lips say "sir" and "your honour";

that his conduct is full of truth and love,
with no deceit in it, no cunning or falsehood;

that he shares his parents' cares with all his heart,
sympathetic, helpful, a man of understanding;

that whatever his father thinks best, he is content with it,
patient, respectful, sensible and self-respecting;

that he keeps the honour of the family in mind,
a friend of the good, averse to bad company;

that day and night he is set on acquiring excellence,
his heart full of the love of learning and skill.

But if there is no trace of these qualities at all,
and still there is joy, then it is the joy that is at fault.

# 53. A national poem, at the request of Nawab Mohsin-ul-Mulk Bahadur

Muslims, tell me, do you know anything of your own state?
Do you see at all what has become of your rank?
If you do, then think: has it any effect on your heart?
Does your rivals' boasting burn your liver at all?
Do you know at all what you have become from what you were?
Where have you strayed to, parted from the road of progress?

No one was ahead of you in the race of progress;
if one in ten shone, you stood out in a hundred.
It was you who showed everyone the difference between wheat and barley;
it was by learning from you that the Western nations became learned.
You had gained honour by telling truth from falsehood;
even your opponents valued you from the heart.

Yours were honours, yours the height, the rank, the glories;
yours was the word, yours the commands, yours the say, yours the dignity;
the tongues of the world were busy speaking of you;
you, only you, were in the world, and yours the stories.
A whole world had to lower its pride and its airs;
a whole world had to bow its head in submission.

Your mutual unity was a wall of iron;
whoever opposed one of you was as good as the enemy of all.
Your courage had its nest on the Highest Throne;
in your hands was every science and every art of the world.
By your devotion to truth you held the world down;
by bowing before God you made your enemies bow.

There were none of these mutual quarrels, none of this unjust self-worship;
the demon of the self had not this violent hold over the mind;
there was no suspicion in the heart, no such lowness in courage;
in your sight the whole of existence showed forth the light of reality.
Your bearing was charming, your dignity lofty;
your good manners showed forth the glory of beauty.

Alas, that conduct of yours is no longer left;
neither that beauty of action remains, nor that good opinion of others;
neither that taste for skill, nor the love of learning and art remains;
nor does that fervour of love for friends and homeland remain in your hearts.
If you have any cares, they are for the comfort of your own selves;
is it on this that you expect God's kindness?

What a calamity: everyone's breast is empty of the Islamic spirit;
there is envy, there is weakness, there is unkindness, there is spite;
each one lives only for his own pleasure;
just think, is this the ladder of national progress?
Where now is the custom of mutual affection among the Muslims?
If any is left among the poets, then it is a sickness of affection.

What shall I tell you of what passes over my heart at this moment?
When the thought comes into my heart, my eyes fill with tears;
my mind can hardly stay steady enough even to speak;
there is such a pang in my breast that it makes me restless.
"I have a pain in my heart; if I speak, my tongue will burn;
and if I hold my breath, I fear the marrow of my bones will burn."

Learn the things by which nations are becoming renowned:
rise, learn civilisation, learn the crafts, learn skills;
increase your experience, learn to travel to the ends of the world;
learn the properties of dry and wet, learn the sciences of sea and land.
For God's sake, young men, come to your senses;
give self-respect a place in your hearts, be fired with zeal.

Fine speech, reasonable and measured, pleases everyone's heart;
the words of the eloquent change colour with meaning;
words of praise and eulogy come from the tongue every moment;
but only when there is a real desire to act does the work go forward.
If the attention is not from the heart, how can there be any effect,
whether it be Akbar's charming verse or Mahdi's lecture? [TN: Nawab Mohsin-ul-Mulk (1837–1907) succeeded Sir Sayyid as the leader of the Aligarh movement. The Persian couplet in the seventh stanza is a well-known verse. "Mahdi" is probably Nawab Mahdi Ali Khan, that is, Mohsin-ul-Mulk himself.]

# 54. Mr Darwin was very far from the truth

Mr Darwin[1] was very far from the truth;
I will not accept that your ancestors were *langurs* (monkeys).

One should act in keeping with one's own condition;
what does it matter if one's grandfathers were Caesar and the Faghfur?

This nearness gives us no occasion for pride;
though they sat close by, they were far from their hearts. [TN: The Faghfur is the emperor of China in Persian usage.]

# 55. The wind of heresy is changing the colour of the community on every path

The wind of heresy is changing the colour of the community on every path;
how can what has gone wrong be put right? What has started keeps going.

It was we who opened the door to this wind, and silenced whoever spoke;
now we ourselves are uneasy about it, and the mind is wringing its hands.

No one fears the hereafter, no one considers the nation's honour;
madness has entered the heads, and self-respect is leaving the hearts.

When the leaders themselves are of the revellers' creed, how can the sermon on religion take hold?
The hearts follow Satan, while the tongue goes by the Quran.

# 56. When I asked them for a sermon on the way of action

When I asked them for a sermon on the way of action,
they said, "Write down the following poem:

since you have been born in India in this age,
give thanks to the Creator, and take your ease.

These Western sciences are useful beyond measure;
study them too, morning and evening.

Go to Europe and see Paris and London;
study the country morning and evening.

Be satisfied with the Western way;
put the fear of the hereafter out of your mind.

The lamp of the dim old pirs has gone out;
do not needlessly make your heart the follower of superstitions.

Do not keep your heart turned away from temple and church;
give up the restriction of the pilgrim's garb.

Just forget the words 'unbelief' and 'sin';
show respect to every community and way.

In this world one gets a good name by broad-mindedness;
[illegible] win the Hindus over.

Keep your eyes on display, fame and honours;
spend your wealth and make a name.

Collect your goods, build a mansion,
and invite the officials with every sincerity.

Make your house civilised with decorations;
adorn niche and ceiling, door and roof.

Join the company of friends of like taste;
when the chance comes, take to wine and the cup.

Do not be heedless of the eyes and lips of the idols either;
perfect your taste for pistachios and almonds.

Keep your eyes fresh with the sight of misses;
take your recreation in the park morning and evening.

Use the name of religion, but do not practise it;
whoever does not agree, give him a bad name.

The maulvis who [illegible] in the old style,
make them the target of blame at once.

Break the chain of jurisprudence, saying it is against the *sharia* (sacred law);
write articles, and claim inspiration.

Taking plural marriage to be forbidden,
go round about and [illegible].

The pursuits of national progress are necessary too;
do some work under this head as well.

Without boys there can be no bustle;
[illegible] for scholarships and prizes.

Collect donations by sending out the boys;
make [illegible] of the whole territory of India.

Why spend your life in dullness?
Why wait for the turning of the days?

Do whatever you like; only this is necessary:
in every association, make a claim to Islam.

But if your honour cannot manage these things,
then take your ease in the grave along with the dead."

# 57. I see that peace and love have gone

I see that peace and love have gone
from every heart, from every group, from every family.

There is no cause for it except this:
that God has gone from among us.

# 58. Babu Sahib said in amazement

Babu Sahib said in amazement,
"Why is the Government so kind to the Sayyid?

Why has he met with such success,
that this is the only story at every gathering?

Sometimes the Lat Sahib is his guest,
sometimes he is the Lat Sahib's guest.

He is in no way my equal;
I have passed the examination in every department.

He knows nothing of English;
here all the English there is, I have by heart."

Akbar laughed and said, "Babu Sahib,
hear from me the secret hidden in this:

you bear no comparison at all with the Sayyid;
you know English, and he knows the English." [TN: The pun: angrezi-dan, "one who knows English", and angrez-dan, "one who knows the English".]

# 59. The mind thought it was rising higher

The mind thought it was rising higher;
the tress is pleased that it is mounting the gallows.

He is a dullard, she is crafty, the moment is delicate;
among the people of insight this poem is recited:

"That bane of the soul has a wondrous beauty and grace;
she has a wondrous drunken eye and a wondrous mole.

She is bent on plundering my heart, and I am bent on her;
she has a wondrous thought, and I a wondrous fancy." [TN: The last two couplets are Persian. "Mounting the gallows" (phansi par charhna) plays on the noose of the curling tress.]

# 60. A few verses from a lost piece of [illegible]

Nothing remains fixed in one colour;
the glory and splendour of Jamshed and Kai do not remain.

The star of Europe's progress is shining;
the honour of Arabia, the greatness of Rayy, do not remain.

The word "November" looks very charming;
the adornment of the faces of Bahman and Dai does not remain.

The cry of "Good-bye" goes up all over the world;
"Taslim" no longer remains, "Jai" does not remain.

The sounds of the piano charm the world;
that melody does not remain in the songs of the nightingale.

New instruments are struck up for the music of delight;
the reed-flute does not remain for the companionship of friends.

The cupbearer's glances change towards the revellers;
that warmth, that cup, that wine do not remain.

This stage is very hard, but, Akbar,
if there is courage, it does not remain uncrossed. [TN: Jamshed and Kai (Kai Khusrau) are legendary kings of Iran; Rayy is an ancient Persian city. Bahman and Dai are months of the Persian calendar. "Taslim" is the Muslim salutation and "Jai" the Hindu one.]
