# Glossary

## Glossary

*Adab*: a respectful greeting, made by raising the hand to the forehead.

*Ahadi*: an idler; originally a gentleman trooper of the Mughal army who drew pay but did little work.

*Asar-us-Sanadid*: Sayyid Ahmad Khan's book (1847) on the buildings and monuments of Delhi and its surroundings.

*Ayah*: a maidservant or nurse, especially one employed by an English family.

*Bigha*: a measure of land, in northern India a little over half an acre.

*Bismillah*: "In the name of God", said before beginning anything.

*Charhawa*: the clothes and jewellery sent by the bridegroom's family to the bride before the wedding.

*Chhatank*: a small weight, one sixteenth of a ser.

*Doha*: a Hindi couplet, often a proverb or moral saying.

*Fatiha*: the opening chapter of the Qur'an, recited for the souls of the dead and on other occasions.

*Ghadar*: "the Mutiny"; Nazir Ahmad's word for the Revolt of 1857.

*Ghari*: a measure of time of about twenty-four minutes.

*Girah*: a measure of length, one sixteenth of a yard (gaz).

*Gokhru*: a raised edging of gold or silver thread, used to trim clothes.

*Gulbadan*: a striped silk cloth, often used for women's trousers.

*Hakim*: a physician of the Greek-Arabic (Unani) school of medicine. Husn Ara's father is called Hakim Sahib.

*Imam*: one who leads the congregational prayer.

*Ka'ba*: the sacred house at Mecca, towards which Muslims pray.

*Kornish*: a deep bow of respect, made with the hand to the forehead, as at a royal court.

*Kos*: a measure of distance of about two miles.

*Manjha*: the yellow clothes worn by a bride in the days of *mayun*.

*Masha*: a small weight used for gold, medicines and the like, about a gram.

*Mayun*: the days before a wedding when the bride sits apart in plain clothes, is rubbed with turmeric paste and does no work.

*Mem*: an English lady; "Mem Sahib".

*Mirat-ul-Uroos*: "The Bride's Mirror" (1869), Nazir Ahmad's first novel, of which this book is the second part.

*Mughlani*: a woman who does sewing in the houses of the wealthy.

*Nasta'liq*: the flowing, hanging script in which Urdu and Persian are written.

*Pargana*: a subdivision of a district.

*Qibla*: the direction of the Ka'ba, towards which Muslims face in prayer; also a respectful word for a father or elder.

*Salaam*: a greeting of peace.

*Saliqa*: good sense and skill in managing a household; neatness and good order.

*Sair-e Asman*: "A Tour of the Heavens", a treatise on astronomy that the teacher says her father wrote.

*Sarai*: an inn for travellers, built round a courtyard.

*Ser*: a weight of about two pounds.

*Suhag*: the married state of a woman whose husband is living; songs of *suhag* are sung at a wedding.

*Tahajjud*: a voluntary prayer said in the last part of the night.

*Tahsildar*: the revenue officer in charge of a subdivision of a district.

*Tasbih*: a rosary of prayer beads, or the praise of God said on it.

*Tola*: a weight of about twelve grams, used for gold and silver.

*Vaid*: a physician of the Hindu (Ayurvedic) school of medicine.
