# Glossary

## Glossary

*Abdar-khana*: the household department for drinking water: filtering, cooling and serving it, under the *abdar* (water-steward).

*Abkhora*: an earthen drinking cup; in Lucknow made paper-thin (*kaghazi*).

*Achkan, sherwani*: long, close-fitting buttoned coats of the later period, which developed from the *chapkan*.

*Adab, taslim*: forms of salutation made by raising the hand to the forehead, with a bow; *kornish* is the deeper court bow.

*Aftaba, sailabchi*: ewer and basin for washing the hands; the *sailabchi* has a perforated cover so that the used water is hidden.

*Angarkha*: the long coat of Lucknow and Delhi, tied on the chest, with a fitted bodice and a full skirt; *jama*, *nima*, *balabar* and *qaba* are older or related coats.

*'Aqiqa*: the sacrifice and shaving of the head for a newborn child.

*Bahli, rath*: bullock carts: the *bahli* a light canopied cart for ordinary travel, the *rath* a domed carriage for ladies.

*Balai, malai*: clotted cream skimmed from boiled milk.

*Banaut, bank, patta*: arts of single combat: disarming an opponent, knife fighting, and fencing with the gauntlet sword; a *banka* or *bankait* is a swaggering bravo of the city.

*Barat*: the bridegroom's procession to the bride's house.

*Begam*: a lady of rank; *Begam Sahiba*, a title of respect for a noblewoman.

*Bhand*: a professional mime and comic entertainer.

*Bira, gilauri*: a folded *pan* ready for eating.

*Bismillah*: the ceremony at which a child begins to read, with the words "In the name of God".

*Bocha, hawadar*: litters carried by bearers: the *hawadar* an open throne-like litter with a folding hood, the *bocha* a heavier closed one.

*Chauthi*: the fourth-day visit after a wedding, with games and exchange of gifts between the two families.

*Chaugoshiya, dupalli, panjgoshiya*: Lucknow caps: four-cornered, two-piece, and five-cornered.

*Chhathi, chilla*: rites on the sixth and the fortieth day after a birth.

*Chikan*: white embroidery on fine white cloth, a speciality of Lucknow.

*Chikni dali*: betel nut boiled in milk and dried, eaten with or without *pan*; *do-rukhi* and *yak-rukhi* are its trimmed grades.

*Chilam, chambar, mahnal*: the bowl of the *huqqa*, the cover set over it, and the mouthpiece.

*Dastan-goi*: the art of the professional storyteller, reciting long romances such as that of Amir Hamza.

*Dhari, dom, domni*: hereditary musicians; *domni*s were women singers who performed at women's gatherings.

*Doli, pinas, miyana, sukhpal*: litters carried by *kahar*s (bearers); the *sukhpal* was the domed red litter of royal ladies.

*Doshala*: a double shawl, often given as a robe of honour (*khil'at*).

*Fatiha*: prayers recited over food offered for the souls of the dead.

*Gharqi, janghiya*: the short loincloth worn at home in Lucknow.

*Ghazal*: a lyric poem of rhyming couplets, chiefly on love.

*Huqqa, pechwan*: the water pipe; the *pechwan* has a long coiled flexible stem.

*Husn-dan, khasdan, pandan*: the boxes of the *pan* service: the *pandan* for leaves and condiments, the smaller domed *husn-dan* that replaced it, and the *khasdan* in which prepared *gilauri*s are brought into company.

*Imambara*: a hall where the Muharram mourning assemblies are held.

*Kahar*: a palanquin-bearer; *kahari*, a woman of the bearers' caste who attended ladies' litters.

*Karchob*: embroidery in gold and silver thread worked on a frame.

*Kasgar, kumhar*: potters: the *kumhar* the Hindu potter caste, the *kasgar* (bowl-maker) the Muslim potters who came from Delhi.

*Katha, chuna*: catechu and slaked lime, spread on the *pan* leaf.

*Kathak*: the classical dance of North India, perfected in Lucknow by masters of the Kathak caste.

*Khamira*: tobacco pounded with treacle and perfumes for the *huqqa*.

*Khil'at*: a robe of honour conferred by a ruler.

*Lota, lotiya*: a round metal water pot with a spout; the *lotiya* is the small one carried by attendants.

*Majlis*: a mourning assembly for Imam Husain and the martyrs of Karbala, with recitation of elegies.

*Marsiya, nauha, soz*: elegies on the martyrdom at Karbala: the long narrative *marsiya*, the short dirge (*nauha*), and the *soz* chanted to a melody.

*Masnavi*: a long narrative poem in rhymed couplets.

*Maulud Sharif*: a gathering in which the account of the Prophet's birth is recited.

*Mehndi*: henna; also the wedding rite in which henna is sent from the bride's house to the bridegroom's.

*Missi*: a black powder for the teeth and gums, used by women.

*Mujra*: a courtesan's performance of song and dance before an audience.

*Mujtahid*: a senior Shia jurist entitled to give independent rulings.

*Mushaira*: a poetry gathering.

*Naubat*: ceremonial music of drums and pipes played at set watches (*pahar*s) of the day from the *naubat-khana* (drum house) at a royal gate.

*Nikah*: the marriage contract, solemnised with the dower (*mahr*) and a sermon.

*Pan*: the betel leaf, folded with lime, catechu, betel nut and spices and eaten after meals and in company; *begami pan* is the leaf ripened by long storage underground.

*Pulao, qorma*: rice cooked in meat broth, and meat braised in a rich sauce; the national dishes of the Muslims of India in Sharar's account.

*Purdah*: the seclusion of women.

*Qiwam*: a thick perfumed paste of tobacco boiled down, eaten with *pan*.

*Rahas*: a dance-drama on the loves of Krishna, staged at Wajid Ali Shah's court.

*Rekhti*: verse written in the idiom of women.

*Rikabdar, bavarchi*: cooks: the *bavarchi* cooks in bulk, the *rikabdar* is the master cook of fine dishes.

*Sachaq*: the wedding rite in which gifts and the bride's clothes are sent from the bridegroom's house before the *mehndi*.

*Sehra*: the veil of strands of flowers or tinsel tied over the bridegroom's face.

*Shari'a*: Islamic law.

*Shar'i paijama*: trousers cut to cover the body as religious law requires, as opposed to the narrow or very wide styles of Lucknow.

*Soyam, chehlum, barsi*: observances on the third and fortieth day after a death and on its anniversary.

*Soz-khwani, soz-khwan*: the chanting of elegies to melodies; a chanter of elegies.

*Surahi, jhajhri*: long-necked earthen water jugs; the *jhajhri* has a short neck instead of a long one.

*Ta'ziya*: a replica of the tomb of Imam Husain carried in procession in Muharram.

*Taht-ul-lafz-khwani*: recitation of elegies in the speaking voice, without melody.

*Tora*: the full set of dishes sent as a portion from a feast.

*Ubtan*: a cosmetic paste of flour, oil and perfume rubbed on the body, especially before a wedding.

*Ugaldan*: a spittoon for the red juice of *pan*.

*Zakir*: a reciter of the sufferings of the family of the Prophet at a *majlis*.

*Zila', ri'ayat, phabti*: forms of verbal wit: punning on associated words, and the cutting comparison.
