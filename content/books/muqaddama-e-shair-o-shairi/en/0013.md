# Glossary

## Glossary

*Amad*: verse that comes to the poet unforced, as if of itself; opposed to *award*.

*Award*: verse produced by effort and contrivance; the opposite of *amad*.

*Bhasha*: the Hindi vernacular, especially the literary Hindi of Braj and Awadh, as distinct from Persianised Urdu.

*Barahmasa*: a popular Hindi song of the twelve months, in which a woman separated from her beloved describes each season.

*Dhrupad, thumri, tappa*: forms of North Indian classical and light classical song.

*Diwan*: a poet's collected lyric poems, usually arranged by the last letter of the rhyme.

*Ghazal*: a lyric poem of rhyming couplets on a single rhyme and refrain, chiefly on love; the dominant form of Urdu poetry.

*Ghalat al-awamm*: a usage that is wrong by the rules of grammar or lexicon but current among common people; *ghalat al-amm*, a usage of the same kind accepted by the educated, and so correct.

*Houri*: a maiden of paradise.

*Husn-e-matla'*: a second opening couplet of a *ghazal* or *qasida*, in which both lines rhyme.

*Husn-e-ta'lil*: the figure of 'fanciful cause', giving an imaginary reason for a real fact.

*Iham*: a figure using a word with two senses, a near one and a far one, where the far one is meant.

*Isti'ara*: metaphor; speaking of one thing in the terms of another.

*Kajal, surma*: black eye cosmetics: lamp-black and powdered antimony.

*Khamriyat*: poems in praise of wine.

*Kinaya*: allusion; saying something indirectly through what goes with it.

*Kos*: a measure of distance, about two miles.

*Kulliyat*: a poet's complete works.

*Marsiya*: an elegy, above all on the martyrdom of Imam Husain at Karbala; perfected in Lucknow by Anis and Dabir.

*Masnavi*: a long poem in rhyming couplets, each with its own rhyme, used for narrative, romance and teaching.

*Missi*: a black powder rubbed on the teeth and gums, part of a married woman's adornment.

*Mubalagha*: exaggeration; overstating a thing beyond its reality.

*Muhawara*: idiom: a phrase whose sense is established by usage rather than by its words; also, idiomatic usage in general.

*Mura'at al-nazir*: the figure of bringing together words that belong to the same set, such as garden, rose and nightingale.

*Musaddas*: a poem in six-line stanzas; Hali's own *Musaddas* (1879) is the best-known example.

*Mushaira*: a gathering at which poets recite their verse.

*Nazm*: verse in general; also a poem on a single subject, as opposed to the *ghazal*.

*Pan*: betel leaf folded with areca nut and lime, chewed after meals and offered to guests.

*Purdah*: the seclusion of women from the sight of men outside the family.

*Qafiya*: the rhyme word that comes before the *radif* in each couplet.

*Qasida*: an ode, usually of praise addressed to a patron, often opened by a *tashbib*.

*Qit'a*: a short poem of connected couplets without an opening rhyming couplet.

*Radif*: the refrain: the word or words repeated at the end of every couplet of a *ghazal*, after the rhyme.

*Raga*: a melodic mode of Indian music, each with its own time and mood; Bhairavi, Bihag and Jogiya are examples.

*Roz-marra*: everyday usage: the way native speakers actually phrase things.

*Ruba'i*: a quatrain in a special set of metres, rhyming in the first, second and fourth lines.

*Sama'*: Sufi gatherings for listening to music and mystical verse.

*Sanglakh zamin*: 'stony ground': a hard and unusual combination of metre, rhyme and refrain set as a challenge.

*Sher*: a couplet, the basic unit of Urdu and Persian verse; *shi'r* also means poetry in general.

*Tajnis*: punning on words alike in sound or spelling but different in sense.

*Takhayyul*: imagination, the faculty Hali names as the first condition of the poet.

*Takhallus*: a poet's pen name, usually worked into the last couplet of a *ghazal*.

*Tamsil*: illustrating a statement with an example or parable.

*Tashbib*: the opening section of a *qasida*, on love, spring or some other theme, before the praise begins.

*Tazkira*: a biographical anthology of poets, with short notices and selections.

*Tibaq*: antithesis; bringing together words of opposite meaning.

*Tarji'-band, tarkib-band*: stanzaic poems whose stanzas are closed by a couplet; in a *tarji'-band* the same couplet is repeated each time.

*Wasokht*: a poem in which the lover turns against the beloved, reproaching her and threatening to love another.

*Zu-qafiyatain, zu-bahrain*: verse with a double rhyme, and verse that scans in two metres: feats of craft that Hali counts among needless artifice.
