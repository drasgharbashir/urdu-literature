I am the trust-house of the cruel probing of eyelashes:
every drop of blood in my body is the seal-stone of the beloved's name.

To whom can I tell how darkness spreads in my bedchamber?
It would be a moonlit night if they put cotton in the chinks of the walls.

Reproach came to stop the disorder of madness's tumult:
the friends' laughter has become the stitching of collar and hem.

Before the radiance of that sun-like one's image,
the grain in the mirror flutters its wings like a mote in a sunbeam.

I do not know if I am good or bad, but company is against me:
if I am a rose I am in the furnace, if straw, in the garden.

The fervour of love's madness gave me a thousand hearts:
turning black, every drop of blood in my body became a heart's core.

Asad, I am a prisoner of the effect of the fair ones' love:
the collar on my neck has become the curve of a caressing hand.

The pleasures of the world are dust in my eyes:
nothing but the liver's blood, and there is none of that in the liver.

Unless, when I turn to dust, the wind carries me off,
there is no strength or power at all in wing and feather.

Whose coming is this, with the bearing of paradise,
that there is nothing on the road but the radiance of roses?

Granted she feels none, I might at least pity myself:
there is no effect at all in my ineffectual breath.

The drinkers are ruined by the thought of the roses' radiance:
there is nothing at all in the walls and doors of the tavern.
