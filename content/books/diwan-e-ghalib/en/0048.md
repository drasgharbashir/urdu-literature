Where we see your footprint,
we see paradise, garden after garden.

Those distraught for the mole at the corner of her mouth
see in its black spot a tour of non-existence.

Beside your cypress stature, we find
the tumult of Doomsday a man's height shorter.

Watch the show, O you absorbed in holding the mirror:
with what longing we look at you!

Trace the heat of lament from the scar of the heart,
for we are looking at the footprint of a night-walker.

Ghalib, putting on the dress of a beggar,
we watch the spectacle of the generous.

◆

She does not accept being reminded of me even with ill words:
if the stranger's case is spoiled, it would be no wonder.

There is a promise of a walk in the garden: happy is longing's fortune!
The good news of being killed is fated, though it is not mentioned.

The world is the waist of the beauty of Absolute Being:
people say "it exists", but we do not accept it. [TN: the beloved's waist is proverbially so slender as not to exist.]

Our drop too is in truth the ocean, but
we do not follow the shallowness of *Mansur* (the Sufi martyr who cried "I am the Truth").

Alas, O taste for ruin, that strength is gone:
the body worn by quarrelsome love is not the same.

When I say that I will have you on Doomsday,
with what arrogance she says, "I am no *houri* (maiden of paradise)!"
