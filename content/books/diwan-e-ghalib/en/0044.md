Weeping blood is not so easy:
where is strength in the heart, or life in the liver?

We have left the gaming-house of love:
if we went there, where is the money in our purse?

I rack my brains over the cares of the world:
where am I, and where this burden?

Ghalib, my faculties have grown feeble:
where is that balance among the elements?

What honour has that rose which is not in the garden?
The collar is a shame to the shirt if it is not in the hem.

O weeping, from weakness nothing is left in my body:
the blood that is not in my hem has turned to colour and flown.

The parts of the sun's gaze have gathered:
the atoms are not in the chinks of the walls of her house.

What can I say? The darkness of grief's prison is so black
that cotton is no less than dawn's light in its chinks.

Life's splendour comes from love that lays the house waste:
the gathering has no candle if there is no lightning in the harvest.

For having my wound stitched I am taunted with seeking a cure:
the stranger thinks there is no pleasure in the needle's wound.

We are so struck down by a single spring of coquetry
that there is nothing but the rose's radiance around our grave.

Drop by drop is the matter of a new ulcer:
even the blood in my body is not free of the taste for pain.
