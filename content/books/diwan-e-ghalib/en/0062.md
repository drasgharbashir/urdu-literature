Wash your hands of any gain, O you who walk with desire:
the heart is a debtor drowned in the surge of weeping.

Like that candle which someone snuffs,
I too am among the burnt, the scar of incompleteness.

Those fragrant green meadows, how marvellous!
Those delicate self-adorned idols, alas, alas! [TN: these three couplets belong to the qit'a on Calcutta and appear again on page 104.]

Those glances of theirs that test patience, a pity to look on;
that gesture of theirs that steals strength, alas, alas!

Those fresh sweet fruits, bravo, bravo!
Those pure, agreeable wines, alas, alas!

◆

How narrow is the world of us who are oppressed,
in which an ant's egg is the sky!

The universe has motion from your love:
from the sun's reflection there is life in the atom.

Though it is tulip-coloured from the slap of hard stone,
the heedless think my glass holds wine.

She has made her place warm in the breasts of the lustful:
why should she not like it, since it is a cold house?

How fine! You did not kiss the stranger!
Just be quiet: we too have a tongue in our mouth.

Whoever sits in the shadow of the beloved's wall
is the ruler of the realm of Hindustan.
