Friend, when you spoke of Calcutta,
you shot an arrow into my breast, alas, alas!

Those fragrant green meadows, how marvellous!
Those delicate self-adorned idols, alas, alas!

Those glances of theirs that test patience, a pity to look on;
that gesture of theirs that steals strength, alas, alas!

Those fresh sweet fruits, bravo, bravo!
Those pure, agreeable wines, alas, alas!

**[illegible: the Persian heading of a qit'a on a gift of betel-nut]**

This smooth betel-nut that lies on my lord's palm:
however well you speak of it, it suits it.

The pen puts its finger to its teeth: what can one write of it?
Speech bows its head into its collar: what can one say of it?

Write that it is the seal on letters from dear friends;
call it the amulet on the arms of wonderful self-adorned beauties.

Write that it is the fingertip of the fair, stained with *missi* (a black tooth-powder);
call it the scar on the side of the frenzied lover's liver.

Write that it resembles the ring on Solomon's hand;
call it like the nipple of a fairy-born maiden.

Liken it to the burnt-out star of Qais;
call it the musky mole on Laila's charming cheek.

Take it to be the *Black Stone* in the wall of the Kaaba;
call it the musk-pod of the deer of the desert of *Khotan*.

If in its shape you take it for the *qaf* of *tiryaq* (antidote),
call it in its colour the young green growth of the Messiah.
