If in the cloister you make it the prayer-stone,
in the tavern call it the brick that seals the wine-jar.

Why write that it is the lock on the door of love's treasure?
Why call it the centre point of desire's compass?

Why imagine it to be a rare pearl?
Why call it the pupil of the anqa's eye?

Why write that it is the button of Laila's shirt?
Why call it the hoofprint of *Salma's* (a beloved of Arabic verse) camel?

Take the palm of my lord, kind to his servants, to be a heart,
and call this smooth betel-nut its black core.

### Ruba'is

*Ruba'i* (quatrain): four lines in a fixed metre. Each is printed here as two couplets.

After the children's festival gathering was over,
the days of youth went on drinking the cup of the moment.

We have now reached the outskirts of the land of non-existence:
O life gone by, take one step forward to welcome us.

◆

Last night I grieved over tresses and a face scattering sweat:
how can I explain? It was a stranger world.

I wept with a thousand eyes till dawn:
every tear was a brimming eye.

◆

Love is like fireworks, a children's pastime:
the burning of the liver is the same.
