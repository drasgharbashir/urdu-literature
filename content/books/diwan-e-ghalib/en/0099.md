### Selected qasida in praise of Ali al-Murtaza, peace be upon him

A *qasida* (formal ode) in praise of *Ali* (the Prophet's cousin and son-in-law, the fourth caliph and, for Shia Muslims, the first Imam).

Not one atom's instrument is idle from the bounty of the garden:
the shadow of the tulip without its scar is the black heart of spring.

From the drunkenness of the morning breeze, the shard of the wine flask
displays the green grass as the temper of the mountain's sword.

The leopard's spot is green like an emerald cup:
the face of the spark is fresh like the fibre of an orange.

From the drunkenness of the cloud, longing gathers the flowers of joy,
for in this embrace the pressure of both worlds is possible.

Mountain and desert are all filled with the nightingale's longing:
the sleeping road has woken at the rose's laughter.

The bounty of the air entrusts, like an orphan's eyelashes,
the fate of both worlds to the cloud in a single line of dust.

If you cut a fingernail and throw it away like a crescent,
the power of growth will not leave even that idle.

Every handful of dust has flown to the sky like a turtle-dove;
every snare of burnt paper hunts peacocks.

If in the tavern you long to pick flowers,
forget one cup of wine on the niche of the garden.

Look for the wave of roses in the private chamber of the garden's bud
if you lose your turban in a corner of the tavern.

If the *Mani* of thought should draw a picture of the garden,
the grass would be like the young down, the line of the compass.

To sing the praise of the King, the green parrot of the mountain grass
has made itself a beak out of ruby.
