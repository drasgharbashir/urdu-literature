The tear's pride in its costliness is justified then,
when a piece of the liver comes into the blood-raining eye.

Give me leave to complain, so that, tyrant,
you too may find some taste in my torment.

If it caught a hint from that enchanting eye,
the mirror would begin to speak like a parrot.

O Lord, the thorns' tongues are dry with thirst:
may someone with blistered feet come into the thorny valley.

Why should I not die of envy when that delicate body
comes into the embrace of the curve of the zunnar's ring?

If greed for gold were not the plunderer of honour,
why would the beauty of the rose come from the garden to the market?

O lamenting heart, the tearing of the collar has its savour then,
when one breath comes tangled in every thread.

My breast is a fire-temple from a hidden secret:
alas, if it should come to be revealed!

Ghalib, think of it as the talisman of a treasury of meaning,
whatever word comes into my verses.

If my death did not comfort you, so be it;
if there is still another test to come, so be it.

There is at least the pricking of the pain of longing to see you:
if longing is not the flower-picker of the garden of comfort, so be it.

Wine-worshippers must put the wine-jar to their mouths:
if one day there is no cupbearer in the gathering, so be it.
