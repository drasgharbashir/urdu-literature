Perhaps she came into someone's dream at night:
today that delicate idol's feet ache.

Ghalib, how could my verse not have flavour?
I drink the water I wash the feet of *Khusrau* (Amir Khusrau, the sweet-tongued poet) in.

When I reach there and faint for my own sake,
a hundred times I mean to kiss the ground at her feet.

I keep my heart, and my heart keeps me, absorbed in faithfulness:
how much we love to be captive to each other!

From weakness, the ant's footprint is a collar on my neck:
where have I the strength to flee from your street?

Neglect me knowingly, so there may be some hope too:
this glance that misses its aim is poison to me.

Envy of a rival in the same style and the pain of the plaintive cry's effect:
the dawn bird's lament is a two-edged sword to me.

When I asked her to repeat her promise to cut off my head,
she laughed and said, "I swear by your head!"

What reason is there to turn the heart to blood? Yet, helpless,
I must care for the dullness of the eye.

You are so delicate that you call silence a cry;
I am so helpless that even neglect is cruelty to me.

The reason for coming to *Lucknow* is not clear:
if it is the wish to tour and see the sights, I have little of that.

This city is not the end of the chain of longing:
I mean to visit *Najaf* and go round the *Haram* (the shrine at Mecca).
