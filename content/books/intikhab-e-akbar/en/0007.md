# There Is No God but God: Poems 35–47

## 35. There was a merry party yesterday in a tavern

There was a merry party yesterday in a tavern;
someone sang this nicely in a song:

"By the grace of God we are Muslims in name;
otherwise we would not live at ease in this age."

## 36. If you want to shine on the tree of existence

"If you want to shine on the tree of existence,
do not stay unripe; ripen in some colour."

I said, "I am no believer in Sufism."
He said, "Come to this gathering, then, and dance."

I said, "I have no fear of the Collector."
He said, "If he comes along now, you will cower."

I said, "Surely there is some limit to exercise."
He said, "Its only limit is that you get tired."

I said, "I cannot get free of my worries."
He said, "Then make a dash towards the tavern."

I said, "There is no colour in Akbar."
He said, "If you hear his verses, you will leap with delight."

## 37. When I had finished Spencer

When I had finished Spencer,
everyone began to look at me.

The teacher asked, "Have you understood something?
Have these subtleties made some way into your heart?"

I said, "Its whole meaning
is plain: there is no god but God."

The master said, "You are a dunce."
Truth cried out, "Bravo, Akbar, bravo!" [TN: Herbert Spencer (1820–1903), the English evolutionary philosopher, much read in Indian colleges.]

## 38. I heard that some Muslims were gathered in one place

I heard that some Muslims were gathered in one place,
God-fearing, of good character and lofty outlook.

Someone said to them, "Just tell us this:
how is your honour and standing to be kept up?

Look at the power of the Europeans:
in their grasp are country, wealth, treasure and army.

Their coin runs from here to London;
every white and black man is under their seal.

They have made such machines that, seeing them,
'Wonderful!' bursts spontaneously from people's lips.

Have you too got anything to be proud of?"
They said, "Yes: there is no god but God."

## 39. Neither Beck is left, nor Sir Sayyid

Neither Beck is left, nor Sir Sayyid;
a sigh rises from the hearts of their friends.

There was comfort in the person of Mahmud;
today he too has taken the road to Paradise.

The Lesson said, "Come to your senses,
you who are greedy for glory, splendour and rank!

The mark of Ahmad and Mahmud is erased;
there remains: there is no god but God." [TN: Theodore Beck, principal of the Aligarh college, died in 1899; Sir Sayyid Ahmad Khan in 1898; his son Justice Sayyid Mahmud in 1903.]

## 40. To the editor of the magazine Yad-e Baiza

You have the knowledge of the heart's secrets and the solving of riddles;
you have verse above the verse of the Deccan, the verse of the Pleiades.

What need have you of the beauty of our speech?
You have the beauty of Joseph, the breath of Jesus, the White Hand;

what all the fair ones have, you alone have. [TN: The piece is in Persian. Yad-e Baiza, "the White Hand", is the miracle of Moses and the name of the magazine; the last two lines are a famous Persian verse.]

## 41. English education among the Muslims can no longer be stopped

English education among the Muslims can no longer be stopped;
the alliance of East and West cannot be stopped by anyone.

That cold cannot be stopped, this [illegible] cannot be stopped;
but this wish of the elders also cannot be stopped:

that the taste of the nation should not become a stranger to "God is great",
that this life-giving image should not be erased from the register of the heart.

## 42. In a hotel, with Europeans

In a hotel, with Europeans,
the Sayyid one day tasted curry.[1]

The *khansama* (butler) said in his ear,
"You are not without learning;

say some grace before eating,
so that you stay loyal to your faith too."

Then these verses of the honoured Sa'di
came onto his tongue:

"O Generous One, who from the treasury of the Unseen
feed the fire-worshipper and the Christian,

how could You leave Your friends deprived,
You who look kindly on enemies?"

## 43. A religious poem, at the request of the editor of the Deccan Review

The hearts that keep guard over the Truth
have God's hidden grace upon them;
those who understand the meanings of the Quran,
I have heard this from their own tongues:

"That cloud of mercy still scatters pearls;
the wine-jar and the tavern still bear their seal and sign."

Islam is the joy of the heart and the amulet of the soul;
Islam is the helper of kingship and of kings;
Islam is in the world with its full equipment;
Islam is still the guardian of faith.

"That cloud of mercy still scatters pearls;
the wine-jar and the tavern still bear their seal and sign."

In the mosques there is the same sound of the call to prayer,
the same "God is great" on every tongue,
the same fervour in the hearts of the Muslims,
the same honour, the same pride and bearing.

"That cloud of mercy still scatters pearls;
the wine-jar and the tavern still bear their seal and sign."

In hearts there is still the remembrance of God;
the mind still rejoices in the *dhikr* (remembrance of God);
there are still many masters of spiritual guidance;
there are still many in whom the garden of faith flourishes.

"That cloud of mercy still scatters pearls;
the wine-jar and the tavern still bear their seal and sign."

The reflection of Muhammad's face is manifest;
the scent of Muhammad is in the nostrils of the soul;
caravans are moving towards Muhammad;
the street of Muhammad has the same splendour.

"That cloud of mercy still scatters pearls;
the wine-jar and the tavern still bear their seal and sign."

Why is there this rawness in your hearts?
There is no disorder in Nature;
the remembrance of God still supports the heart;
hear this song of the master Jami:

"That cloud of mercy still scatters pearls;
the wine-jar and the tavern still bear their seal and sign."

The British Government has these kindnesses,
that it is not opposed to religion;
then why do you not recognise it,
when the eye of the gnostic tells you:

"That cloud of mercy still scatters pearls;
the wine-jar and the tavern still bear their seal and sign."

The world has accepted the Messenger of God;
that story is still on people's tongues;
the age is not empty of Islam;
hear this national song of Akbar:

"That cloud of mercy still scatters pearls;
the wine-jar and the tavern still bear their seal and sign." [TN: The refrain is Persian; the poem itself names it as a line of Jami (15th century).]

## 44. May God heal the Aligarh school of all its diseases

May God heal the Aligarh school of all its diseases;
it is full of the sons of chiefs, of nobles, of gentlemen.

Refined and well-mannered, smart and quick, clean and neat, happy and cheerful;
there is intelligence in their natures, and good intentions in their hearts.

With perfect diligence they are studying, with perfect self-respect they are advancing;
riders on the road of the East, and on the Western road on foot.

Each one of them, no doubt, is just as you know him to be:
he shows his graceful figure at a gathering, and bows his head if you come in.

If a beggar asks, they tell him plainly, "You are strong; go and earn your bread";
if you accept an invitation, he would feed you his whole capital.

They have no attachment to idols, they pay no heed to the footsteps of misses;
all their strength is spent on reading; simple of sight, simple of heart.

If they ever see twisting tresses, they take them for some policy;
if a lightning-like one smiles, they take it for electric light.

They go out in groups in the name of civilisation and sympathy,
and collect donations from everyone, saying, "Give to us, and God will give to you."

They firmly believe that this alone is the root of the work of the faith;
this will bring the nation's glory, this will make their fathers and grandfathers shine.

All who live in the college building have, as yet, no experience;
they do not know what kind of stage lies ahead, or what roads.

There is the light of faith in their hearts, but its guardian is not strong;
the air of logic, the ways of childhood: let these not put out this candle,

deceive them to gain their own ends, teach them contempt for religion and faith,
and in the end destroy the ways of the community, though it may increase their own display.

This alone is Akbar's plea, this is his prayer at the court of God:
let the professors give them the lessons of learning and science, and God give them understanding.

## 45. Translation of the saying of a European statesman, 189[illegible]

Religion without power is but a Philosophy

Why is this shaikh so angry with Akbar?
[illegible]

There is nothing in it to quarrel about;
this is the saying of a clear-minded sage:

when religion has not the force of government,
what is it? Only a philosophy. [TN: The English sentence is printed in English in the original.]

## 46. Nations rise through trade

Nations rise through trade;
this alone is their ascension.

Trade is indeed a kingdom;
Europe owes its power today to it.

The word "tajir" is itself the proof, Akbar:
look, on the trader's head there is a crown. [TN: A pun: tajir, "trader", contains taj, "crown".]

## 47. Your origin is the clear word of God

Your origin is the clear word of God;
this zeal to imitate the conquering nation is futile.
Hear this saying, which is a collection of counsels:
the cause of honour is the deed that is righteous.
If there is no sympathy with one's religion and community,
the age will say plainly that this is cowardice.

For them the intoxication of wine is fitting;
for them abundance of joy in the heart is fitting;
for them pride upon the face of the earth is fitting;
for me only the hope of a houri is fitting.
In this very hope I would count all progress,
if only you understood the virtues of the meaning of "houri".

## Notes

[1] Akbar's note: "The meaning is: why should we not eat such good things as pudding and curry in the hotel?"
