# The Sayyid, the Preacher and the Oudh Punch: Poems 12–15

## 12. To the Sayyid today the honoured preacher said (1877)

To the Sayyid today the honoured preacher said,
"Your ruined state is talked of everywhere.

You have taken Nature and policy for God;
not the least effect of 'There is no god but God' is left in your heart.

You have abandoned fasting and prayer, alms and pilgrimage;
you have no fear of the Refuge of Prophethood.

Satan has shown you the beauty of the world's bride
and made you a slave to the love of rank."

He answered, "Be it religion or be it custom,
whatever disturbs comfort is a thorn in the road.

It is a pity you are unaware of the world;
what would you know of the colour of evening and morning?

If you should happen to travel to Europe,
and see for yourself the state of subjects and king,

that brilliance and splendour of the royal palace,
the dignity of the departments, the display of the army;

if you saw the light of the new sciences,
before which the light on the faces of sun and moon is put to shame;

if you were invited to some rich man's house,
and the talk with the young misses turned to love and longing,

budding, beguiling, flower-limbed, delicate,
whose cheeks a glance itself would burden;

and if one lovely idol bumped into you and laughingly said,
'Well, Maulvi! This is not a sin!'

then, reverend sir, I would bow low and salute you
if your honour even mentioned the *khanqah* (Sufi hospice) after that!

You would be bound by the craze for trousers and coat, bungalow and biscuit;
your honour too would be mad for a hat for a cap.

Sitting in a corner on the pulpit, sir, it is all very well;
everyone knows the sermon about reward and sin."

## 13. In the heat of debate, Anwar said this to Akbar

In the heat of debate, Anwar said this to Akbar:
"You have not kept to the path of Ahmad the Messenger.

Only superstition is left in you;
the intoxication of the wine of ignorance has come over you.

There is no loftiness in your aims, no soundness in your thoughts;
you are plainly drowned in the sea of sin and bigotry.

The shaikhs and mullas are badly short-sighted;
the nation is being ruined openly."

Akbar said, "The charge is no doubt correct,
but on this road you are slower even than I.

You care only for [illegible] adornment and display;
in your heart is denial, and on your lips the name of Islam.

In your caravan there is no dust of obedience to God;
there is no cold sigh, no heart full of pain.

If we in our narrowness go towards rawness,
your inclination is towards heresy and servility.

You too are deprived of that colour, and we are deprived too;
the saying of the late martyr fits exactly:

'O breeze, neither you nor I have the capital for this madness;
neither you nor I have the scent of those cross-shaped tresses.'" [TN: The closing couplet is Persian. "The late martyr" (shahid-e marhum) may also be read as "the late Shahid", the pen name of an earlier poet.]

## 14. A letter to the Oudh Punch, dated [illegible]

O pearl of the ocean of wit,
O jewel of the mine of grace,

capital of the mind's delight,
comfort of the heart and joy of the mind,

preface to the register of eloquence,
title-page of the book of rhetoric,

creator of meanings that bring delight,
revealer of mysteries that stir pleasure,

guide of the man of letters, teacher of knowledge,
scattering pearls and gathering pearls,

adorner of the beloved called Speech,
mirror of laughter and of smiles,

fountainhead of word and counsel and talk,
treasury of counsel and advice and secrets,

O storehouse of the two-tongued Urdu,
O raiser of the banner of Urdu,

in colour the envy of the rose garden,
in sparkle the rival of the flashing lightning:

what a fine thing is the Oudh Punch!
Everyone loves the Oudh Punch.

Day and night it is the talk of the town;
its pages are read straight into the heart.

God's creation are its victims;
the envious man's envy is its proof.

If any temper is reasonable, it is this;
if anything is lawful by the sacred law, it is this.

Though there is a good deal of poison,
though the taunting phrase is a lancet,

yet that is dissolved in sugar,
this is tempered in the water of life.

That is a sherbet that preserves reason and faith;
this is the life-vein for dead hearts.

The spoiled were mended through laughter;
if there is wisdom, it is in such play.

Whoever saw it said, "It is good;
by God, it gladdens the heart."

Welcome counsel in the language of the revellers:
glory be to God! Bravo, bravo, bravo!

Though the style of the London Punch
is no doubt pleasing and full of art,

yet that is the first draft;
it bears no comparison with this.

God be praised, this second draft
is better in form and in meaning.

That is a very old man, full of years;
this, thank God, is a young sapling of fortune.

That is a flower that has seen a hundred springs;
this is a fresh bud just opened.

Happy offspring of a Mary-like mind,
with the breath of Jesus, a pearl of the ocean of talent;

the evening of Oudh is adorned by it,
the name of Oudh is made bright by it.

It is a light from the sun of Lucknow,
a star of the sky of Lucknow.

That is cold, like the fire of the rose;
this is warm, like the nightingale's sigh.

If that is a tangle in the discussion of a theme,
this is the master in untying fine points.

There the arm of work is of weak foundation;
here the pen is a lance born of Rustam. [TN: reading uncertain]

And what a pen: the tongue of meaning!
Why speak of tongue? It is the soul of meaning.

In rising, the glance of a magic eye;
in moving, the rival of the sword of the eyebrow;

key to the treasury of imagination,
engraver of the seal-stone of imagination.

When is it proper to call it a candle?
In its qualities it is more than a candle.

That shows the face of the gathering of forms;
this lifts the veil from reality.

Though there is collyrium in its throat,
yet it is eager in conversation.

Graceful and refined and saucy and fearless,
eager and a match for anyone, quick and clever;

the dresser of the beloved called Meaning,
the founder of the house of fine speech;

it puts words into intricacies,
it combs the tresses of speech.

If that one prides itself on freedom,
here there is pride greater than that:

that is to say, that one has a free rein,
while this, though bound, runs in every direction.

There the mind has the strength of "Fear not";
the difficulty, what there is of it, is on this side:

the fetter of reason on the feet,
an irregular account of the heart's pain,

the threads of the gaze of the envious ill-wisher
spread like a net at every step,

the short-sighted and the base-natured
eager in mischief and hostility.

There the song is on the branch of the tree;
here the nest is in the eye of the snare.

How should it not lay claim to a miracle?
It has opened its wings for flight inside the cage.

It toured both worlds in a single breath,
and then, look, it is in the same cage.

A sea surges in a drop,
the spring of a hundred gardens is in a bud.

It practises flight on the point of the spear,
it dances on the edge of the sword with a hundred airs,

a salamander in the press of flames,
a fish swimming in the waves.

What danger is there from the multitude of thorns?
Here the journey is on the shoulder of the breeze.

When is there regret here for confinement?
Joseph is Joseph even in prison.

The same splendour, the same radiance,
the same majesty, the same comfort.

If the Joseph of speech is bound,
the scent of his shirt has spread abroad.

In every colour is the spring of meaning,
every word is a curtain veiling meaning;

every point is a subtlety of insight,
every letter a revealer of reality.

It is free from the violence of the hot wind;
this branch is green even in autumn.

That one is put to shame by the sun in the sky;
here is the light of mind and heart.

That one's lot is begging;
here, like the Pole Star, steadfastness day and night.

Those from whom there was fear of possession by spirits,
those demons have beaten their heads well;

though they were Ghalib, "prevailing", in name,
its spell was never broken.

If they had not frowned with envy,
the envious would surely have set their seal of approval.

Hear one more fine point:
freedom of speech is a fault.

I offer a poet's proof;
look at the workshop of Nature.

The tongue is set inside the mouth,
lying within a fortress of teeth.

Thirty-two hard-natured young men
stand ready to do harm;

like White Demons they are fearless,
cruel, tyrannical, greedy, merciless.

If the tongue of speech goes beyond the limit,
these bloodthirsty ones run to cut it.

Whatever sits beside them as a companion
is pained by the point of a toothpick.

However soft and moist it may be,
the grain is ground when it comes among them.

Where can they bring iron gram from,
to give them a taste of hardness?

When the tongue is in such a prison,
where is freedom of speech?

Though this point is subtle, O heart,
the wise must understand from it

that it was the will of the God of body and soul
that the sauciness of the tongue be limited.

Do not blurt out whatever comes into your heart;
walk carefully, do not stray.

The river of thought is in flood;
it is given over to God and to Ahriman.

It is a public highway for truth and falsehood;
the thought of the wise is its watchman.

When an evil thought passes by without [illegible: reading uncertain],
[illegible] reject it, enough, by reason.

Do not follow falsehood, listen to truth;
clear away the thorns and pick the flowers.

Silence, enough, O tongue of the pen;
the thought intends to end the letter.

Though this is the world of speech,
where the bounty of eternity casts its light;

in every corner is the breadth of the sky,
in every atom the shine of the sun;

at every step are thousands of gardens,
in each one thousands of flowers of speech;

in every rose-petal of speech a hundred colours,
in every colour a hundred thousand wonders;

such wonders that reason is amazed,
such amazement that it is the light of gnosis;

on every side a thousand taverns,
in every one a hundred thousand wine-jars filled,

in every jar a purple wine,
that is to say, the colourfulness of meanings;

if one drop makes the mind distinguished,
the breast becomes the keeper of the secret,

a secret by which the heart is lost in rapture
and inclines towards the worship of speech.

If the chain of speech grows long,
[illegible]

but what is the use of length of statement?
What is the use of this spending of the tongue?

Enough, enough, now hold back the tongue;
a hint is enough for one who knows the point.

Make ready with heart and soul,
be lost in prayer with tongue and heart:

as long as there is the quatrain of the elements,
the colourful image on the tablet of the mind;

as long as this verse-house of existence
is metrically sound for self-worship;

as long as there is the hexagon of the six directions,
proof of the easts and the wests;

as long as there is the subtlety of the soul,
every breath the daily office of the breaths;

may this charming and beautiful paper
be the companion of the impatient soul.

With its movement may it turn copper to gold;
when it stays, may it make the heart its home.

In every place may it prove unanswerable,
in every colour may it prove the pick.

May it be the fire of the heart of friend and stranger,
may it become the lamp of Kaaba and temple.

As long as "Be, and it is" has effect,
may everyone be enchanted by this spell.

May the moth take it for its lamp;
may the nightingale see it and take it for a garden.

Wherever the sun's light is,
may the pull of the atoms be that way.

O preserver and creator of the Oudh Punch,
may the lovers of the Oudh Punch be glad at heart;

may they all have their wishes,
whenever they look, may they find their hearts happy.

May every high-natured buyer
find elevation in its two leaves; [TN: reading uncertain]

may he not need silver or gold,
may he be the recipient of high regard.

The friends who are its helpers,
of lofty mark and of good inner nature,

wits and authors of jokes,
printers and illustrators of drolleries,

may they flourish in the garden of the world,
may they wander happy in garden and orchard.

With their colourful natures may they make flowers bloom,
may they make the evil eye weep blood.

May they bring forth such pearls of theme
that a cry of praise rises from the lips of the sea,

and the poets spontaneously exclaim,
"God! What a mind and thought has Akbar!"

(Oudh Punch! Glory be to God, glory be to God!) [TN: The Oudh Punch (Avadh Panch) was the comic weekly founded in Lucknow in 1877 by Munshi Sajjad Husain; Akbar was one of its best-known contributors. This mock-ode plays on its debt to the London Punch.]

## 15. I told her, "I will give up religion and be happy"

I told her, "I will give up religion and be happy;
I long for a few posts and a few rewards.

There is no benefit to anyone in this war and strife;
in this gathering there is one prayer and a few curses."

She said, "Be quiet, for faith is the axis of the community;
do not leave this road for the sake of a few selfish ends.

You have told all the faults of religion; tell its merits too;
do not deny wisdom to please a few common people."
