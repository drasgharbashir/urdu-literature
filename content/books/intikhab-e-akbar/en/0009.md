# Glossary

## Glossary

*Abjad*: the system of giving each Arabic letter a number, used to make chronograms (poem 30).

*Ahriman*: the evil principle in Zoroastrian belief, opposed to Yazdan, God.

*Aligarh*: the town where Sir Sayyid founded the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College in 1875, later Aligarh Muslim University.

*Ash*: a thick pottage or broth.

*Banjara*: an itinerant grain-carrier who travels with a train of pack-oxen.

*Buraq*: the heavenly steed on which the Prophet made his night journey.

*Chilman*: a screen of thin bamboo slats hung in a doorway, behind which women could see without being seen.

*Dhikr*: the remembrance of God by repeating His names, a Sufi devotion.

*Dohar*: a light cotton wrap or quilt.

*Durbar*: a royal court or a state assembly; the imperial durbars at Delhi in 1877, 1903 and 1911 proclaimed the British monarch Emperor of India.

*Fatiha*: the opening chapter of the Quran, recited for the souls of the dead.

*Ghats*: steps leading down to a river, used for bathing.

*Ghazal*: a lyric poem of couplets on one rhyme and refrain.

*Ghazi*: a holy warrior; one who has fought for the faith.

*Hafiz*: Shams-ud-Din Hafiz of Shiraz (d. 1390), the great Persian lyric poet, whose lines Akbar often parodies.

*Halwa*: a sweetmeat.

*Iblis*: Satan.

*Jihad*: holy war; striving in the cause of God.

*Kausar*: the river or pool of Paradise.

*Khanqah*: a Sufi hospice where a master lives with his disciples.

*Khansama*: a butler or head cook.

*Lat*: from the English "Lord": the Viceroy or a Governor; "Lat Sahib".

*Majnun and Laila*: the proverbial lovers of Arabic and Persian legend; Majnun, "the madman", went mad for love of Laila.

*Maulvi*: a Muslim religious scholar; often used of a traditional, old-fashioned teacher.

*Miss*: Akbar's word for an English or Westernised young woman.

*Mulla*: a religious teacher or preacher; often used mockingly.

*Musaddas*: a poem in six-line stanzas (poems 47 and 53).

*New Light*: the common phrase for Western education and modernist ideas.

*Oudh Punch*: the comic weekly of Lucknow (1877–1912), founded by Munshi Sajjad Husain, to which Akbar contributed.

*Peshwaz*: a long, full gown worn by women, especially dancers.

*Pir*: a Sufi master or spiritual guide.

*Purdah*: the seclusion of women from the sight of men outside the family.

*Qita*: a short poem, or a set of couplets on one subject without an opening rhymed couplet.

*Sa'di*: Sa'di of Shiraz (d. 1291/92), Persian poet and moralist, author of the Gulistan and the Bustan.

*Sarangi*: a bowed stringed instrument.

*Sayyid*: a descendant of the Prophet; in these poems, "the Sayyid" is Sir Sayyid Ahmad Khan (1817–1898), founder of the Aligarh college.

*Shaikh*: an elder, a religious man; in poetry often the sanctimonious preacher.

*Sharia*: the sacred law of Islam.

*Takhallus*: a poet's pen name, used in the last couplet of a ghazal; Akbar's is Akbar.

*Vilayat*: "abroad", in Indian usage England; also "sainthood".

*Yakhni*: meat broth.
