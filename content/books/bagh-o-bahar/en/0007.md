# The Beginning of the Tale (3)

Khiradmand had been vizier to the emperor's father and had loved him ever since he was a prince; besides, he was wise and well-meaning. He said, "It is never right to despair of God's mercy. For Him who created the eighteen thousand worlds with a single command, what great matter is it to give you children? Refuge of the world, put this vain notion out of your heart, or the whole world will be thrown into confusion. With what labour and hardship your forefathers and you yourself won this empire! In a moment it will slip from your hands, and through neglect the country will be laid waste. God forbid, but you will earn a bad name. And on top of that you will surely be called to account on the Day of Judgement: 'I made you king and put My servants in your charge; you despaired of My mercy and left your subjects distressed and bewildered.' What answer will you give to that question? Then even your worship will not help you that day, because a man's heart is God's house, and kings will be questioned only about justice. Forgive your slave's boldness, but to leave home and wander from forest to forest is the work of *jogis* (Hindu ascetics) and *faqirs* (Muslim mendicants), not of kings. You do the work that is yours to do. God's remembrance and worship do not depend on forests and mountains. You must have heard this verse:

God is beside him, and he searches the forest for Him;
he cries through the city for the boy who is on his hip.

"If you will be fair and accept your servant's petition, it would be best for your majesty to fix his mind on God every moment and every hour and pray to Him; no one has ever been turned away from His court. By day, attend to the government of the country and do justice to the poor, so that God's servants may live happily in peace and safety under the shadow of your fortune; and by night, worship, offer blessings to the pure spirit of the Prophet, and seek help from dervishes who live in seclusion and trust in God. Give daily allowances to orphans, prisoners, men burdened with families, the needy and poor widows. Through the blessing of such good works and good intentions, God willing, there is strong hope that all the wishes and desires of your heart will be fulfilled, that the longing which troubles your exalted mind will be granted, and that your noble heart will be gladdened. Keep your eyes on the kindness of the Sustainer, for in one moment He does whatever He wills." At last, through the wise vizier's speaking and pleading in this way, Azad Bakht's heart took comfort. He said, "Very well, let us try what you say too; after that, whatever God wills will be."

When the emperor's heart was comforted, he asked the vizier, "And what are all the other nobles and grandees doing, and how are they?" He replied, "All the pillars of the state pray for the life and wealth of the Refuge of the World. They are all troubled and anxious with worry for you. If you will show them your blessed face, they will all be at ease; indeed they are waiting in the Hall of Public Audience at this moment." Hearing this, the emperor ordered: "God willing, I will hold court tomorrow. Tell them all to be present." Khiradmand was overjoyed at this promise, raised both hands and blessed him: "As long as this earth and sky stand, may your crown and throne endure." Then he took his leave, came out full of joy and gave the good news to the nobles. All the nobles went home laughing and happy. The whole city was filled with delight, and the subjects rejoiced that the emperor would hold public court the next day. In the morning all the servants of the household, high and low, and all the officers of state, great and small, came and stood each in his own place and rank, waiting for the emperor to appear.
