# The Beginning of the Tale (5)

As soon as Azad Bakht saw them he felt sure: "Your wish will certainly be granted through the blessing of these men of God, and the dry tree of your hope will turn green and bear fruit through their favour. Go and wait on them, tell them your story and join their company. Perhaps they will take pity on you and pray for you, and their prayer will be accepted by Him who needs nothing." With this intention he was about to step forward, when reason warned him at once: "Fool, don't be hasty, take a good look first. What do you know of who these are, where they come from and where they are going? Who knows whether they are demons, or *ghouls* of the wilderness who have taken human shape and sat down together? In any case it is not good to rush in and disturb them. For now, hide in a corner and find out the truth about these dervishes." In the end the emperor did just that: he went and sat quietly in a corner of the place, so that no one heard him come, and fixed his attention on them to see what they would say to one another. By chance one *faqir* sneezed and gave thanks to God. At the sound the other three *qalandars* (wandering dervishes) started up. They trimmed the lamp, which was burning well enough, and sitting on their own bedding they filled their *huqqas* (water pipes) and began to smoke. One of these free men said, "Friends who share my grief, companions who have wandered the world! We four have roamed for a long time from door to door, dust on our heads, through the turning of the heavens and the changes of night and day. Praise be to God that with the help of our stars and the favour of fortune we have met together today in this place. And we know nothing of what tomorrow may bring, whether we stay together or go our separate ways. The night is as long as a mountain, and it is not good to lie down to sleep so early. Better that each of us should tell the story of what has befallen him in this world (on condition that there is not a cowrie's worth of falsehood in it); then the night will pass in talk. When only a little of the night is left, we will lie down." They all said, "Guide, we accept whatever you command. First begin with your own story and what you have seen, and we will profit from it."
