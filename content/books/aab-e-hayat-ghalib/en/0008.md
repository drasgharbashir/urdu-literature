# Glossary

## Glossary

*Aab-e-Hayat*: "the water of life", the spring of immortality that Khizr found; the title of Azad's history of Urdu poetry (1880).

*AH*: *Anno Hegirae*, a year of the Islamic lunar calendar, counted from the Prophet's migration to Medina in 622.

*Baba log*: Anglo-Indian for "the children" of an English household; Ghalib uses it as a joke about his own family.

*Bu Turab*: "father of dust", a title of Ali, the Prophet's cousin and son-in-law.

*Chronogram* (*tarikh*): a phrase or line whose letters, counted by their numerical values, give the year of an event.

*Diwan*: a poet's collected poems, usually *ghazals* arranged by the last letter of their rhymes.

*Durbar*: the court or formal assembly of a king or governor.

*Ghazal*: a lyric poem in couplets on one metre and rhyme, usually of love, human or divine; the poet's pen name appears in the last couplet.

*Hajj*: the pilgrimage to Mecca.

*Jagir*: a grant of the revenue of land, given for service or as a pension.

*Jigha* and *sarpech*: jewelled ornaments worn on the turban, given by a ruler as marks of honour.

*Kausar*: a river or pool in Paradise; its "Cupbearer" is Ali.

*Khalifa*: a successor or chief pupil, who carries on a master's line.

*Khizr*: the immortal prophet-guide of Muslim legend, who drank the water of life and shows travellers the way.

*Lata'if* (singular *latifa*): witty anecdotes or sayings.

*Lisan-ul-Ghaib*: "the Tongue of the Unseen", a title of the Persian poet Hafiz; Ghalib gives it to himself in jest.

*Malik-ush-Shu'ara*: "King of Poets", the title of the poet laureate at a royal court; held by Zauq at the court of Bahadur Shah Zafar.

*Mansur*: Mansur al-Hallaj (died 922), the Sufi executed for crying *Ana'l-Haqq* ("I am the Truth").

*Masnavi*: a poem in rhyming couplets, often long and narrative.

*Mirza*: a title of men of Mughal or Persian noble descent; in this book "the Mirza" is usually Ghalib.

*Missi*: a black powder that men and women of fashion rubbed on their teeth and gums.

*Mushaira*: a gathering at which poets recite their verse, often on a set pattern of metre and rhyme.

*Mutiny* (*ghadar*): the Revolt of 1857, which ended the Mughal court at Delhi.

*Nawab*: a title of a ruler or nobleman.

*Nazr*: a ceremonial gift, usually of coins, presented to a superior.

*Pargana*: a district made up of several villages, a unit of revenue.

*Qasida*: an ode, usually of praise, in a single metre and rhyme.

*Qazi*: a judge of Islamic law.

*Qita*: a "fragment", a short poem of two or more couplets on one subject.

*Rekhta*: an older name for Urdu, and for poetry in Urdu.

*Rubai*: a quatrain, a poem of four lines.

*Sehra*: a veil of flowers or pearls tied over the bridegroom's face, and a poem written to celebrate it.

*Sher*: a couplet, the unit of Urdu and Persian verse.

*Takhallus*: a poet's pen name, such as Ghalib, Asad, Zauq or Azad.

*Tarawih*: extra night prayers said in congregation during Ramazan.

*Tazkira*: a memoir or biographical dictionary of poets, with specimens of their verse.

*Urdu-e Mualla*: "Exalted Urdu", the collection of Ghalib's Urdu letters (1869).
