# The Prince Restored, and the Preparations

## Madan Ban Comes Back to the King and Queen and Gives Them the Longed-For News

Leaving Rani Ketki, Madan Ban arrives in a trice at the mountain where Raja Jagat Parkas and Rani Kam Lata were sitting, greets them with the yogis' greeting, *ades*, stands before them and says: "Here you are: your house is built anew, and good days have come. Not a hair of Rani Ketki's head has been harmed. I have brought this letter in her own hand. Read it, and then do whatever you wish."

The King pulled a hair from that same tiger skin and put it on the fire. In the space of a word Gosain Mahendar Gir arrived, and saw with his own eyes this new show of yogi and yogini that had come about. He clasped them all to his breast and said: "I left the tiger skin with you for this very reason: if anything befell you, burn one of its hairs. Your house has come to this state; what were you doing all this time, and what sleep were you sleeping? But what can you do? That Player shows whatever forms He pleases and makes us dance whatever dances He likes. What business was it to give the girl the ash? It was I who turned Ude Bhan, and Suraj Bhan his father, and Lachhmi Bas, into deer and doe. To make those three just as they were before is nothing much for me. Well, what is done is done. Now come, get up, take your seat in your kingdom again, and make ready for the wedding. Now gather up your daughter. I have made Kunwar Ude Bhan my own son, and I will bring him myself to marry her."

As soon as he heard this, the King came and sat on the throne of his kingdom, and gave the order that very hour: "Cover all the roofs and terraces with gold lace, tie gold and silver tinsel on all the trees and hills, and string ropes of pearls in the trees. And proclaim this: for forty days and forty nights, any house in which there is not dancing all the eight watches, I will be displeased with its master, and know that he is no companion of mine in sorrow and joy." That kingdom stretched so far that if a traveller walked day and night without stopping anywhere, it would take him six months to go round it. That was the scale of it.

## The King, the Queen and Gosain Mahendar Gir Go to Fetch Rani Ketki

Then the Guru and the King and Queen, with Madan Ban, arrived where Rani Ketki sat silent and stunned. The Guru took Rani Ketki in his lap, made the betrothal offering on behalf of Kunwar Ude Bhan, and said: "You go home with your mother and father. Now I will go and bring my son Kunwar Ude Bhan."

So the Guru, the Gosain, to whom be our prostrations, sets off in this way. What happens next will be told in its turn. Now picture the pomp and splendour here: Maharaj Jagat Parkas gave word through all his country, and had it proclaimed: "Whoever does not do this will fare badly. In every village build triple gateways facing one another and hang red cloth on them, and set on them rainbow-coloured lace, and silver and gold braid, and gold fringes, and stitch on spangles. And on every old, old banyan and pipal tree, wherever they may be, tie such fresh green garlands of lace flowers that their glitter and sparkle reach from crown to root."

The plants put on red dresses of every colour, and the branches put anklets on their feet. Every shrub put on ornaments of flowers and fruit, a little at a time if it had not many. All the fresh leaves swaying in the green put henna on their hands, adorned as finely as their adorning could allow. And as far as the eye could reach, the newly married brides wore dresses of tiny pods, and the wives dresses of petals of new buds, and all of them filled their laps with flowers and fruit of wedded love. And the three years' revenue that people used to pay throughout that king's realm, in whatever way they earned it, by farming, by ploughing, by selling cloth and goods: all of it was remitted to them: "Let them make merry in their own homes." And into every well throughout the kingdom, cartloads of raw sugar from the sugar-works were taken and poured. And in all the forests and the foothills a glory of lanterns began to be seen glittering by night. And in all the lakes safflower and *tesu* (flame-of-the-forest) and night jasmine floated, and some saffron too was dissolved in them. And on all the bushes and thickets, from the tip to the root, wherever there were leaves and little clusters of leaves, silver and gold spangles were stuck on with gum. And it was proclaimed: "Let no one go about in any manner or any shape except in a red turban and a red robe." And all the singers, players, mimics, *bhagatiyas*, bards, *ras* performers and dancers were told: in every village where they might be, let them come out of their own places, spread fine carpets, and keep on singing and playing, making merry, dancing and leaping.

## Gosain Mahendar Gir Searches for Kunwar Ude Bhan and His Mother and Father, Fails to Find Them, Is in Great Torment, and Raja Indra Reads His Letter and Comes

Let whatever happened here, and all the merriment, stay here. Now listen to what follows. The yogi Mahendar Gir and his nine hundred thousand ascetics searched every forest, but nowhere could they find Kunwar Ude Bhan and his mother and father. Then he wrote a letter to Raja Indra. In the letter it was written: "I turned these three into a deer and does. Now I go about looking for them and they are nowhere to be found. I have done everything that is in my power. And now I have said with my own mouth that Kunwar Ude Bhan is my son and I am his father, and at the bride's house all the wedding preparations are going on. Now I am in sore trouble. Do whatever you can."

Raja Indra, to see Guru Mahendar Gir, arrives in person with all his court and throne, and says: "As your son, so my son. Together with you I will gather all of Indra's heaven and go in procession to marry Kunwar Ude Bhan." Gosain Mahendar Gir said to Raja Indra: "You and I are of one mind; but think of some way by which we can lay hands on Ude Bhan. Let us take all the singers, men and women, who are here, and you and I will go round all the forests; somewhere or other we shall find them."

## The Game of the Deer Is Spoiled, and Kunwar Ude Bhan Takes His Own Shape Again

One night Raja Indra and Gosain Mahendar Gir were sitting in the clear moonlight, listening to music. All around them, millions of deer, absorbed in the music, had forgotten their leaping and stood with their heads bowed. At this Raja Indra said: "Let every devotee of my Guru's company recite the spell of the divine word over all the deer, and sprinkle a drop of water on each one." Who knows what that water was? With the sprinkle of water, Kunwar Ude Bhan and his mother and father, all three, left the shape of deer and became just as they had been. Mahendar Gir and Raja Indra embrace all three and seat them beside them with great honour. And they give that same pitcher of water to their people and send it to the place where the hail had fallen on them the moment they shaved their heads [TN: proverb for disaster striking at the very start of an undertaking; here, the battlefield where Suraj Bhan's army was destroyed]. When Raja Indra's people sprinkle the water there, reciting the same divine spell, all who had been killed rise up, and all who had fled half-dead come together again. Raja Indra and Mahendar Gir take Kunwar Ude Bhan, Raja Suraj Bhan and Rani Lachhmi Bas, seat themselves in a flying couch, set them back in their own kingdom with great pomp, and prepare for the wedding. Diamonds and pearls are scattered over them all by the five-seer measure. Raja Suraj Bhan and Ude Bhan and his mother Rani Lachhmi Bas, having won the hope of their hearts, swell with joy beyond what they can hold, and keep telling their whole kingdom: "Open the mouths of all your purses and bags, and let everyone ask for whatever whim comes into his head. What other day will there ever be like today for the apples of our eyes, the day that brings us peace? The wedding of our darling only son, and the three of us coming out of the shape of deer and sitting on the throne again! First of all, this must be done: all whose daughters are unmarried girls, give them all enough that they can deck out and send off their dolls with every fancy they like. [TN: that is, pay for their weddings.] And as long as they live, let them eat and drink and cook at our expense. And let all the daughters of the whole realm stay ever happy wives, and let no one ever wear anything but red and saffron. And let doors of gold and silver, inlaid in two metals, be fitted to every house. Let the fronts of all the houses bear marks of saffron and sandalwood. And for every hill in our country, let as many hills of silver and gold stand facing them, and let the peaks of all the ridges be filled, unasked, with partings of pearls, and all the trees and hills be laden with ornaments of flowers and garlands. And from this kingdom to that, stretch a kind of canopy in mid-air; let there be not one inch anywhere without crowds and bustle, din and drumming. Let flowers be scattered so thickly that the rivers look as if they truly were streams of flowers. And arrange this: along the way by which the bridegroom goes in procession to his wedding, let screens of lotuses made of rubies, diamonds and topazes rise on either side, like flower beds, and let him pass between them. And let no ridge or foothill, no rise or fall of ground, be seen whose lap is not brimming with petals, flowers and fruit."

## Raja Indra's Preparations for Ude Bhan's Wedding

Raja Indra gave the order: "Tell those lively women who fly about in their own high spirits: put on the sixteen adornments, thread pearls through every strand of your hair, and with your wonderful and astonishing flying couches stretch a kind of canopy in mid-air from this kingdom to that. But fly in such a way that the flying couches make flower beds and gardens for hundreds of *kos*, and high overhead let drums, *bin*, *jaltarang*, jew's harps, ankle bells, *tabla*, castanets and hundreds of strange instruments of that kind come playing. And in the midst of those beds let there be seen diamonds, topazes, sprays of unstrung pearls, and the glitter of crowds of lanterns, and from those same lanterns let hand-held fireworks, sparklers, *jahi* and *juhi* jasmines, *kadam*, marigold and jasmine fireworks go off in such a way that the doors of the watchers' hearts fly open. And from the crackers that leap and burst, let laughing areca nuts and talking sweetmeats come tumbling out. And when you all laugh, then with that laughter let strings of pearls fall, so that everyone who gathers them up becomes a king of kings. In the guise of minstrel women, play your *sarangis* and sing wedding songs, wave both your hands, make your fingers dance. Show such airs and graces, such comings and goings, such pomp and pleasure as no one has ever heard of. Make your chins quiver and arch your noses and brows as you act out the songs. Let none of you be left out. Make such a show as happens once in a hundred thousand years."

Whatever Raja Indra had said with his own mouth began to happen in the blink of an eye. And whatever the two kings had ordered here and there was all carried out in the same way. When a wedding has this much spread and display and splendour, one upon another, in such a throng, imagine what the rest of it must have been like.
