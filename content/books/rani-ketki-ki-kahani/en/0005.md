# The Magic Ash, and Ketki Goes into the Forest

## Rani Ketki Asks Her Mother, Rani Kam Lata, for the Ash to Play Blind Man's Buff, and Sulks, and Raja Jagat Parkas Calls Her and Speaks Lovingly to Her, and Gives Her the Ash

One night Rani Ketki, beguiling her mother Kam Lata, asked her this: "The ash that the Guru, Gosain Mahendar Gir, gave Father: where is it kept? And what does it do?" Her mother said: "I would give my life for you; why do you ask?" Rani Ketki said: "I want it for playing blind man's buff. When I play with my friends and I am the thief, then nobody will be able to catch me." Rani Kam Lata said: "It is not for playing. Charms like that are put away to save us on some evil day. Who knows what any hour may bring?" At these words of her mother's, Rani Ketki pulled a long face, got up, and lay there all day without eating or drinking. When the King called her, she said: "I have no appetite." Then Rani Kam Lata spoke up: "Why, have you heard anything about it? Your daughter was asking for that ash the Guru gave us, to play blind man's buff. I did not give it to her, and said: 'Girl, these childish ideas are not good; the Guru left it for some evil day.' That is why she is sulking with me. I coax and cajole her as much as I can, but she will not listen." The King said: "Never mind the ash; not even my own life is dearer to me than she is. To amuse her for a single moment I would give, not one life, but a hundred thousand lives if I had them." He gave Rani Ketki a little of the ash from the box. For several days she played blind man's buff with her friends in front of her mother and father, and kept everyone laughing. Trays of pearls by the hundred were scattered as offerings over her. What can I say? It was such merriment that if you tried to tell it, ten million books could not hold it as it was.

## Rani Ketki Wanders Restless with Love, and Madan Ban Refuses to Go with Her

One night Rani Ketki, with this same thought in her mind, burst out to her Madan Ban: "Now I will cut myself loose from this wretched modesty; come with me." Madan Ban said: "How do you mean?" Rani Ketki told her about getting the ash, and said: "I played all those games of blind man's buff and hide-and-seek for this very day."

Madan Ban said: "My heart has begun to tremble. Look, granted you will put this ash on your eyes like collyrium and put it on mine too, so that nobody will see us and we shall see everyone. But where have we got lives to spare, that we should go wandering from forest to forest with our youth in tow, and hang from the horns of deer with both hands? And the one all this is for: where is he? And even if he were found, how would he know that this is Rani Ketki, and this wretched, wicked, scratching Madan Ban is her friend? Into the stove and the furnace with such love, for which one has to leave mother and father, kingdom, comfort, sleep and modesty, and roam the thickets by the river! And in such a sorry state, too: if they were in their own shapes, there might be some hope at least. No, madam, I cannot do this. Are we knowingly to ruin the house of Maharaj Jagat Parkas and Maharani Kam Lata, lead astray their only, darling daughter and carry her off, make her wander here and there eating forest leaves, and wag our own topknots at them? Tell me, did you not see this that day, when your mother and father and his were at war? He sent you word by the flower-woman: 'Let us run away.' What you wrote then on the back of his letter in your own betel juice: have you forgotten it? What airs you put on then! Now that Kunwar Ude Bhan and his mother and father, all three, have become deer and does of the forest, and who knows where they are, you have gone and set your mind on doing what nobody in your whole family has ever done. Throw dust on this idea, or you will be sorry and reap what you sow. I can do nothing. If it were some good thing of yours, it would never pass my lips while I live; but this I cannot keep down in my stomach. You are still a silly girl; you have seen nothing of the world. If I see you really set on this, I will tell your mother and father and have that ash snatched out of your hands, twisting your wrist, the ash that wretched, cursed ghost, Machhandar's brat, that wild ascetic left behind."

Rani Ketki heard Madan Ban's harsh words, laughed them off and said: "Someone whose heart is not in her own hands thinks up hundreds of thousands of such things, but there is a great difference between saying and doing. Is it some madness, that I should leave my mother and father and go running after deer? But listen, you are a really crazy little bird, to take this seriously and start quarrelling with me."

## Rani Ketki Puts the Ash on Her Eyes and Slips Out of the House, and Everyone, Great and Small, Is in Anguish

Ten or fifteen days later, one night, without telling Madan Ban, Rani Ketki put that ash on her eyes and went out of the house. What befell her mother and father cannot be told. They settled on this: "The Guru must have had some reason to call Rani Ketki to himself." Maharaj Jagat Parkas and Maharani Kam Lata, in this grief of separation, left their kingdom and everything behind and went to sit on the top of a mountain, leaving someone from among their people to hold the kingdom.

Then Madan Ban told them everything. Rani Ketki's mother and father said: "Listen, Madan Ban: if only you had been with her, our hearts would be a little steadier. Now if they come to take you, do not make difficulties; go with them. Keep all the ash there is; what shall we do with these ashes, throw them in the stove? The Guru has wiped out every trace of both royal houses: Kunwar Ude Bhan and his mother and father are left homeless, and Jagat Parkas and Kam Lata have been turned upside down like this. If there had been no ash, how would all this have come about?"

In the end Madan Ban too went out to look for her, with the ash on her eyes, going along calling: "Ketki! Rani Ketki!" After many days Rani Ketki too appeared somewhere among the herds of deer, crying out: "Ude Bhan! Ude Bhan!" When each caught sight of the other, they called out like this: "Wash your eyes, both of you!" Sitting by a pond, the two met face to face. They embraced and wept so that a wail went up through the mountains.

A doha in my own speech

A cold sigh spread through the thickets;
a wail went up through the mountains.

The two of them picked out a good shady spot on a hillock, sat down, and began to go over everything that had happened to each.

## Rani Ketki Talks with Madan Ban

Rani Ketki told all that had befallen her, and Madan Ban repeated all her old complaints, and told her all about the penance her mother and father had undertaken for her, and how they had renounced the world. When Madan Ban had told it all, she began to laugh again. Rani Ketki began to recite this doha:

I do not stop anyone laughing; let whoever wishes laugh.
It is just as the saying goes: once caught, the heart is caught.

Now this whole tangle of trouble has fastened on behind me.
Why look in my foot? It is in my heart the thorn is stuck.

Madan Ban set about drying Rani Ketki's tears, as it were. She proposed this: "If you will stay somewhere, I will quietly bring your ruined mother and father here and settle the matter with them. Gosain Mahendar Gir, whose doing all this is, is in the hands of those two ruined ones too. Even now, if you take my words to heart, the days that have gone can come back. But nothing pleases you; why should I go on babbling? I take this task upon myself."

After many days Rani Ketki agreed, and sent Madan Ban to her mother and father, and wrote a letter in her own hand: "If you can do anything, settle this with that yogi and come."
