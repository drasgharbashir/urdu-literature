# The Wedding

## Gosain Mahendar Gir's Pageant

When Kunwar Ude Bhan set out in this state to be married, they took along the Brahmin who had been shut up in the dark cell, joined their hands many times over and said: "Brahmin god, pay no attention to what we said and heard before. Keep telling us the rites, as your custom has always been." He too went along on a flying couch to direct the rites. Raja Indra and Gosain Mahendar Gir, swaying on the elephant Airavat, looking all about them, went along leading the whole troupe of dancers. Raja Suraj Bhan walked on foot beside the bridegroom's horse, telling his rosary. Just then there was a great hush, and everyone was alarmed. Out of that hush the nine hundred thousand ascetics of the yogi, every one of them dressed as a yogi, with cords of strings of pearls round their necks and their cloaks bound in the same fashion, dropped down on their deer skins and tiger skins. All the eagerness that filled their hearts grew four and five times over. All the queens following Maharani Lachhmi Bas in palanquins, litters and chariots began to feel a kind of tickling delight.

In the midst of this, here came a play of Bhartari, there the yogi Jai Pal came and stood, here Mahadev and Parbati appeared, there Gorakh awoke, there Machhandar Nath fled, here the Fish, the Tortoise and the Boar came face to face, there Parasram, there the Dwarf, there Harnakas and Narsingh, here Ram, Lachhman and Sita came before the eyes, there the whole business of Ravan and Lanka began to be seen from beginning to end; here Kanhaiya's birth on the eighth day, and Basdev carrying him to Gokul, and his growing up in that guise, and herding the cows, and playing the flute, and making merry with the milkmaids, and Radha's love-play, and his winning over of Kubja; there Bansi Bat, Chir Ghat, Bindraban, the groves of Karil, Bindraban's Seva Kunj, the stay at Barsana; and all that happened with that Kanhaiya came before the eyes just as it was; and his going to Dwarka, and building houses of gold there, and never coming back to Braj, and the anguish of the sixteen hundred milkmaids. [TN: these are tableaux from Hindu legend: the Nath yogis Bhartrihari, Gorakhnath and Matsyendranath; Shiva and Parvati; Vishnu's incarnations as fish, tortoise, boar, Parashurama, the dwarf and Narasimha (who killed Hiranyakashipu); the Ramayana; and the life of Krishna in Braj and Dwarka.] Among those milkmaids, one milkmaid took Udho by the hand, and what she said made everyone weep, as she opened her choked heart in these words:

Kabitt (a Braj verse form)

When Hari left the groves of Karil and went to dwell in Dwarka,
he built many mansions of gold and became king of kings;
he gave up his peacock crown and his blanket and made quite other ties;
he took new forms, made new loves, and forgot the herding of the cows.

## The Splendour of the Landing-Places

All the landing-places on the rivers of both kingdoms were like ingots of raw silver, leaving people dumbfounded. Pleasure boats, barges, skiffs, peacock-prowed and golden-faced boats, the *Shyam Sundar* and the *Ram Sundar*, and boats of every kind, finely decked out, trim and taut, came and went, swaying and rocking with a hundred lissom turns. All of them were crammed with these same singers, dancing girls, *ramjanis* and minstrel women, each dancing in her own style, singing and playing, leaping and bounding, making merry, stretching and yawning, waving their fingers and lolling about. And there was no boat that was not sheathed in plates of gold and silver and covered with silk. And on many boats there were swings of the same kind, and women singers sat swinging on them and singing wedding songs in the modes *Kedara* and *Bageshri Kanhra*. Fleets of such boats, like banks of cloud, covered all the lakes as well.

## Kunwar Ude Bhan Arrives at the Bride's Gate in His Wedding Pomp

When, with this pomp and splendour, Kunwar Ude Bhan, wearing the wedding veil of flowers, arrived at the bride's house, and the rites that had always been observed in their family began, Madan Ban said teasingly to Rani Ketki: "Now gather up your happiness, fill your lap with it. Why do you sit there with your head bowed? Come on, let us peep at him for a moment from the lattices, you and I together." Rani Ketki said: "Listen, do not talk such shameless talk to me. What has come over me, that at such a moment I should go through such an ordeal, get up in all this crush, and, dripping with oil and perfume, go and stand there to peep at him?" Madan Ban, turning this coldness aside with a teasing twist [TN: the idiom here is obscure], spoke in dohas in her own kind of speech:

Doha

Well, look at that! Fine, madam, very fine!
Now you have started to put on hard airs with us.

The one for whom you searched forest after forest,
that deer, flushed with youth, stands here as a bridegroom.

That you will not go to look at him means something,
when great and small alike are peeping at him with this in mind.

There is a saying: "It pleases the heart, yet the head shakes no."
We will take you there; we are set on it.

Heaving a cold sigh, Rani Ketki said: "It is true,
everything has turned out well, but now we are in a fix."

## Madan Ban Makes a Sacrifice of Herself for Rani Ketki, Smells Her Perfume, and Dozes from Sleeplessness

At that moment Madan Ban was charmed by Rani Ketki's turmeric-yellow wedding dress, her fragrance, her shy eyes and her dishevelled state, and she began to smell Rani Ketki's perfume, and half closed her eyes as if dozing. Circling round her from head to foot as a sacrifice, she began to stroke the soles of her feet. Rani Ketki quickly, laughing softly, rose with a sway. Madan Ban said: "The touch of my hand must have hurt that blister on your foot that you got hunting for the deer." Smarting from the sting of such a pinch, Rani Ketki said: "If a thorn stuck, it stuck, and if a blister came, it came; but you wretch, why must you be my tell-tale?"

## In Praise of Rani Ketki's Youth

Rani Ketki's charm is beyond writing or reading. The arch of her two brows, the modesty brimming in her eyes, the choked look of her pointed lashes, the allure of her laughter, the dark tint of *missi* (dentifrice) on her little teeth, the way she wrinkled her nose and frowned at the slightest check, her friends' scolding, her running off, and her leaping away like a doe: none of this can be told.

## In Praise of the Kunwar's Youth

No one can find anything to fault in Kunwar Ude Bhan's beauty. Ah, the loveliness of his budding days, the grace and poise of his bearing, the freshness of a sprouting shoot, the full bloom of youth in his face: it was just as when, at first light, a ray of the sun breaks out of the lap of green hills. The sweetness that dripped from his darkening moustache, and his strutting at the sight of his own reflection: wherever he went, his shadow kept pace with him, while under his feet it was like sunshine.

## The Bridegroom Ude Bhan Takes His Seat on the Throne

The bridegroom Ude Bhan sat on the throne. On either side Raja Indra and the yogi Mahendar Gir settled themselves. The bridegroom's father, behind his son, began to hum something softly over his rosary, and the dancing began. And above, all the flying couches of Indra's troupe, forming a canopy as before, kept up their rhythmic dancing. The two Maharanis, the mothers of bride and groom, met and mingled, and came to sit on the roof terraces behind the screens of sandalwood doors to watch. Plays and music, mimes and rhythm and *rahas* dances began. All the ragas and raginis there were, *Yaman Kalyan*, *Jhinjhoti*, *Kanhra*, *Khamaj*, *Sohni*, *Paraj*, *Bihag*, *Sorath*, *Kalingra*, *Bhairavi*, *Khat Lalit* and *Bhairon*, took on shapes like real singers, and in that form began to sing, each at its own proper time, men and women alike. Who has the tongue to tell the graces and the splendour of that dancing?

All the pleasure-houses there, *Madho Bilas*, *Ras Dham*, *Kishan Niwas*, *Machhi Bhawan*, *Chandar Bhawan*, all wrapped in gilding and gathering fringes of real pearls into their folds, swaying like revellers with a fine air, were kissing the faces of those who sat in them. In the very midst of all these houses a Hall of Mirrors had been built, in whose ceiling, doors and courtyard there was not so much as a fingertip's breadth of wood, brick or stone, nothing but mirror. On the fourteenth night, when about six *ghari* (twenty-four-minute periods) were left, they seated a bride like Rani Ketki, dressed in a gown of gauze, in that Hall of Mirrors and sent for the bridegroom. Kunwar Ude Bhan, dressed as Kanhaiya, with a crown on his head and the wedding veil tied on, with his moon-like face, arrived with the same pomp and throng. Just as the Brahmins and pandits directed, and as the customs that had come down among the great kings required, in that very manner and form, the circling of the fire and the tying of the knot were all carried out.

Dohas in my own speech

Now Ude Bhan and Rani Ketki have both met;
the flowers of hope that had withered have bloomed again.

The one who knew no peace without the other,
now they began to live together, night and day.

O Player, this was much; nothing small has come to pass,
now that the two have met and the knot is tied between them.

O my Giver, may all who are drowning in love come safe across,
and as their days have turned, so may our days turn.

Those women of the flying couches, who had been dancing in mid-air forming a canopy, came down with their laps and fists filled with diamonds and pearls to scatter as offerings, while the flying couches stayed just as they were, forming a canopy in mid-air. They wore themselves out circling round the bride and groom together as a sacrifice, and all of them were seized with a kind of sobbing.

Raja Indra, as his gift at the unveiling of the bride's face, gave a canopied bed made of a single diamond and a low stool of topaz, and planted before the bride a *parijat* tree (wish-granting tree of paradise), from which whatever you ask for, you receive, and tied beneath it a calf of the wish-fulfilling cow *Kamdhenu*. And from among those women of the flying couches he gave her twenty-one maids, the very best of them, refined singers and players, skilled at sewing and threading, the most accomplished of all, and told them: "Have no dealings at all with her bridegroom, except through Rani Ketki. I am twisting your ears in advance; otherwise you will all become figures of stone and reap what you have sown."

And Gosain Mahendar Gir set before them twenty-one pots of what is known as the fifty-two-*tola*-and-a-quarter-*ratti* elixir [TN: proverbial alchemists' elixir that turns base metal to gold] and said: "This too is a game. Whenever you wish, melt a great deal of copper and drop in just this much of it, and it will turn to gold." And the yogi told everyone: "In the houses of all the people who have kept vigil at this wedding, let it rain for forty days and nights in the form of golden locusts, and as long as they live may they never again want for anything." Nine hundred thousand and ninety-nine cows, with horn-caps of gold and silver, wearing jewelled ornaments and jingling with bells, were given in charity to the Brahmins, and the whole kingdom was let off seven years' revenue. Twenty-two elephants and thirty-six camels loaded with rupees were given away to be scrambled for. In all that throng there was not one inhabitant of either kingdom who did not get a horse, a suit of clothes, a bag of rupees, and a pair of jewelled gold bracelets. And apart from Madan Ban, no one dared go to the bride and groom unbidden. Whoever might come running unbidden, it was she; and whoever might make them laugh, it was she. To tease Rani Ketki she would call Kunwar Ude Bhan "Kunwar, Kunwar-ji," and dress up this same joke in a hundred ways.

Dohas in my own speech

On the night their home was made, Madan Ban, at that very hour,
said to the bride and groom a hundred such sharp things:

"Ketki's heart has opened at the scent of the screwpine;
it is true, these two have no care now for anyone else.

Did you feel no shame at all before your own people and strangers?
Was there any such hurry for this so soon?"

The bride, from behind her veil, said:

"I have a mind to rub your lips this minute.
Bless me, you minx, the line of *missi* on your teeth!"
