# Glossary

## Glossary

*Ades*: the greeting used among yogis of the Nath order.

*Bhabhut* (sacred ash): ash smeared by ascetics; in this story, magic ash that makes its wearer invisible when put on the eyes.

*Bhakha*: the rustic speech of the countryside, as opposed to the polished speech of the city.

*Bagambar* (tiger skin): the seat of a yogi; here, a magic tiger skin whose hairs, when burned, summon the Guru.

*Doha*: a rhymed couplet in Hindi metre. Insha calls his verses "dohas in my own speech".

*Ghari*: a measure of time of about twenty-four minutes.

*Gosain*: a title for a Hindu ascetic or religious teacher.

*Hindvi*: the plain speech of Hindustan, without Persian or Arabic words; the language Insha set out to write in.

*Indra*: king of the gods, who rules over the heaven Indralok with its dancers and musicians.

*Kabitt*: a verse form of Braj poetry.

*Kailas*: the sacred mountain in the Himalaya, home of Shiva; here, the home of the yogi Mahendar Gir.

*Kamdhenu*: the wish-fulfilling cow of Hindu myth.

*Kanhaiya*: a name of the god Krishna, whose life in Braj is shown in the wedding pageant.

*Kos*: a measure of distance, about two miles.

*Kunwar*: a prince, a raja's son.

*Missi*: a black powder used by women to colour the teeth, considered a mark of beauty.

*Parijat*: the wish-granting tree of Indra's paradise.

*Raga* and *ragini*: the male and female melodic modes of Indian music, which in the story take human form.

*Rani*: a queen or princess.

*Savan* and *Bhadon*: the months of the monsoon rains.

*Yogi* and *atit*: ascetics; the yogi Mahendar Gir travels with nine hundred thousand of them.
